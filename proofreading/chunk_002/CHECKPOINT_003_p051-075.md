## Structural interpretation

The unit is substantially more than a biographical lecture on Abélard. Its architecture is:

1. define `prégothique` as a historical-formal category;
2. locate the twelfth century as a creative/fermenting phase of Western Christian civilisation;
3. debate the explanatory value of `Renaissance`, explicitly engaging Haskins;
4. contrast classical revival with deeper institutional/social forces — Church, monastic reform, chivalry, crusade, corporations, towns, schools;
5. move from twelfth-century `formation/fermentation` toward thirteenth-century `ordre`/system;
6. use Abélard (and then Héloïse) as a personality-form inside that broader civilisational argument.

This is a high-value Huizinga historiography unit because it shows him actively negotiating the `Renaissance of the Twelfth Century` thesis rather than simply borrowing or rejecting the label.
