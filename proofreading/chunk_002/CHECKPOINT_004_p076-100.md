## Structural interpretation

Pages 76–95 substantially sharpen the intellectual architecture of the Abélard section.

The sequence is not `biography -> doctrine`. Huizinga constructs an ecology of intellectual production:

1. book scarcity and oral teaching;
2. schools as new cultural centres;
3. mobile masters and students;
4. dialectic as competitive method;
5. `jeu`, `tournoi` and `disputa` as homologous cultural forms;
6. logical virtuosity spilling into theology;
7. Abélard as innovative but premature/formative (`primitif`, `prégothique`), not Renaissance;
8. Saint Bernard as the opposite pole of the same century;
9. longer-lived Christian intellectual temperaments (Jerome/Augustine) used to explain recurring types.

This is particularly significant because `primitive` here is explicitly relational and developmental. Huizinga does not mean simply `backward`: the word marks forms operating before stabilised limits, mature systems and disciplinary restraints have fully formed.
