## Structural interpretation

The Jean de Salisbury section is now much clearer. Huizinga is constructing a second `prégothique` type through several interacting registers:

1. **social permeability** — cleric, courtier and knightly/gentlemanly forms still overlap;
2. **moral temperament** — fortitude, good humour, service, loyalty, limited ego;
3. **epistemic disposition** — probability, moderation and tolerance of unresolved questions;
4. **humanities** — classical reading without later Renaissance self-conscious rupture;
5. **ancient/modern relation** — inheritance rather than simple revival (`dwarfs on giants`);
6. **comparative knowledge** — Peter the Venerable's Islamic translation/knowledge project as an example of outward intellectual curiosity;
7. **style** — concise, living, humorous Latin rather than formalised scholastic or later humanist rhetoric.

This strongly reinforces the lecture's broader use of `prégothique`: not a chronological label only, but a condition in which later social, disciplinary and stylistic boundaries have not yet hardened.

## High-value research note

The p123 material on Peter the Venerable is especially reusable for Huizinga's history-of-knowledge profile. He is interested in an institutional act of acquiring/translating knowledge of Islam, not merely in `tolerance` as an abstract attitude. That belongs in the same family as other Huizinga material where contact, mediation and institutional arrangement matter as much as doctrines.
