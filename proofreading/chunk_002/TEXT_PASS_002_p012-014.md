## p13 — `chunk_002:p0013`

Huizinga manuscript page 2; continuation under `S.'s werk` / sources and internal structure.

### Earlier works / manuscript references

Huizinga notes that Salimbene had earlier composed smaller historical works/tractates; the titles include a work concerning `Friderici imp.` and another title containing `similitudinum` (full diplomatic title still uncertain). He says Salimbene wrote these before/alongside the chronicle and repeatedly points back to them; manuscript folio references are given (`f. 1–207`, later folia around `491–526`, etc.).

Huizinga explicitly notes that Salimbene had the manuscript in his own hands, `afschrijft, aanvult, corrigeert` and adds references/annotations: this is a material-text observation about the chronicle's working manuscript, not merely a contents summary.

### Principal sources

1. `Kroniek v. Sicard v. Cremona.` Huizinga notes its survival in a shorter form, edited by Holder-Egger in `MG SS 31`, with a reference to `N. Archiv 29`. He states that Salimbene used a fuller/redaction or continuation now partly/largely lost; page/folio references follow.

2. From roughly `1212` onward, Huizinga identifies `Albert v. Milioli, notar. v. Reggio` as a principal source, together with the `Liber de temporibus` / a Reggio `Doppelchronik` tradition (wording abbreviated in the manuscript). He notes the relationship running to about `1280`, after which Albert in turn drew on Salimbene.

Other names/sources listed include `Martin v. Troppau` and additional chroniclers; exact abbreviated sequence to be authority-checked.

### Huizinga's content map / page ranges

A clearly structured outline near the bottom gives:

- `p. 29–187: 1212–1247: zijn jeugd`
- `187–254: reizen in 1247 en 1248`
- `254–294` — further travel / Waddinga-related note (middle wording not yet secure)
- `294–333: reizen 1248, 1249`
- `333–444: het jaar 1250`
- `444–506: 1251–1280: meer en meer de draad kwijt`
- `506–652: 1281–1287: meest berichten over N. en M. Italië; veel losse van horen, anecdoten enz.`

This page is therefore especially reusable as Huizinga's own analytical map of Salimbene's chronicle.

## p14 — `chunk_002:p0014`

Huizinga manuscript page 3; `overlevering`, `aard van het werk`, and an evaluative characterisation.

### `overlevering`

Secure anchors:

- `één ms. Cod. Vaticanus no. 7260. autograaf` (Huizinga explicitly treats the Vatican codex as the autograph).
- parenthetical reference to proofs/evidence in an introduction/preface section (`... XXVII`); precise abbreviation to be finalised.
- Huizinga notes self-numbering / self-written headings and additions/corrections; he directs himself to facsimiles and a collation (`facsimile ...`, `collation ... Codex`, with Roman-numeral page references).
- later copies are mentioned (`15. eeuw`, `18. eeuw`, and around `1800`); he says the manuscript's whereabouts were for a time unknown and that it was again at/in Parma by `1857` (exact institutional wording pending).

### `aard van het werk`

Huizinga characterises the chronicle as a mixture of:

- `autobiografie`,
- local/regional history and annalistic material,
- world history,
- theology / moral-publicistic material,
- personal reminiscence / `Erinnerungen eines Bettelmönches`-type memoir.

He explicitly treats the value of the work as arising from Salimbene's personality and the immediacy of lived observation rather than systematic historical criticism.

### character/evaluation

The lower section stresses a markedly individual, egocentric, spiritually coloured personality (with Joachimite inclinations), combined with practical/observational energy and interests ranging across prophecy, songs/poetry, dreams, omens and related materials.

Huizinga then writes, in substance and with several phrases legible directly:

- `betrouwbaar in zijn naïviteit, maar zonder kritiek` (with a cross-reference to an introduction/preface note).
- `gezichtspunt: zijn orde en hijzelf` — his point of view is his order and himself.
- he lacks broad causal/systematic historical explanation; the final lines contrast abundant lively associations/observations with the absence of a wider social/natural framework (`geen natuur- ...`, final wording not yet fully secure).

OCR assessment: the OCR preserved the page's broad sequence but badly deformed key concepts (`autobiografie`, `annalistische`, `egocentrisch`, etc.). The image confirms that p14 is analytically significant, not just bibliographical filler.
