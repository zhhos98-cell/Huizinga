## Method and capture warning

The Latin blocks were re-read from enlarged PDF images, with PaddleOCR used only as a locator. Exact readings are preserved only where the hand is secure; unresolved proper names/abbreviations remain `[unclear]` rather than being normalised from external editions.

This pass promotes physical PDF pages **p33, p36 and p37** from `image_checked` to `text_checked`. p32 was already text-checked; its Latin block is additionally controlled here.

## Latin leaf A — repeated on p32 / p33

The leaf is a crusade/pilgrimage ecclesiastical text copied by Huizinga in a clear blue hand. The opening is securely of the form:

`Dilectis in Christo amicis abbatibus, prioribus, prepositis, decanis, plebanis et sacerdotibus ...`

The copy addresses ecclesiastical office-holders and develops a crusading/pilgrimage exhortation. Secure lexical/topographical anchors include:

- `Christi` / `crucis` and the language of those signed with the cross;
- `peregrinationis` and perseverance in pilgrimage;
- `Damietam`;
- `Babyloniam`;
- `Alexandriam`;
- `obedientiam` / ecclesiastical obedience;
- a concluding enforcement/exhortation field involving ecclesiastical authority/censure.

The PaddleOCR version is not usable as a diplomatic transcript: it inserts digit runs and repeatedly corrupts ordinary Latin formulae. The enlarged image, however, is sufficient to establish the genre, the crusading geography, the institutional addressees and the continuity of the copied passage.

Because the exact source heading is not visible on this repeated leaf, **do not silently identify an edition/source beyond what Huizinga wrote on the image**. Any publication quotation longer than the secure formula/anchors above should return to the image or source edition.

## Latin leaf B — repeated on p36 / p37

Huizinga gives a source control at the top:

`Van Mieris, Groot Charterboek I 176.`

The text begins securely:

`A divina permissione dictus patriarcha Hierosolimitanus dilectis in Christo fratribus ... salutem in Domino et apostolicam ...`

The following field is readable enough to establish the document's function. Secure anchors include:

- `patriarcha Hierosolimitanus`;
- `crucis signatos`;
- `terram Ierosolimitanam`;
- movement toward/through `Aegyptum`;
- `in servicio Jesu Christi strenue laboraverunt et devote` (wording substantially secure; retain image for exact punctuation/abbreviation);
- `Quocirca discretionem vestram monemus in Domino et rogamus`;
- a request that the addressees support/recognise the pilgrims and, where necessary, proceed through ecclesiastical authority/censure against obstruction.

The document is therefore not miscellaneous Latin filler: Huizinga is copying a charter/document concerning crusaders/pilgrims, ecclesiastical recognition and coercive support. The source reference to Van Mieris is part of the manuscript evidence and should travel with any later citation.

## Relation to the Florence notes

These Latin leaves are physically embedded among Huizinga's Florence notes, but their content should **not** be treated as part of his continuous Florence argument merely because the captures share PDF pages with right-hand notes. They are source transcriptions/reused notebook material co-present with the Florence working pages.

This is exactly the kind of case where image layout outranks OCR reading order.
