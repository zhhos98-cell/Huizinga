## p121–150 — continuation and closure of the `Nieuwe Tijd` / state-system manuscript

### p121–130 — monarchy, territorial strategy and Charles V

Themes include:

- French kingship, Burgundy and imperial claims;

### p131–141 — Habsburg limits, Reformation and European differentiation

Huizinga repeatedly returns to the mismatch between nominal authority and usable political/military resources. The Reformation is treated both as religious transformation and as a change in the territorial/political field: religion becomes tied to rulers, territories and state structures.

The sequence compares the Netherlands/Habsburg position, Swiss politics, Spain, England and eastern/northern Europe; it emphasises differing combinations of military resources, territorial coherence, nobility, economic structure and religion.

### p142–150 — Russia/Ottomans and the problem of political form

The last part broadens the comparison eastward. It includes Russian historiography, social/service categories, Ottoman diplomatic/military organisation and Byzantine/Phanariot continuities. The conceptual comparison is not simply West-versus-East: Huizinga asks what forms of service, administration, standing groups, money, industry, military technique and centralisation make a state capable of acting.
