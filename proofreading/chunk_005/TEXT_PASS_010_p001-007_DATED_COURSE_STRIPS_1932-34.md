## Document class

Seven mounted chronological teaching strips. They are not continuous prose. The strips preserve a lecture/course schedule whose successive dates map an institutional history from medieval land/feudal structures and church–state relations into urban institutions, and then (by 1933–34) into North-American colonial and United States political history.

## p1–4 — medieval institutional sequence

Secure dated anchors include `5.X.'32`, `12.X.'32`, `19.X.'32`, `26.X.'32`, then November/December 1932 and January–May 1933. The recurring vocabulary is sufficiently clear to control the intellectual structure even where individual abbreviations remain clipped:

- `grondheerlijkheid`, villa/estate, free property and agrarian/household units;
- `beneficium`, `vassaliteit`, `leenwezen`, immunity and service;
- secularisation/transfer of benefices and the relation between office, wealth and territorial lordship;
- papal/imperial conflict, simony, Cluny, Gregory VII, Henry IV, Worms, Bernard of Clairvaux and the Investiture conflict;
- rise/emancipation of towns, municipal/episcopal jurisdiction, merchants, markets, tolls, coin, commune and urban law;
- `Bücher's Stadtwirtschaft`, merchant/craftsman types, drapery, guilds and the urban economy.

The strips work as a chronological teaching control rather than a polished argument: dates, names, institutional terms and short causal prompts are placed side by side so Huizinga can move from landholding/service relations to church organisation and then to towns/markets/corporate forms.

## p5–7 — North American / United States sequence

The later strips change field but retain the same institutional emphasis. Secure anchors include Pennsylvania/Oglethorpe, French colonisation/Champlain/La Salle/Louisiana, colonial conflict with the mother country, Stamp/Townshend measures, Continental Congresses, the War of Independence, Confederation, the 1787 constitutional settlement and amendments, Jefferson/Louisiana, Monroe, Jackson, sectional/slavery questions, Mexico/Oregon/California, the Republican party, secession, Lincoln and the Civil War.

The course is organised through relations among crown/colonies, taxation, representation, constitutional competence, central versus state authority, territorial expansion, finance, labour and slavery. The dated strips therefore preserve a teaching method based on institutional sequence and changing political capacity, not only narrative chronology.
