## p166–167 — French intervention and the 1494 expedition

Huizinga frames Italy as the field into which external powers intervene and uses the French expedition of Charles VIII as the opening transformation.

Stable anchors include:

- Charles VIII;
- Florence, Milan and Naples;
- Lorenzo il Magnifico as part of the immediate prehistory;
- the 1494 French invasion;
- Commines as a narrative/source reference;
- Fornovo and the French withdrawal.

The treatment is operational rather than only dynastic: movement of armies, alliances, local support, provisioning and the vulnerability of Italian political structures matter alongside hereditary claims.

## p168 — Franco-Spanish struggle for Naples

The manuscript turns to the partition/conflict over Naples and to Spanish military effectiveness.

Stable components include:

- French and Spanish competition over Naples/Sicily;
- the Spanish advantage in field organisation/tactics/communications;
- Cerignola and the Garigliano as decisive military moments;
- Alexander VI / Cesare Borgia and the papal-political field;
- transition toward Julius II.

The page is useful because military success is related to organisation and connection, not simply courage or dynastic legitimacy.

## p169–170 — leagues, Venice and the internationalisation of Italy

Huizinga follows the League of Cambrai/Agnadello environment, Venice's territorial position and subsequent alliance shifts. Marignano and the continuing Valois-Habsburg struggle appear as part of a political field in which Italy increasingly becomes the **theatre of general European power politics**.

A stable conceptual formulation in this section is that Italy is no longer merely a set of local wars: it becomes the `gevechtsterrein`/field of wider European relations.

## p171 — Charles V, Clement VII and Habsburg ascendancy

The notes move through Charles V and Clement VII, the assault/sack environment around Rome, and the re-establishment of Medici power in Florence (with Cosimo I in the later settlement). Bologna/imperial-papal arrangements enter as markers of Habsburg predominance.

The political result is not represented as a single conquest but as a reorganisation of papal, Florentine, imperial, Spanish and French relations.

## p172 — the final Valois-Habsburg phase

The last page carries the narrative through the 1550s:

- Charles V's abdication/settlement context;
- renewed papal-French opposition to Spanish/Habsburg power;
- Saint-Quentin;
- Calais;
- the final exhaustion/settlement of the long Franco-Habsburg struggle.

The OCR is weakest in the closing names and treaty wording, so publication-level chronology should be checked against the image/secondary authority; the structural end of the war sequence is secure.

## Research significance

1. The packet translates `international politics` into movements, logistics, alliances, local regimes and military organisation.
2. Italian political fragmentation is not treated as a moral defect but as a structural condition that allows external intervention and repeated recombination.
3. The transition from local Italian politics to a European power field gives concrete content to the `state system` argument developed earlier in pp.91–150.
4. The notes reveal how Huizinga built chronological narrative from source/bibliographical cues after first establishing analytical categories elsewhere in the chunk.
