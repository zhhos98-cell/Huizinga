## Tier 1 — sources, reconstruction, and the production of a synthetic historical “image”

### `chunk_016:p0200`, `p0217–p0218`

This sequence is methodologically strong.

- `p0200` explicitly describes a social/economic account as **historically reconstructed by Thorold Rogers**, then subjects that reconstruction to criticism; the note also observes that the interpretation circulated into William Morris's *A Dream of John Ball*.
- `p0217` is a compact historiographical/bibliographical apparatus: Ch. Gross, Stubbs, Thorold Rogers, Ashley, constitutional/economic histories and related reference works.
- `p0218` immediately turns from that apparatus to a broad comparative synthesis, opening with an explicit **beeld** of the Schism-era world as unstable, divided and changeable, then moving across England, France, Germany, the Low Countries, Spain/Italy and eastern Europe.

Research value: this gives a documentary chain from secondary-source apparatus -> critical historical reconstruction -> synthetic characterization of an epoch. It is useful for studying how Huizinga made an historical “image” from heterogeneous political, social and intellectual materials, rather than treating `beeld` only as a loose metaphor.

## Tier 2 — symbol as a historical and argumentative mechanism

### `chunk_016:p0014–p0015`, `p0087`

`p0014–p0015` trace the medieval political-theological figures of the **two swords** and the **two luminaries**. The notes explicitly describe comparisons becoming mystical **symbolen**, connect them to biblical passages, and follow how the images were used to express relations between ecclesiastical and secular power.

`p0087` returns to the same symbolic apparatus in the Dante sequence and criticizes the use of the two swords/two lights as scholastic proofs of papal supremacy.

Research value: here image, metaphor and symbol are not decorative. They are historical evidence and argumentative operators whose transmission and reinterpretation can themselves be narrated historically. This is a useful first-order counterpart to the material/reproduction evidence in chunks 013–015.

## Tier 2 — monuments, statues and political antiquity

### `chunk_016:p0124–p0128` — Cola di Rienzo / Rome sequence

This run brings political action into contact with Rome's material antiquity.

- `p0124` notes aristocratic fortifications and resources associated with old monuments.
- `p0126` records the erection of an **image/statue on the Capitol** and places Cola di Rienzo's program beside classical names, monuments and inscriptions; the same page characterizes his imagination through antique grandeur.
- `p0128` moves toward the idea of a confederated Italy centered on Rome and a parliament on the Capitol.

The OCR on `p0126` is partially contaminated by repetition/noise, so exact wording beyond secure anchors should be checked against the scan.

Research value: the cluster is useful for the relation between material remains, antiquarian/classical reference, political performance and the imaginative reconstruction of Rome.
