# Chunk 014 core-theme hits

Flagging policy: pages that directly bear on the project's core questions are surfaced during review rather than left buried in the audit manifest. Claims below distinguish what is directly visible/legible from what still needs close transcription.

## Tier 1 — print/visual production and circulation

### `chunk_014:p0007–p0009` — Paul Hazard / publisher-material sequence

This sequence physically interleaves mounted handwritten slips with externally produced publisher material.

- `p0007`: Paul Hazard, *La crise de la conscience européenne (1680–1715)* prospectus/publicity sheet. The printed material includes a request for a `justificatif`, placing the object inside a publicity/review circulation mechanism rather than treating it as neutral book content.
- `p0008`: the prospectus describes the edition materially: two square-octavo volumes, `papier pur alfa`, new type, `bandeaux`, `lettrines`, `culs-de-lampe`, and `planches hors texte en héliogravure`.
- `p0009`: the publisher catalogue again specifies `papier pur alfa` and `planches hors texte d'après des documents de l'époque`; a Firmin-Didot printer line gives Paris and 1935.

Research value: Huizinga's working archive contains publisher descriptions of how historical knowledge is materially formatted and visually produced — paper, typography, ornament, photographic/photomechanical plates, and images derived from historical documents — alongside his own slips. The relevant object is therefore a production-and-circulation chain, not simply “Huizinga's image.”

### `chunk_014:p0229–p0237` — review-copy infrastructure around architecture books

A second high-value cluster makes the circulation chain unusually explicit.

- `p0229`: Basil Blackwell review-copy slip for S. Gardner, *English Gothic Foliage Sculpture*, asking for a copy of the review when it appears.
- `p0230`: a separate Cambridge University Press review-copy slip for the same Gardner title, dated **24 June 1927**, again asking the editor to send the published review.
- `p0236`: B. T. Batsford, Publishers & Booksellers, sends Walter H. Godfrey, *The Story of Architecture in England*, Part I, Pre-Reformation, “for your acceptance” and asks for an early review.
- `p0237`: Batsford letter dated **19 September 1928** sends Godfrey's book for review in *History* and explicitly connects the book to increased popular interest in architecture and its introduction into schools.

Operational chain:

`book / illustrated or architectural object → publisher or bookseller → review copy → editor/reviewer → printed review → wider readership / school use`

The two Gardner slips should be retained as two distinct documentary witnesses; their coexistence does not by itself establish why both Blackwell and Cambridge University Press were involved.

### `chunk_014:p0248` — direct publisher-provenance marker

A mounted-slip board contains the clearly printed phrase **`ÜBERREICHT VOM VERLEGER`** (“presented by the publisher”). The rest of the page is OCR-poor and requires close reading.

Research value: this is a compact first-order provenance marker showing that at least one item entered the working dossier through publisher supply rather than solely through Huizinga's own copying or collecting.
