## Tier 1 — ludic competition and scholastic disputation

The strongest first-order conceptual hit is a coherent French-language Abélard/twelfth-century sequence. The OCR explicitly preserves an **`élément ludique`** in relation to social competition. Nearby text compares **tournament/combat and disputation** as alternative forms of a combative impulse and presents Abélard through the image of exchanging weapons of war for weapons of the mind, preferring dialectical argument and disputes to trophies.

This is materially stronger than a loose lexical `play` occurrence. The packet treats competition/disputation as a form-producing historical dynamic and therefore deserves priority in any later visual genealogy of Huizinga's play vocabulary.

Primary raw-OCR anchor: `dce87a52-0729-4f16-9fe7-8c2ed0d604dc`.

## Tier 1 — `primitive` inside the same twelfth-century intellectual-history argument

The same coherent packet describes an intellectual or mental attitude as something **`qu'on peut nommer primitive`** and connects it to twelfth-century thought and the still-unrestricted use of dialectic. The surrounding discussion concerns masters of disputation, logical argument and theological/intellectual novelty.

This context is decisive: `primitive` here is not an anthropology label applied to a people or living society. It operates inside a historical argument about intellectual form and chronological position.

Primary raw-OCR anchor: `dce87a52-0729-4f16-9fe7-8c2ed0d604dc`.

## Tier 1 — Renaissance category boundary / Abélard as `prégothique`

A closely related sequence explicitly discusses the applicability of **Renaissance** to the twelfth century. The OCR names the modern formulation *The Renaissance of the Twelfth Century* and contains a passage arguing that Abélard should **not** simply be treated as a precursor of the Renaissance, but rather as **`prégothique`**; the same passage describes his intellectual apparatus as **`primitive`**. Nearby material invokes Gilson and stresses comparison with the age of St Thomas.

This is a category-building hit rather than a mere occurrence of `Renaissance`: it asks how the twelfth century should be periodized and rejects a simple linear precursor model.

Primary raw-OCR anchor for the Abélard/pregothic passage: `f1e3ac39-ebfe-4aea-a9f7-94f1937ebe13`.
