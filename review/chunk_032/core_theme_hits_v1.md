# chunk_032 core-theme hits — OCR-layer calibration

This file records research-facing results that survive a second pass through the existing chunk-032 PaddleOCR JSON. It is **OCR-grounded, not scan-verified**. Raw OCR is preserved unchanged, and unstable wording is not silently normalized.

## Tier 1 — French Revolution research as source triangulation (`p0108-p0159`)

The strongest result is methodological rather than a single keyword. The OCR preserves a coherent sequence in which a historical problem is pursued across several kinds of reference and source apparatus.

One working note tries to learn more about an Assemblée nationale deputy and explicitly lists several consulted tools: a general biographical dictionary, an `Almanach des députés à l'Assemblée nationale`, another biographical work, a historical/biographical dictionary of the Revolution and Empire, and revolutionary-periodical/bibliographical material. Names and publication details are OCR-damaged, but the consultation sequence itself is secure enough for retrieval.

Another run begins with the OCR heading `Robespierre : Idées religieuses et fèles nationales.` It compares statements concerning Lafayette and Dumouriez with biographical and parliamentary evidence. The Dutch note says, in substance, that the writer could not find a closer factual basis for one accusation, then proposes that Robespierre was trying to give an historical `beeld` of the politics pursued by the people concerned.

The subsequent Stanhope/Pitt pages check biographical claims against `Parliamentary History`, distinguish Commons/Lords institutional constraints and compare motions in January/April 1794 with Robespierre's wording. The OCR corrupts several dates into `994` or `1994`; those forms are rejected as machine errors rather than treated as source chronology.

Other stable retrieval anchors in the same packet include National Convention, `10 Frimaire II`, Barère and Le Chapelier. Some occurrences sit on pathological pages, so exact quotation requires the scan.

**Research value:** direct evidence of a working historical procedure: quotation/problem -> identification -> bibliographical/biographical control -> event/parliamentary cross-check -> cautious interpretation. This fits the broader archive-wide evidence for Huizinga's historical `beeld` being produced through heterogeneous source comparison rather than simple extraction from one authoritative text.

## Tier 1 — wartime academic relief and Huizinga as chair (`p0219-p0235`; clearest anchor `p0233`)

The late packet contains a coherent committee/relief document despite surrounding OCR noise. OCR-supported wording describes help for `krijgsgevangen of geinterneerde studenten` under a principle rendered approximately as strict reciprocity. Assistance includes study books/material and means for scientific or scholarly work; another block refers to committee-funded scholarly activity, information and consultation with academic teachers.

A disciplinary allocation includes `Oudgerm.`, `Vergel. taalwetenschap`, `Sanskrit` and `Vad. of alg. geschiedenis`. The same document environment gives the roster:

- `J. HUIZINGA, Voorzitter, Leiden.`
- `L. VAN ITALLIE, Leiden.`
- `J. H. KERN, Groningen.`
- `A. NOORDTZIJ, Utrecht.`
- `L. H. SCHOLTE, Amsterdam.`

This is substantially stronger than a generic `academic-relief fragment`: it is institutional evidence for an organized scholarly-support apparatus in which Huizinga is explicitly chairman. The formal committee name and diplomatic wording remain unresolved until the scan is available.
