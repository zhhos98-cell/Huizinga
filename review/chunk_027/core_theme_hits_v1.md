## p0030-p0034 — natural science explicitly framed as a cultural factor

`p0030` is headed **“Wereldbeeld en wetenschap omtrent 1700.”** It announces a 1932-1933 Leiden evening-lecture series on **“de intrede van de natuurwetenschap als cultuurfactor in de na-middeleeuwsche beschaving”** and says the aim is to show humanists that natural science had become an integral part of modern civilization, while showing natural scientists how scientific development had been shaped by religious, philosophical and social currents. Huizinga is scheduled for **“Cultuurhistorische inleiding”**, **“De aard van het denken”**, **“Het wereldbeeld omtrent 1700”**, and **“Terugblik en perspectieven.”** A. Schierbeek's lecture is **“De verlengde blik. I. Ontwikkeling van de microscopie”**; other sessions cover astronomy, mathematics in natural research, atomism/continuity, vitalism/mechanism, natural classification, alchemy/chemistry and Boerhaave.

`p0032-p0034` is a typed planning/position-paper sequence on how seventeenth-century mathematics and natural science should be presented historically. It resists judging seventeenth-century empirical and mechanistic science simply by later philosophical standards, stresses the historical importance of the mathematical/mechanistic mode of explanation, and sketches symbolic algebra, Indo-Arabic numerals, decimal fractions, logarithms, analytic geometry, differential/integral calculus, Simon Stevin, Cavalieri, mathematical physics, Galileo, Descartes, Huygens and Newton. The source sequence does not securely identify the author on these pages, so no authorship is assigned here.

### Negative-control vocabulary inside this cluster

Retain this as a vocabulary occurrence; it does not by itself support a claim about anthropology, Malinowski or a generalized theory of the “primitive.”

## p0094-p0098 — learned periodicals as communication infrastructure

This typed sequence discusses **Denys de Sallo**, **l'abbé Gallois**, the **Journal des savants**, **Bayle**, the *Nouvelles de la République des Lettres*, Oldenburg's *Philosophical Transactions* and other learned periodicals. `p0094` notes numerous imitations of the *Journal* before 1701; `p0095-p0096` explicitly treats periodicals and editors in relation to correspondence networks and the **République des lettres**, with the journals functioning as a **“trait d'union”** among savants. This is a direct first-order hit for scholarly communication, editorial mediation, correspondence and the circulation of knowledge.
