# Chunk 022 core documentary/research hits

This file records high-priority, source-grounded hits surfaced during the full retrieval-grade OCR/page audit. Page images are used for OCR quality control, layout recovery and verification of missed text, not as a separate visual-culture research strand.

## 1. `p0001–p0003`: a dated 1937–1938 Restoration teaching sequence

The chunk begins with compressed dated notes starting `20.X.37` and continuing into May 1938. The sequence revisits Bourbon Restoration, the Charter, Vienna, Holy Alliance/intervention, Metternich, de Maistre, liberalism, nationalism, the German Confederation, France, July Revolution, Belgium, Poland and Switzerland.

This extends the chronology established in chunk 021, where related Restoration material survives with reuse markers `1907/8`, `1920/21` and dated 1927–1928 course cards.

**Use:** evidence for repeated teaching/reworking of the same broad Restoration/political-concept field across decades. Exact stemmatic relations among the surviving note layers remain unproven.

## 2. `p0023–p0037`: printed Webster/Temperley material, not Huizinga manuscript

`p0023` begins a Historical Association pamphlet titled **The Congress of Vienna 1814–15 / The Conference of Paris 1919**. It contains C. K. Webster's comparative essay, H. W. V. Temperley's discussion of international government, a Corfu Incident note and bibliography, and identifies the material as papers read at the Fifth International Congress of Historical Sciences, Brussels, 1923.

**Use:** separate this printed comparative source from Huizinga's own notes. It is potentially relevant to his bibliography/teaching environment, but authorship must not be collapsed.

## 3. `p0038`: packet title corrected from the scan

Baseline OCR:

`Duitsche Rijk 1688. 1924/25. 1934/35`

Verified scan reading:

`Duitsche Rijk 1648–1688. 1924/25. 1934/35 ten deele`

Two corrections matter: the historical range is **1648–1688**, and 1934/35 is explicitly qualified **`ten deele`**.

**Use:** establishes the first major early-modern dossier boundary and warns against treating its 1934/35 use as complete reuse.

## 4. `p0139–p0149`: dated 1924/25 teaching notes inside the `Duitsche Rijk` packet

The sequence begins with `5.XI.24` and continues through November/December 1924 and January–May 1925. Topics correspond to the larger German Empire dossier: imperial constitution/institutions, Brandenburg/Prussia, Rhine and imperial politics, Sweden/Poland, imperial election, Hungary/Ottomans, Louis XIV and related European diplomacy.

Several date numerals remain OCR-unstable.

**Use:** links the main `Duitsche Rijk 1648–1688` working dossier to an explicit 1924/25 teaching sequence.

## 5. `p0155–p0165`: `Varia bij Duitsche Rijk 1648–1688`

The supplementary packet is explicitly labelled **`Varia bij Duitsche Rijk 1648–1688`**. It contains mounted bibliographical/reference slips rather than one continuous narrative text.

**Use:** shows that the surviving dossier has a main-note layer and a separate supplementary retrieval/reference layer. Slip order should be reconstructed before quotation.

## 6. `p0166`: false-empty recovery establishes a second early-modern packet

The baseline OCR is empty. The scan reads:

`Europ. pol. gesch. rondom 1700.`
`(met materiaal ter voortzetting)`
`1926/27`
`1934/35`

This is a second major dossier boundary.

**Use:** prevents the whole `p0038–p0249` run from being flattened into one course. The Europe-around-1700 packet is separately labelled, broader in scope, and has its own reuse chronology.

## 7. `p0168`: comparative classification of European states around 1700

The page synthesizes the `Europ. pol. gesch.` material at a higher level, working with consolidation and relative state position. France/England appear as strong centres; Prussia/Russia as rising; Italy/German Empire as fragmented or structurally weak; Poland/Turkey/Spain as weakening; Austria and the Dutch Republic receive more qualified treatment.

**Use:** evidence that the dossier moves between event chronology and comparative structural classification. `State-space` is an analytical shorthand, not Huizinga's wording.

## 8. `p0243`: explicit comparison with 1914 and 1935

The scan verifies:

`het maximum van monarchale militair-diplomatieke autonomie! - heel anders dan 1914, en 1935?`

The surrounding notes connect political/military technique with increased means and mobility of power/government. In the margin Huizinga writes:

`de krijg als vak en techniek`

**Correction:** this should not be paraphrased as “war becomes a science.” The secure source language is **warfare as a field/profession and technique**, followed by an explicit comparison of monarchical military-diplomatic autonomy with 1914 and 1935.

**Use:** unusually direct evidence that the 1935 teaching/reworking horizon enters Huizinga's comparison with the seventeenth-/early-eighteenth-century political-military order.

## 9. `p0245–p0248`: 25-meeting 1934–1935 teaching sequence

The final course sequence is numbered 1–25, beginning `10.X.34` and ending `29.V.35`. It covers state/power/consolidation; Louis XIV and religion; Ottoman war; Augsburg League/1688; Palatinate/Nine Years' War; Spanish Succession; Denmark and Sweden; Brandenburg/Prussia; Rhine League; Poland; Muscovy/Russia/Peter I; Northern War; and the German Empire/Reichstag.

A scan check corrects meeting 16 from baseline `6.II.35` to **`6.III.'35`**, fitting the sequence between 27 February and 13 March.

**Use:** the strongest surviving running order for the 1934/35 deployment of this material.

## 10. Archival synthesis: overlapping packets and partial reuse

Chunk 022 does not preserve one stable master course. Its own labels distinguish:

1. `Duitsche Rijk 1648–1688` — `1924/25`, `1934/35 ten deele`;
2. `Europ. pol. gesch. rondom 1700` — `1926/27`, `1934/35`.

The safest working hypothesis is that selected German-Empire material was reused **in part** within or alongside the broader 1934/35 Europe-around-1700 teaching. The exact direction and extent of reuse should be established by collation, not inferred from adjacency alone.
