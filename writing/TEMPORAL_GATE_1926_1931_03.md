# Temporal gate: Huizinga–Malinowski article

## Core evidentiary window

**December 1926 → March 1931 (working endpoint; exact terminal correspondence date still requires direct archival control).**

The article's causal and narrative claims must be carried primarily by evidence inside this window. The four-scene architecture remains:

1. **December 1926 — the “calf”**: Malinowski's books, *Crime and Custom*, and Huizinga's immediate medieval analogies.
2. **1926–late 1920s — conversations in transit**: New Haven–Boston / later Dutch encounters; “functional side of cultural phenomena”; spoken words, emotional usage, vanity, generosity, friendship.
3. **1929 — Philip the Good**: Huizinga's own historical method in operation through portrait comparison, photographic acquisition, copy genealogy, written/iconographic traditions and corrigible character reconstruction.
4. **1931, ending by March where direct correspondence permits — Leiden Centre**: institutional anthropology, Malinowski's demand for intellectual leadership/coherent sociological programme, and Huizinga's “only a benevolent outsider.”

No fifth scene should be added.

## Pre-1926 evidence: formation/context only

Earlier material may enter only to establish what Huizinga brought into the encounter. It must not become a hidden first act or a retrospective genealogy of Malinowskian anthropology.

Permitted contextual uses include:

- Sanskrit / Vidūsaka / semantic and comparative-philological work;
- the `Beteekenisleer` dossier: cross-sensory association, `stemming`, metaphor, Grimm, animistic/mythic versus aesthetic/associative routes;
- the Hendrik Kern / Indological working apparatus and Max Müller references;
- Indian medicine / Ayurveda / Suśruta as evidence of Huizinga's older habits of distinguishing conceptual strata;
- pre-1926 medieval work on form, ritual, historical experience and periodization;
- *The Waning of the Middle Ages* only as the prior problem-space into which Malinowski's questions later entered.

Rule: these sources may establish **repertoire, prior problems, evidentiary habits, and available categories**. They may not establish Malinowski's influence, disciplinary membership, or a continuous genealogy from Oriental philology to social anthropology.

## Post-March-1931 evidence: afterlife/control only

Later material may enter only as a limited afterlife or ex post control. It may show what became explicit after the core encounter, but it cannot be used to back-project a completed synthesis into 1926–31.

The strongest case is Huizinga's 1933 *Over de grenzen van spel en ernst in de cultuur* material preserved in `chunk_067`:

- explicit praise and use of Malinowski's *Argonauts of the Western Pacific*;
- Kula treated through gift, credit, reciprocity, honour, trust, generosity and non-utilitarian exchange;
- Malinowski's description said to illuminate comparable phenomena elsewhere;
- Mauss/potlatch brought into the same analytical field;
- Huizinga explicitly adds Philip the Good's **Vœu du Faisan** (1454) as a medieval parallel and remarks that it is not very far removed from a potlatch;
- Sanskrit and Semitic play-language remain active in the same broader play-analysis, showing that the later synthesis draws simultaneously on philological and anthropological repertoires.

This material is exceptionally strong precisely because it is **later**. Its proper argumentative role is:

> by 1933 Huizinga himself made explicit a comparison that the 1926–31 encounter had made thinkable; therefore the later text can validate the historical plausibility of the earlier trajectory without serving as evidence that the trajectory was already conceptually complete in 1926–31.

Maximum use in the article: **one compact epilogue paragraph or a tightly controlled concluding return**, not a fifth scene.

## Temporal asymmetry rule

The evidence should be read asymmetrically:

`pre-1926 repertoire` → **1926–March 1931 encounter and boundary formation** → `post-March-1931 later uptake`

The middle interval is the object of explanation. The outer intervals are controls.

This also protects the “benevolent outsider” thesis. If later *Homo ludens*-adjacent material is allowed to dominate, Huizinga can look retrospectively like an anthropological synthesizer. Restricting causal weight to 1926–31 keeps visible the historical moment when the borrowing was still partial, the disciplinary jurisdiction unsettled, and Huizinga could plausibly describe himself as an outsider.

## Drafting rule

When increasing density, every candidate paragraph should be tagged mentally as one of:

- **CORE** — inside 1926–March 1931; may carry causal/narrative claims.
- **PREHISTORY** — before 1926; maximum function is formation/repertoire.
- **AFTERLIFE** — after March 1931; maximum function is later confirmation/consequence.

If a PREHISTORY or AFTERLIFE paragraph begins doing more argumentative work than a CORE paragraph, compress or remove it.
