# Anton van der Lem (2014): direct evidence for Huizinga books entering Vosmaer's private library

_Status: direct reconstruction evidence, synced 2026-09-03._

## Source

Anton van der Lem, `Gezocht: boeken uit de bibliotheek van Johan Huizinga`, *De Gulden Passer* 92 (2014), pp. 225–226. Full searchable text is available through DBNL:

https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_gul005201401_01/_gul005201401_01_0015.php

Van der Lem was curator of early printed/special books at Leiden University Library and the central figure in the institutional reconstruction of Johan Huizinga's private library.

## 1. Stronger than the later Huizinga Online wording

The later Huizinga Online page says, more cautiously, that according to a story/rumour Vosmaer kept many Huizinga books for himself and supplied them with his ex libris. Van der Lem's 2014 appeal gives a firmer formulation.

After describing the division of some books among Huizinga's children and grandchildren, he states that another portion was sold in the second half of the 1940s to the Leiden antiquarian Vosmaer and then writes:

> `Sommige boeken uit Huizinga's bibliotheek gingen in die van Vosmaer over. Van daaruit hebben Huizinga's boeken vele wegen naar elders gevonden.`

Thus, for at least some books, the chain

**Huizinga -> C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's own library -> later dispersal**

is not merely a rumour about possible retention; it is the explicit reconstruction used by the Leiden curator leading the recovery effort.

The article does not identify every book that followed this route and does not explain Van der Lem's evidence for the statement. It therefore strengthens the general provenance model but does not by itself place the three Malinowski books in Vosmaer's private library.

## 2. Object fingerprints supplied by Van der Lem

The article gives two especially useful recognition markers.

### C.J.J.G. Vosmaer

Van der Lem reproduces **Vosmaer's ex libris** and explicitly says that it can help identify Huizinga books that passed through Vosmaer's library.

### Johan Huizinga

Van der Lem writes that Huizinga usually wrote his name **in his neat hand in the upper-right corner of the title page or cover**:

> `Meestal schreef hij zijn naam, in zijn keurige hand, in de rechter bovenhoek van titelpagina of omslag.`

Books presented to Huizinga may instead contain a dedication to him and need no separate ownership signature.

These marks materially change the search strategy for the three Malinowski books. Candidate copies should be inspected for:

- `J. Huizinga` / `Johan Huizinga` in the upper-right of title page or cover;
- a Malinowski presentation inscription naming Huizinga;
- the C.J.J.G. Vosmaer ex libris;
- C.J.J.G.'s additional small ink numbering found in other surviving books;
- combinations of these marks, which would be substantially stronger than any one mark alone.

## 3. Article-level correction / caution

The 2014 article prints the Saint/Lucas shop address as **Rapenburg 93**. Independent Saint Lucas documentation and later Heijbroek research place the shop at **Rapenburg 83**. The `93` in the brief 2014 appeal should therefore not be propagated as an alternative location without further evidence; it is most plausibly a typographical error.

## 4. Search result for the Malinowski titles

Fresh exact Google searches combining the three titles with `Huizinga`, `J. Huizinga`, `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`, `ex libris`, and presentation/inscription vocabulary produced **no current public web hit that can be identified as one of Huizinga's three physical copies**.

This is a weak/date-bounded negative. Auction and antiquarian descriptions often omit previous ownership, inscriptions are frequently not indexed, and many historical sale catalogues remain unsearchable or unscanned.

## 5. Operational consequence

The highest-value physical-copy search is now an **object-fingerprint search**, not a provenance-name search alone. For each target edition:

1. collect all digitized/publicly described copies and historical sale descriptions;
2. inspect title-page/front-board images where available;
3. search descriptions for `inscribed`, `presentation`, `ex libris`, `bookplate`, `ink inscription`, `provenance`, `opdracht`, `naam op titelpagina`;
4. flag any copy bearing C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's ex libris even if Huizinga is absent from metadata;
5. flag any copy with an upper-right ownership inscription compatible with Huizinga's hand;
6. give exceptional weight to *Sex and Repression in Savage Society* (1927 imprint), because Huizinga demonstrably possessed his copy by 12 December 1926.

## Evidential status

**Confirmed general chain:** some Huizinga books entered C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's own library and subsequently dispersed.

**Not confirmed:** that any of the three Malinowski presentation copies entered Vosmaer's private library.

**Current decisive targets:** Nationaal Archief 2.21.271 inv. 836 (Saint Lucas file), postwar Saint Lucas Catalogue 3 *Wetenschappelijke adversaria*, and surviving physical copies carrying Huizinga/Vosmaer object fingerprints.
