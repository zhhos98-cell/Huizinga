## Controlled sequence

Huizinga challenges Wells's story that nomadic peoples repeatedly refresh civilization.

For the Germanic peoples he separates several inferential steps that Wells compresses:

1. migration / invasion / conquest does not itself establish a nomadic social character;
2. even if migrating/conquering peoples had all been nomadic, the supposedly refreshing or renewing effect of their conquests still requires demonstration;
3. the Roman Empire was not simply `smashed by the nomads of the great plains`;
4. Roman political organs continued processes of development or decay;
5. imperial civilization had become impoverished and dislocated substantially through an internal process;
6. Germanic penetration was, in Huizinga's formulation, `veel meer symptoom en gevolg dan oorzaak van verval geweest` — much more symptom and consequence than cause of decline.

He applies a related test to the Mongol conquests. Temporary opening of routes between East and West does not by itself prove a strong and sustained civilizational transfer, and technological items attributed to Mongol mediation require more specific causal and transmission analysis.

## Article significance

This is unusually close to Draft 04's inferential problem:

`SOCIAL CLASSIFICATION / MOBILE PEOPLE`
`!=`
`HISTORICAL CAUSE`

Huizinga does not merely reject Wells's conclusion. He decomposes the proposed causal chain and asks which relations actually connect classification, movement, transfer, and historical consequence.

This predates the direct Malinowski exchange and therefore blocks a story in which relational or causal decomposition arrived wholesale from functional anthropology.

At the same time it should not be overread as a mature theory of comparison. The same Huizinga could still use `primitive` as an explanatory model in *Herfsttij* and later retain stadial language. The value of the passage is precisely coexistence of different inferential habits.
