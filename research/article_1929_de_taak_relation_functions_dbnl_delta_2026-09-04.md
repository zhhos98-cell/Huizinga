## Controlled wording and sequence

Huizinga says religious studies and ethnology provide the `begripsbepaling` of forms including myth, consecration, sacred action, contest-play and secret association. Cultural history can then show their `werking en het bestaan` in the varied course of history, using knowledge of those forms to understand particular events.

He immediately makes the point more concrete with civilizational functions: `Dienst, Eer, Trouw, Gehoorzaamheid, Navolging, Verzet, Vrijheidsstreven`. These may each be objects of sociology, he says, but systematic treatment does not exhaust them unless cultural history displays their `telkens wisselende werking en gedaante door eeuwen en landen heen`.

The same passage then states that cultural history may learn from American practical sociology and from Marcel Mauss without reducing itself to subordinate labour for systematic social science.
