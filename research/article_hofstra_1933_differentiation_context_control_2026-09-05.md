## What Hofstra's dissertation was doing

Bibliographic control:

Sjoerd Hofstra, *Differenzierungserscheinungen in einigen afrikanischen Gruppen. Ein Beitrag zur Frage der primitiven Individualität* (Amsterdam: Scheltema & Holkema, 1933), 214 pp.

AfricaBib classifies the work under `individualization` and `economic inequality`:
https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=049433954

A later historical reconstruction by Jan Platvoet, based directly on Hofstra 1933 with page references and a German quotation from p. 38, characterizes the dissertation as an attack on the collectivist/homogeneity implications of Lévy-Bruhl's `primitive mentality`. Hofstra used data on nine African societies to identify ordinary and institutionally available forms of differentiation, individuality, competition, opposition, variation in belief, etc. Platvoet quotes Hofstra p. 38:

`Unser Ziel wird es sein, an einigen Phänomenen aufzuzeigen, auf welche Weise die Gemeinschaft differenzierend auf die Individuen einwirkt und welche Möglichkeiten der Differenzierung die letzteren haben oder suchen. [...] Die Ergebnissen werden dann erweisen ob die kollektivistische Theorie, die in zugespitzter Form eine Homogeneität der Gruppenglieder bedeuten würde, Recht hat oder nicht.`

Platvoet also reports that Hofstra criticized the `geneticist` study of African religions as primitive religions primarily for reconstructing the origin of religion, arguing that this emphasis obscured diversity and discrepancies among individual believers.

Control source:
Jan Platvoet, historical discussion of Dutch academic work on African religions, PDF surfaced in bounded web check, pp. around the Hofstra discussion:
https://jangplatvoet.nl/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/MerchantsMIssionariesAcademicsOnAfricanReligions.pdf

A 2024 African Studies Centre Leiden inventory independently confirms that the 1933 dissertation is preserved in the Sjoerd Hofstra collection and that Hofstra's Sierra Leone fieldwork followed in 1934–36:
https://www.ascleiden.nl/sites/default/pubfiles/inventaris_sjoerd_hofstra_updated_20240924.pdf

## Evidentiary limit

This material establishes the **intellectual content of the Hofstra object**: `primitive individuality` was not merely a title phrase; the dissertation challenged homogeneity/collectivism and investigated differentiation within societies classified as primitive.

It does **not** establish that Huizinga had read, accepted, or incorporated Hofstra's full anti-collectivist argument by 6 Nov 1933. The 27 Oct letter mentions encountering a review, and the 6 Nov lecture proves use of Hofstra p. 126 for one prophet example. No current control proves Huizinga read the dissertation's p. 1 / p. 38 programme or its conclusion.

Therefore:

- BODY / SECURE: the late-Oct pair exchange concerned a book whose explicit problem was `primitive individuality`; Huizinga then used Hofstra p. 126 for a specific relation to the past.
- NOTE / CONTEXT: the book itself belonged to a differentiation/anti-homogeneity critique of `primitive`.
- DO NOT CLAIM: Hofstra's anti-Lévy-Bruhl programme caused Huizinga's 6 Nov formulation; Huizinga absorbed Hofstra's general theory of individuality; Malinowski supervised this dissertation in 1933 without contemporaneous control.
