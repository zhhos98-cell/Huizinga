## Source

Johan Huizinga, review of Marc Bloch, *Les rois thaumaturges*, *Tijdschrift voor Rechtsgeschiedenis* VI, no. 3 (1925), pp. 354–356; reprinted in *Verzamelde Werken* IV, DBNL TEI `sources/dbnl/verzamelde_werken/huiz003verz05_01.xml`, VW pp. 127–129.

## Controlled sequence

Huizinga summarizes Bloch's history of royal healing by touch in France and England rather than reducing it to an origin claim. He notes:

- differing ritual forms in France and England, with prayer and liturgical action more prominent in England;
- the general connection to sacral kingship, Germanic royal sacrality, Old Testament models and anointing;
- Bloch's argument that the practice was deliberately fixed in both kingdoms for purposes of political legitimacy;
- later transformation and contestation, including William III's refusal, altered royal formulas and eighteenth-century scepticism;
- the persistence of belief when cures failed: failure could be reassigned to insufficient faith or mistaken diagnosis;
- rationalizing explanations that retained the claimed effect but shifted its mechanism from miracle to stars, aromatic diet, inherited bodily properties, imagination or blood movement.

## Article function

**PRE-CONTACT EFFICACY / PERSISTENCE CONTROL.** By 1925 Huizinga was already reading a historical problem in which ritual action, political legitimation, belief, failure and changing explanations had to be followed across time. The review does not establish a Malinowskian method avant la lettre and should not be used that way. It does establish that the pair's later discussion of the `functional side`, law and the efficacy of words met a historian already occupied with how socially efficacious practices persisted and changed.
