# `beleving der Feodaltijd` translation control — 2026-09-05

_Status: publication-QA closure by retention, not by forced one-word translation._

## Article object

Draft 04 cites the mounted-slip anchor in Huizinga's papers:

`Ridderspelen als beleving der Feodaltijd`

Archive/review locator: `review/chunk_013:p0142`.

The present note [^4] leaves `beleving` in Dutch because `lived experience`, `enactment`, and `experienced realization` each capture only part of its force.

## Why retention is preferable

`Beleving` is semantically broad in Huizinga's own usage. It can denote an experienced or lived apprehension, but it can also carry the sense of realization/actualization in and through a cultural form.

Two controlled Huizinga contexts show the range:

1. In *Geschiedwetenschap / hedendaagsche cultuur*, discussing the `historische sensatie`, Huizinga writes of `belevingen` and of a `boven-redelijke beleving` in relation to historical apprehension. Here the term is close to experience or lived apprehension.
2. In *Homo ludens* he writes, in discussing Greek drama, `In Aeschylus voltrekt zich de beleving van den geweldigsten ernst in den vorm ...`. Here a simple English `experience` under-translates the sense that seriousness is realized/actualized in a form.

The archival phrase `Ridderspelen als beleving der Feodaltijd` is too compressed to decide whether Huizinga meant primarily subjective experience, enactment, realization, or a deliberate overlap among them. Nothing in the article depends on choosing one English equivalent.

## Decision

**RESOLVED BY RETENTION.**

Keep the archival wording in Dutch. Do not silently normalize it to `lived experience of the feudal age`, `enactment of the feudal age`, or `realization of the feudal age`.

If an English gloss is needed for a reader, use a deliberately non-exclusive paraphrase such as:

`knightly games as a mode in which the feudal age was experienced or realized`

but do not substitute that gloss for the archival wording.

This item should no longer be treated as an open translation problem. Reopen only if a fuller surrounding Huizinga manuscript context for the slip establishes a narrower intended sense.
