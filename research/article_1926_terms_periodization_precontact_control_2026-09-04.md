# 7 April 1926 — terms, periodization, and pre-contact method control — 2026-09-04

Purpose: source-lock the methodological claims that are securely present in the seven published theses from Huizinga's Historisch Genootschap lecture, and distinguish them from the 1929 elaboration in *De taak der cultuurgeschiedenis*.

## Secure 1926 theses

Source: Johan Huizinga, `Taak en termen der beschavingsgeschiedenis`, report of the general meeting of the Historisch Genootschap, Utrecht, 7 April 1926, pp. 5–7; reprinted in *Verzamelde Werken* VII / DBNL TEI `sources/dbnl/verzamelde_werken/huiz003verz08_01.xml`.

The seven theses securely include:

- thesis 2: `evolutie` as a general historical explanatory principle does more harm than good; narrower natural-scientific notions may be useful only metaphorically;
- thesis 4: cultural history is not social psychology but `sociale morphologie`; it concerns not what holds the human being together internally but `hetgeen hen onderling verbindt`, the forms of life, art and thought and the structure of society;
- thesis 5: general culture-historical terms contain a `mythologisch element` and suffer inflation; Renaissance, Gothic and Baroque are examples of overextension;
- thesis 6: cultural history should provisionally delimit objectively observable and separable elements of culture and study `vormen en functies`—themes, figures, motifs, symbols, styles, ideas and sentiments; ethnology, literary history and art history already do such work, though too separately or externally;
- thesis 7: periodization is indispensable but secondary, always inaccurate and partly arbitrary; terms that claim to name the essence of a period tend to `prejudicieeren en misleiden`, while relatively colourless temporal designations are safer.

## 1929 boundary

The published 1929 *De taak der cultuurgeschiedenis* is explicitly an `uitwerking` / elaboration of the 1926 lecture. Its opening description of medieval university disputation and tournament as `maatschappelijk spel, waaruit cultuur ontspringt` is therefore **not** to be back-projected into the seven surviving 1926 theses unless an independent 1926 manuscript or report is found. The pre-contact play claim remains HOLD.
