# 1919 *Herfsttij*: explanatory force of `primitive` — control — 2026-09-04

Purpose: prevent Draft 04 from making the pre-1926 baseline too relation-first or too cleanly separated between temporal position and explanation.

## Source 1: symbolism

Johan Huizinga, *Herfsttij der Middeleeuwen* (1919), later reprinted in *Verzamelde Werken* III, DBNL TEI `sources/dbnl/verzamelde_werken/huiz003verz04_01.xml`, VW pp. 247–248.

Huizinga does more than label symbolic thought `een zeer primitieve geestesfunctie`. He immediately characterizes `het primitieve denken` by `een zwakheid van de waarneming der identiteitsgrenzen tusschen de dingen`: a representation of one thing incorporates what is connected to it by resemblance or belonging. He then says `De symboliseerende functie hangt daarmede ten nauwste samen.`

Article implication: the ethnological model of primitive thinking itself performs explanatory work in the medieval argument. `Primitive` is not merely a position marker here.

## Source 2: efficacy of the spoken word

Same work, chapter XVII, VW pp. 292–293.

Huizinga writes that belief in `de werking van het gesproken woord` `zich in de primitieve cultuur in zijn volheid openbaart, en zich in de late Middeleeuwen nog handhaaft` in blessings, magical formulas and legal formulas.

Article implication: this is an explicit persistence / survival structure. Primitive culture supplies the fullest manifestation; a related form is said to remain in the late Middle Ages. The category is carrying temporal and explanatory force before the Malinowski encounter.

## Tension with 1921 / 7 April 1926

This older usage coexisted with Huizinga's later methodological cautions:

- 1921: a wider developmental chronology does not by itself yield historical understanding;
- 7 April 1926: `evolutie` as general explanatory principle is damaging; culture-historical terms may acquire mythological force and inflation; period names claiming an era's essence can `prejudicieeren en misleiden`.

The pre-contact repertoire therefore contains a tension, not a completed solution: Huizinga could still use `primitive` as an explanatory ethnological model while also articulating reasons not to let general developmental or periodizing labels settle historical explanation.

## Article decision

**BODY / STRUCTURAL CORRECTION.** Revise the two pre-contact paragraphs. Do not say that the materials `already separated position from explanation`. Instead state that they show a tension between an older explanatory shortcut and an emerging methodological suspicion of allowing sequence or classificatory terms to decide the argument in advance.

This correction strengthens the article's `waning` claim: explanatory force is progressively redistributed; it was not already absent in 1919.
