## Published text control

Source: Johan Huizinga, `Über die Verknüpfung des Poetischen mit dem Theologischen bei Alanus de Insulis`, internally dated 1932 in DBNL; reprinted in *Verzamelde Werken* IV, especially section VIII, `Ideenwert der allegorischen Figuren`, pp. 64–65; DBNL TEI `sources/dbnl/verzamelde_werken/huiz003verz05_01.xml`.

At the start of section VIII Huizinga asks what intellectual value Alanus's allegorical figures possessed. He rejects two clean reductions: treating allegory as outside intellectual content altogether, or treating the inherited figures as dead rhetorical ornament and school exercise. He writes that things do not lie `so sauber geschieden und so scharf umrissen` in a human mind, and locates a `geistige Zone` between `bewusste Überzeugung` and `gedankenlose Spielerei mit ornamentalen Wendungen` that requires closer determination. He then makes the problem specifically historical: for the medieval thinker, unlike the Renaissance poet, the boundary between belief and fantasy is harder to dispose of because heavenly and infernal beings already possessed defined forms and functions.

## Article function

**DIRECT-PAIR INTELLECTUAL OBJECT / SECOND-ORDER CONTROL.** This makes the November 1932 dispatch more than continuity evidence. The object circulating between Huizinga and Malinowski itself concerned a boundary that Huizinga refused to draw cleanly: conviction / fantasy / `Spielerei`. It therefore supplies a controlled antecedent to the 1933 rectoral address as a boundary question about play and seriousness.

The article should only show that, immediately before the 1933 comparison, a Huizinga text explicitly examining the boundary of `Spielerei` was already in direct pair circulation.
