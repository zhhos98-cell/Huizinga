# Vosmaer estate dispersal: 1986–1989 auction lead

_Status: active lead, synced 2026-09-03._

A further dispersal stage becomes visible after the death of C.J.J.G. (Carel) Vosmaer in 1986. Boudewijn Büch wrote in 1987 that at the end of 1986 he worked through the catalogue of a Hague auction house whose offered books and manuscripts were only weakly identified by provenance; he later learned that the material derived from Carel Vosmaer. Büch purchased a manuscript lot from that sale and explicitly complained that the catalogue made the collection's origin difficult to recover.

Source:
- Boudewijn Büch, "Bibliopolis 3," *Maatstaf* 35 (1987), DBNL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_maa003198701_01/_maa003198701_01_0044.php

This matters directly for the Huizinga-library reconstruction. Since Huizinga Online reports that the majority of Huizinga's books passed to Vosmaer after 1945, the 1986 Hague auction creates a plausible second major dispersal point for surviving Huizinga material that Vosmaer had retained. It also explains why later catalogue searches may fail if they rely on an explicit `Huizinga` provenance field: the Vosmaer provenance itself could be obscured in the sale catalogue.

A parallel family-provenance example shows that some Vosmaer family material remained unsold until later: a painting by Alma-Tadema stayed in the Vosmaer family through C.J.J.G. Vosmaer and his widow/children and was sold at Christie's London on 23 November 1989, lot 80. This does not concern Huizinga's books directly, but it confirms that the family dispersal continued across several years rather than occurring in a single 1986 sale.

Source:
- "The Exhausted maenads: Lawrence Alma-Tadema and Carel Vosmaer," *Van Gogh Museum Journal* (1996), DBNL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199601_01/_van012199601_01_0013.php

## Search consequences

The Malinowski provenance chase should now include:

1. Identification of the unnamed/slyly described Hague auction house and the exact late-1986 sale catalogue mentioned by Büch.
2. Examination of all book/manuscript lots from the Vosmaer consignment, including lots without explicit Vosmaer or Huizinga provenance.
3. Searches across 1986–1989 sale records for the three Malinowski titles: *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (1922), *Crime and Custom in Savage Society* (1926), and *Sex and Repression in Savage Society* (1927).
4. Attention to provenance marks rather than catalogue wording alone: C.J.J.G. Vosmaer ex libris, Huizinga ownership/annotation marks, Malinowski presentation inscriptions, and inserted letters.

## Evidential status

The 1986 auction is evidence for dispersal of Vosmaer-owned material, not yet proof that Huizinga books or the three Malinowski presentation copies were included. It is nevertheless a high-priority second-stage provenance bottleneck after the postwar Saint Lucas Society Catalogue 3.
