# Vosmaer 1986 auction: corrected collateral lead

_Status: corrected 2026-09-03 after distinguishing Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888) from C.J.J.G. Vosmaer (1907–1986)._

## Correction

An earlier working hypothesis treated the Van Stockum sale of 3–5 December 1986 as a likely second-stage dispersal of books that C.J.J.G. Vosmaer had acquired from Johan Huizinga after 1945. That inference is **not supported** and is withdrawn.

The key source, a 1996 *Van Gogh Museum Journal* article, discusses the library and collection of **Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888)**, the writer/art critic/collector and grandfather of C.J.J.G. Vosmaer. Its provenance note states that parts of that older Vosmaer collection were sold after Carel's death, further portions by Van Gendt in the 1980s, and that the **last part of the `Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana`** went under the hammer at Van Stockum in The Hague on 3–5 December 1986.

Therefore the 1986 sale is first and foremost a dispersal event for the inherited nineteenth-century **family Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana**, not demonstrated disposal of C.J.J.G.'s Saint Lucas antiquarian stock or Huizinga-derived books.

Source:
- *Van Gogh Museum Journal* 1996, article on Carel Vosmaer / Alma-Tadema: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199601_01/_van012199601_01.pdf

## Büch's 1987 account, reinterpreted

Boudewijn Büch wrote in 1987 that at the end of 1986 he worked through the catalogue of a Hague auction house, where books and manuscripts gave little indication of whose collection they came from; it turned out to be "Carel Vosmaer." Büch purchased a manuscript lot including a Rhijnvis Feith poem.

In light of the Van Gogh Museum provenance note, this is best read as referring to **Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888)** and the inherited family collection, not automatically to C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's personal/business books.

Source:
- Boudewijn Büch, "Bibliopolis 3," *Maatstaf* 35 (1987): https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_maa003198701_01/_maa003198701_01_0044.php

## What remains confirmed

- Van Stockum held a book/manuscript sale in The Hague on **3–5 December 1986**; contemporary auction agendas record those exact dates.
- The later provenance literature identifies that sale as the sale of the **last part of the Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana**.
- Earlier portions of the older family collection were sold through **Van Gendt Book Auctions** in the 1980s.
- C.J.J.G. Vosmaer (1907–1986) was a descendant/custodian of inherited family material, so family objects could pass through him without belonging to his Saint Lucas antiquarian stock.

Source:
- *De Boekenwereld* 3 (1986–1987), auction agenda: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_boe022198601_01/_boe022198601_01_0021.php

## Consequence for the Huizinga-Malinowski search

The December 1986 Van Stockum catalogue is **demoted from primary to collateral status**. It should not be used to bridge the chain

`Huizinga -> C.J.J.G. Vosmaer -> Van Stockum 1986`

without an actual catalogue entry, consignor annotation, or other evidence showing that C.J.J.G.'s post-1945 Saint Lucas/Huizinga stock was mixed into the family consignment.

The primary route now runs through:

1. Nationaal Archief 2.21.271 **inv. 836**, C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's Saint Lucas Society working file, 1947+n.d.
2. Saint Lucas postwar Catalogue 3, *Wetenschappelijke adversaria*, explicitly advertised with Huizinga-associated material.
3. Object-level searches for C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's ex libris / ink numbering on the three Malinowski editions.

## Why the Van Stockum route is still worth keeping

The sale remains useful as a control and collateral branch. If a target Malinowski volume unexpectedly appears there, the annotated Van Stockum working copy could matter greatly. But absence from that catalogue would now tell us almost nothing about the fate of the Huizinga books, because the sale's documented organizing provenance is the older family collection.

## Evidential caution

The coincidence that C.J.J.G. died in 1986 and that the final *Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana* sale also occurred in 1986 is precisely what made the earlier conflation tempting. It must not substitute for provenance evidence. There are two distinct collection histories here: the inherited family Bibliotheca associated with Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888), and C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's twentieth-century antiquarian/personal holdings, which included books acquired from Huizinga.
