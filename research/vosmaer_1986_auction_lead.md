# Vosmaer estate dispersal: 1986–1989 auction lead

_Status: active lead, synced 2026-09-03._

A further dispersal stage becomes visible after the death of C.J.J.G. (Carel) Vosmaer in 1986. Boudewijn Büch wrote in 1987 that at the end of 1986 he worked through the catalogue of a Hague auction house whose offered books and manuscripts were only weakly identified by provenance; he later learned that the material derived from Carel Vosmaer. Büch purchased a manuscript lot from that sale and explicitly complained that the catalogue made the collection's origin difficult to recover.

Source:
- Boudewijn Büch, "Bibliopolis 3," *Maatstaf* 35 (1987), DBNL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_maa003198701_01/_maa003198701_01_0044.php

## Auction house now identified

The unnamed late-1986 Hague sale can now be identified with high confidence as **Van Stockum, The Hague, 3–5 December 1986**. A 1996 *Van Gogh Museum Journal* article states explicitly that **"The last part of the 'Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana' went under the hammer at Van Stockum in The Hague, 3–5 December 1986."** Contemporary auction-agenda listings independently record Van Stockum's Hague book sale on exactly 3–5 December 1986, with viewing days 28–30 November.

This matches Büch's description unusually closely: late 1986, Hague auction house, books and manuscripts, Carel Vosmaer provenance not clearly disclosed in the catalogue. The Van Stockum sale catalogue for 3–5 December 1986 is therefore now the primary lot-level target.

## Multi-channel 1986 dispersal

The 1986 disposal was not a single auction event. Rijksmuseum provenance records show direct acquisition in 1986 from C.J.J.G. Vosmaer / the estate chain of a group of drawings and related objects for fl. 13,000. Other family material remained in family hands and surfaced later. The correct model is therefore:

**Vosmaer estate, 1986 -> direct institutional purchase(s) + Van Stockum sale (3–5 Dec. 1986) + family retention / later dispersal.**

This matters directly for the Huizinga-library reconstruction. Since Huizinga Online reports that the majority of Huizinga's books passed to Vosmaer after 1945, the Van Stockum sale is a plausible second major dispersal point for surviving Huizinga material that Vosmaer had retained. It also explains why later catalogue searches may fail if they rely on an explicit `Huizinga` provenance field: the Vosmaer provenance itself could be obscured in the sale catalogue.

A parallel family-provenance example shows that some Vosmaer family material remained unsold until later: a painting by Alma-Tadema stayed in the Vosmaer family through C.J.J.G. Vosmaer and his widow/children and was sold at Christie's London on 23 November 1989, lot 80. This does not concern Huizinga's books directly, but it confirms that the family dispersal continued across several years rather than occurring in a single 1986 sale.

## Search consequences

The Malinowski provenance chase should now prioritize:

1. Locate the **Van Stockum auction catalogue, 3–5 December 1986, The Hague**.
2. Examine every book/manuscript lot from the Vosmaer consignment, including lots with no explicit Vosmaer/Huizinga provenance.
3. Search the catalogue for `Malinowski`, `Argonauts`, `Crime and Custom`, `Sex and Repression`, plus generic anthropology/ethnology group lots.
4. Cross-check lot descriptions against Nationaal Archief 2.21.271 inv. 999, the five-volume stand/systematic catalogue of the *Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana*.
5. Track buyer annotations or priced copies of the Van Stockum catalogue if available; these could provide the first post-1986 owner.
6. Continue searches across 1986–1989 sale records for the three Malinowski titles, but now treat the December 1986 Van Stockum sale as the central bottleneck.
7. Attend to provenance marks rather than catalogue wording alone: C.J.J.G. Vosmaer ex libris, Huizinga ownership/annotation marks, Malinowski presentation inscriptions, and inserted letters.

## Evidential status

The Van Stockum identification is strong because two independent source classes converge: Büch's contemporary first-person description of a late-1986 Hague Vosmaer sale, and the later bibliographical statement that the final part of the Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana was auctioned at Van Stockum on 3–5 December 1986. This still does not prove that the three Huizinga-Malinowski presentation copies were included. The decisive next object is the actual Van Stockum catalogue and, ideally, a priced/annotated copy.
