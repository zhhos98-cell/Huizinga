## Controlled wording and historical operation

In the discussion of late-medieval formalism Huizinga writes that it underlies belief in `de werking van het gesproken woord`, which `zich in de primitieve cultuur in zijn volheid openbaart` and still persists in the late Middle Ages in blessings, magical formulas and `dingtalen` (legal/court formulas). He then gives historical examples involving a solemn request to Philip the Good and Gerson's expectation that repeated preaching should change conduct.

This is already a cross-temporal operation:

`primitive culture -> specified relation (efficacy of spoken word) -> late-medieval historical examples`.

## Pair relevance

On 31 December 1926 Huizinga wrote Malinowski that `we emphasize the functional side of cultural phenomena` and immediately listed historical objects including the efficacy/force of words, feudal relations and sentiments such as vanity, generosity and friendship.

Article function: **PRE-CONTACT MECHANISM / DIRECT-PAIR BASELINE.** One of the concrete problems Huizinga brought into the Malinowski exchange had already been formulated in *Herfsttij* through an explicitly ethnological comparison. Malinowski therefore did not introduce either the object or the possibility of ethnological comparison. The later exchange can be made historically specific by asking what his detailed ethnography did to the relation and evidentiary burden of such comparisons.

Recommended Draft 04 operation: add one sentence after the 31 December list of `vanity, generosity, friendship, words and feudal relations`. Do not turn the passage into a *Herfsttij* excursus and do not use it as evidence that late-medieval civilization models the later `waning` of `primitive`.
