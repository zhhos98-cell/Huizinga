# Koninklijke Bibliotheek catalogue-collection route for Saint Lucas Catalogue 3 — 2026-09-04

_Status: focused catalogue-provenance route. This note supplements the KVB/Allard Pierson and Leiden BA2 routes._

## 1. KB has a dedicated historical collection exactly suited to Saint Lucas material

The Koninklijke Bibliotheek describes its **Veiling- en boekhandelscatalogi** collection as containing catalogues, lists and stock/fonds lists from book auctions, antiquarian booksellers, publishers and booksellers from the seventeenth century onward. The collection comprises approximately **50,000 items** and is the largest such collection in the Netherlands.

For this project the crucial institutional-history point is that KB librarian **P.C. Molhuijsen added antiquarian and publisher catalogues to the collection in 1922**, and the catalogue material was physically grouped in 1924. Thus Saint Lucas catalogues from the 1940s fall squarely inside the collecting policy rather than being an accidental edge case.

Source:
- KB, `Veiling- en boekhandelscatalogi`: https://www.kb.nl/onderzoeken-vinden/bijzondere-collecties/veilingscatalogi-boekhandelscatalogi

## 2. The old database is still searchable and includes `Verzameling Catalogi`

KB states that catalogues described through 2005 remain searchable through the separate `Veilingen` database on **Nederlandse Boekgeschiedenis**, under the source file **`Verzameling Catalogi`**. The live page confirms that three source files can be searched simultaneously:

- `Dutch Book Sales Catalogues`
- `Veilingen`
- `Verzameling Catalogi`

The search form exposes fields including:

- free/random word;
- title;
- collector name;
- collector occupation;
- auctioneer name;
- place;
- date.

This database is therefore a direct search target for Saint Lucas antiquarian catalogues even when ordinary KB OPAC or Google indexing returns nothing.

Source:
- Nederlandse Boekgeschiedenis, `Veilingen`: https://www.nederlandseboekgeschiedenis.nl/nl/veilingen

## 3. Search-engine invisibility is not a negative result

The public `Veilingen` page renders the search form, but result records from `Verzameling Catalogi` are not being surfaced by ordinary indexed-web searches for `Saint Lucas Society`, `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`, or `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`.

Accordingly:

- `no Google hit` must not be interpreted as `no KB catalogue record`;
- the database should be queried through its own interface or by KB staff;
- if the old application does not expose stable result URLs, a curator lookup is a legitimate primary discovery method.

## 4. Heijbroek sharpens the bibliographic and address fingerprints

J.F. Heijbroek's 2018 article provides the exact postwar context:

- Vosmaer founded/distributed **`Bibliopolis` around 1948**;
- the periodical ran **1948–1952**;
- its correspondence address was **Wyttenbachweg 43, Oegstgeest**;
- *Bibliopolis* mentions Vosmaer's **`Catalogue 3`**, titled **`Wetenschappelijke adversaria`**;
- the catalogue is characterized as an international scholarly collection and as containing several books with **letters and dedications of the late Prof. Huizinga**.

The catalogue therefore may have been indexed under business/periodical/address metadata other than `Saint Lucas Society` or `Rapenburg 83`.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, `De Saint Lucas Society`, *De Boekenwereld* 34.4 (2018): https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 6. Why the address shift matters

Earlier searches over-weighted `Rapenburg 83`, the Saint Lucas shop/family-house address. Heijbroek's identification of **Wyttenbachweg 43, Oegstgeest** as the correspondence address of *Bibliopolis* creates a second contemporary fingerprint for Catalogue 3-era print circulation.

A KB record may therefore identify the issuer or source under Oegstgeest rather than Leiden, especially if the item entered the collection through *Bibliopolis* or a mailed bookseller catalogue rather than through the physical Saint Lucas shop.

## 8. Priority relationship to other repositories

Catalogue 3 now has **three independent high-probability repository systems**:

1. **KVB / Allard Pierson** — strongest positive prior because Saint Lucas Catalogue I is already known to survive there.
2. **KB `Verzameling Catalogi`** — strongest national bookseller-catalogue collection; systematic antiquarian-catalogue collecting since 1922.
3. **Leiden UBL BA2 / institutional records** — Saint Lucas was an important UBL customer/service provider; the catalogue may survive as received trade ephemera, correspondence enclosure or supplier documentation rather than as a separately catalogued publication.

These routes should be searched independently. Failure in one catalogue system has little evidential value for the others.
