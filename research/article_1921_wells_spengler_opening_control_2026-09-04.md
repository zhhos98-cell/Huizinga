## Primary source

Johan Huizinga, `Twee worstelaars met den engel`, *De Gids* 85 (1921), reviewing Oswald Spengler, *Der Untergang des Abendlandes* and H. G. Wells, *The Outline of History*; reprinted in *Verzamelde Werken* IV, pp. 441–496.

## 1. The 1921 control is stronger than a generic anti-grand-narrative citation

Huizinga explicitly distinguishes **geological/biological development** from the operation of historical understanding. Although both concern events in time, he says that geological or biological `ontwikkeling` has little or nothing to do with `de functie van het historisch begrijpen`. Historical conception is restricted to re-entering human relations and human thoughts; earth history does not become history merely by being narrated chronologically.

This is a direct epistemic distinction among different ways of organizing temporal material, not merely a complaint that Wells writes popular history.

## 2. Wells: flattening religious history by a single progress line

Huizinga criticizes Wells for constructing the history of religion as repeated ascent toward universal benevolence, selflessness, peace and useful labour, followed by relapse into dogma, discipline and ceremony. Huizinga objects that this leaves out the sacramental, symbolic, ritual, cultic, sacred, mystical and ecstatic dimensions of religion and therefore cannot yield a genuine historical image of earlier religious periods.

The point is useful for the later Malinowski article because it shows in 1921 that Huizinga already resisted explanatory schemes that sorted historical phenomena by whether they contributed to a preferred developmental direction.

## 3. Wells: historical interdependence against selective deletion

Huizinga argues that historical periods possess an internal interconnection among their manifestations and invokes the long tradition from Comte's `solidarité` through Marx, Lamprecht and Spengler. His objection to Wells is that political structures cannot simply be discarded as irrational debris while retaining the strands judged to lead toward modern improvement.

This is compatible with, but should not be collapsed into, Huizinga's later interest in social morphology. The 1921 essay is a control for the prior problem: how to preserve heterogeneous but interconnected historical relations against a unilinear or selective developmental narrative.

## 4. Spengler: morphology is not rejected, but totalizing system is

Huizinga recognizes the significance of the distinction between historical and natural-scientific concept formation, locating it in Dilthey, Windelband, Rickert and Simmel rather than accepting Spengler's claim to have discovered it. His test of Spengler's `Morphologie der Weltgeschichte` is practical: what does the principle actually do to historical material? His verdict is that the system produces distortion when it forces the particularity of historical phenomena into a predetermined morphological destiny.

Thus the 1921 position is not `morphology bad`. It is closer to:

`historical form/connection can be legitimate` + `a total morphological system that predetermines the meaning of particulars is illegitimate`.

## 5. Article-facing sequence

This makes 1921 a defensible opening date for the larger 1921–1933 arc:

`1921 Wells/Spengler`
→ distinct temporal/epistemic operations; resistance to unilinear religious progress and totalized world-historical morphology

`1925 Bloch review`
→ ritual efficacy, sacral power and persistent belief already recognized as legitimate historical subject matter before Malinowski

`7 Apr 1926 cultural-history theses`
→ explicit anti-general-evolutionism + social morphology + ethnology as neighbouring special cultural science, immediately before the American trip

`1926–29 Malinowski relation`
→ direct reciprocal reading and transfer of questions into historical practice

`1929 De taak der cultuurgeschiedenis`
→ interdisciplinary contact is necessary; boundaries are repeatedly crossed and remain meaningful

`1930 Dutch-culture lectures`
→ geographical/social/economic/political/ethnographic conditions are explanatory inputs but do not exhaust civilization

`1931 benevolent outsider`
→ the boundary becomes a concrete institutional/jurisdictional test

`1933 coda`
→ continued comparative use of anthropology despite explicit non-membership.

## 6. Recommended use

Do not add a 1921 scene to Draft 03. If the article keeps `1921–1933` as its larger temporal frame, use this source in the introduction or a compact note to establish the prehistory of the epistemic problem.

A safe compressed claim is:

> As early as 1921 Huizinga distinguished biological development from historical understanding and criticized both Wells's selective progress narrative and Spengler's totalizing morphology. The later encounter with anthropology therefore occurred inside an already articulated problem of how heterogeneous temporal and social relations could be connected without being reduced to one developmental logic.

This source should not be used to claim that Huizinga had already formulated the 1926 social-morphology theses or the 1929 disciplinary-boundary argument in 1921. It supplies an earlier problem structure, not a fully formed later solution.
