# Wijttenbachweg 43 / Schoutenburg: Vosmaer and De Gelder address network — 2026-09-04

_Status: address/provenance clarification. Corrects an over-hasty interpretation made during live searching._

## 1. Primary correction: Wyttenbachweg 43 was C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's own post-1943 residence

J.F. Heijbroek explicitly states that C.J.J.G. Vosmaer married Dorothea Maria Hudig in **1943** and then lived with her, and later their son (b. 1944) and daughter (b. 1946), at **Wyttenbachweg 43, Oegstgeest**.

This means the same address later printed as the correspondence address of *Bibliopolis* was not merely an external scholarly forwarding address. It was Vosmaer's **family residence**.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, `De Saint Lucas Society`, *De Boekenwereld* 34.4 (2018), p. 45: https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 2. The same property was owned/occupied by Jan Jacob and Mien de Gelder

Independent sources identify the property as **Schoutenburg** and connect it to Jan Jacob de Gelder (1878–1971) and Petronella Willemina Johanna `Mien` de Gelder-Jansen (1881–1952):

- Mien de Gelder-Jansen's obituary says the De Gelders bought **`de Schoutenburg` in Oegstgeest in 1922** and lived there thereafter.
- The current Rijksmonumenten description states that **Dr. J.J. de Gelder became owner from 1921**, restored the neglected house extensively, and had a **thatched garden house (`tuinhuisje`)** built in the garden.
- A 1936 member list gives J.J. de Gelder as `Schoutenburg, Wyttenbachw.43`.
- The 1945 member list gives both `Gelder, Dr J.J. de` and `Gelder-Jansen, M. de` at **`Wyttenbachweg 43 (tuinz.)`**.

Sources:
- M. de Gelder-Jansen obituary, *Jaarboek Maatschappij der Nederlandse Letterkunde* 1952–53: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_jaa003195201_01/_jaa003195201_01_0012.php
- Monumenten.nl, `Wijttenbachweg 43, Oegstgeest`: https://www.monumenten.nl/monument/31248
- MNL member list 1936: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_jaa003193601_01/_jaa003193601_01_0038.php
- MNL member list 1945: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_jaa003194501_01/_jaa003194501_01_0052.php

## 3. Address-number variation cautions against literal household reconstruction from directories alone

The 1935 MNL member list prints J.J. de Gelder's address as **`Schoutenburg, Wyttenbachweg 21`**, while the 1936 and later records use no. 43. The modern monument is Wijttenbachweg 43. This suggests renumbering or changing address conventions and warns against assuming that every historical address string maps one-to-one onto a modern dwelling unit.

The later notation **`(tuinz.)`** is especially suggestive of a `tuinzijde`/garden-side address distinction, but its exact administrative meaning has not yet been verified.

Source:
- MNL member list 1935: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_jaa003193501_01/_jaa003193501_01_0028.php

## 4. What can and cannot yet be said

### Secure

- De Gelder owned/occupied Schoutenburg in this period.
- C.J.J.G. Vosmaer and his wife/children lived at Wyttenbachweg 43 from 1943.
- `Bibliopolis` later used Wyttenbachweg 43 as its correspondence address.
- The property included at least one garden outbuilding/tuinhuis.

### Not yet secure

- that Vosmaer rented specifically the garden house from De Gelder;
- the contractual/legal relationship between the two households;
- whether the `tuinz.` notation refers to the De Gelders' own unit, Vosmaer's unit, an entrance orientation, or a postal convention;
- whether Jan Jacob de Gelder played any editorial/business role in *Bibliopolis* or Saint Lucas.

These points require municipal population registers, property/lease records, correspondence, or a local architectural history with resident-level detail.

## 5. Why this matters for Huizinga provenance

This address network is potentially important because Jan Jacob de Gelder was not an arbitrary landlord/neighbour: he had been **assistant and from 1909 conservator at the Leiden Prentenkabinet**, embedded in the same Leiden scholarly/collection world in which Saint Lucas operated. He was also active in the Maatschappij der Nederlandse Letterkunde.

The fact that Vosmaer's domestic address was situated within the Schoutenburg address/property system therefore creates a plausible **social-institutional bridge**, but not yet a proven business partnership.

For Catalogue 3 discovery, the address should be treated first as a **Vosmaer household/business fingerprint**, with `De Gelder / Schoutenburg` as a secondary archival association to test.

## 6. Revised search targets

1. Oegstgeest population registers / resident cards for **Wijttenbachweg 43 / Schoutenburg**, 1940–1955, to reconstruct household units and dates.
2. Property/cadastral records under J.J. de Gelder to determine whether part of Schoutenburg was rented to C.J.J.G. Vosmaer.
3. J.J. de Gelder papers/correspondence for `Vosmaer`, `Saint Lucas`, `Bibliopolis`, `Hudig`.
4. Vosmaer family/archive papers for `de Gelder`, `Schoutenburg`, rent/lease/household correspondence.
5. Continue using both `Wyttenbachweg 43` and `Wijttenbachweg 43` spelling variants in catalogue searches.

## 7. Research correction log

During live research on 2026-09-04, the address was briefly interpreted as if *Bibliopolis* were using the De Gelder household as an external correspondence infrastructure. Heijbroek's explicit residence statement corrects that: **Vosmaer himself lived at Wyttenbachweg 43 from 1943**. The De Gelder connection remains real at the property/address level and becomes a separate question of co-residence/tenancy and scholarly-network proximity rather than a substitute business address.
