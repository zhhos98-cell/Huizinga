### A. 1921 Spengler: exact homology and political–ethnographic contrast

Source: Johan Huizinga, `Twee worstelaars met den engel` (1921), *Verzamelde Werken* IV, especially pp. 451 and 461.

Two linked operations were recovered from the priority review:

1. Huizinga criticizes Spengler's system for placing every phenomenon in a fixed position and positing exact `homologie` across civilizations, forcing particulars onto the `Procrustesbed` of the system.
2. Discussing *Preussentum und Sozialismus*, Huizinga calls it a political doctrine based on a `historisch-ethnografische tegenstelling` between English and Prussian character; the method first posits a contrast and then brings convenient particulars under its suggestive force.

Article function: **PRE-CONTACT COMPARISON / POLITICS CONTROL.** This is not evidence about `primitive` directly. It establishes that by 1921 Huizinga was already policing what cross-cultural resemblance and historical–ethnographic contrast could be made to authorize, including when such a contrast carried political doctrine.

### B. 1933 `Renaissance en Nieuwe Tijd`: naming without closed unity

Source: Johan Huizinga, `Renaissance en Nieuwe Tijd`, lecture delivered 6 Nov 1933, published 1934, *Verzamelde Werken* IV, p. 341.

Huizinga says the great objects of cultural history are so complex and diffuse that they cannot properly bear a name or be grasped as a closed unity; yet without names history cannot make them visible to the mind. Such names often arise from the wishes and imagination of the age itself, `dien grooten mythograaf`.

Article function: **CATEGORY / METHOD CONTROL.** This does not define `primitive`. It supplies actor-language for the article's broader claim that historical naming can remain necessary while a name does not constitute a closed or self-sufficient explanation. The passage occurs in the same 6 Nov 1933 lecture that later uses Hofstra/Willoughby for the `return to old loyalties` relation.

Control: `research/article_1933_renaissance_naming_category_control_2026-09-05.md`.

Draft status: queued for integration through the twenty-third pass.

### C. 1921 Wells: nomad classification does not establish historical cause

Source: `Twee worstelaars met den engel`, *Verzamelde Werken* IV, pp. 481–482.

Huizinga decomposes Wells's claim that Germanic and Mongol nomads refreshed civilization:

- migration/conquest does not establish nomadic character;
- even established nomadism would not itself prove a renewing historical effect;
- Germanic penetration of Rome is described as more symptom/consequence than cause of decline;
- Mongol-opened routes require specific demonstration of sustained cultural transfer.

Article function: **REVIEWER-PROOF / CAUSAL-DECOMPOSITION RESERVE.** Strong evidence that classification and causal explanation were already being separated before Malinowski, but the current 1921 paragraph is sufficiently dense.

Control: `research/article_1921_wells_nomad_causal_decomposition_control_2026-09-05.md`.

Draft status: HOLD; do not add unless a reviewer or later edit needs a more explicit pre-contact causal example.
