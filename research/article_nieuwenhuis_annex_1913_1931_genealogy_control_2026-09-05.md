# Nieuwenhuis annex, 1913–1931: genealogy control — 2026-09-05

Purpose: control a potentially article-changing source genealogy exposed while tightening the archival locator for the July 1931 Leiden Centre packet. This note separates a securely documented prewar Nieuwenhuis programme from the still-unproved proposition that the two-page annex circulated with Huizinga's 1931 memorandum directly copied, revised, or reused the 1913 text.

## 1. Secure 1931 packet

The direct-pair apparatus already controls the July 1931 Leiden sequence:

- 17 July 1931: the Centre paper had circulated locally, including to Van Eerde, and been revised; Snouck Hurgronje was proposed as another reader.
- 19 July 1931: Huizinga sent Malinowski `a paper which I made up` and asked for his judgment and possible Rockefeller intervention; correspondence items [897]–[898].
- According to the correspondence apparatus, the packet comprised Huizinga's seven-page signed `Memorandum on the development of a Centre of Anthropological Research in connexion with the reorganization of the Ethnographical Museum at Leyden` plus a two-page signed annex by A. W. Nieuwenhuis.
- The annex title preserved in the project apparatus is `Ethnographical objects as data for psychological research about primitive or semi-cultured races`.
- The old pair apparatus records the annex as **13 July 1931**. That precise date should presently be treated as a project-apparatus reading, not as independently reverified item-level evidence: the public 2019 Huizinga correspondence inventory controls the surrounding 17/19 July correspondence but does not expose the annex as a separate catalogue item.
- The archived Draft 02 apparatus summarizes the annex's analytical chain as ethnographic objects (including wicker-work/textiles/metalwork/carving) -> inferred capacities (observation, memory, causal-logical thought, application/perseverance) -> judgment concerning susceptibility to a `higher stage of civilisation` -> practical implications for colonial administration and indigenous industry. Publication-level quotation still requires the archival copy.

This 1931 packet remains BODY / SECURE as an event and institutional object. Nieuwenhuis's exact classificatory/inferential wording must not be attributed to Huizinga.

## 2. Van Bergen's explicit 1913 antecedent

Leo van Bergen, `De oprichting van de 'Nederlandsche Vereeniging voor Tropische Geneeskunde': een zaak van nationaal belang`, *Studium* 2 (2009), p. 53, explicitly states that **in 1913** Nieuwenhuis wrote an annex for a `Memorandum on the Development of a Centre of Anthropological Research in Connection with the Reorganisation of the Ethnographical Museum at Leyden`, prepared in cooperation with the Leiden university administration.

Van Bergen summarizes Nieuwenhuis's argument as follows: ethnographic-museum objects, normally treated primarily as artistic expressions, could also be used to study the `geestelijk vermogen` of peoples; Nieuwenhuis connected this method to what he had learned among the Dayaks and, from a colonial standpoint, to deciding whether populations were `voor hoogere beschaving vatbaar`.

Van Bergen's note 38 prints the archival reference as `Archief Univ. Leiden, DPL 2591: F9N: Brieven aan Nieuwenhuis`.

Current Leiden University Libraries holdings documentation identifies the **Archive of Anton Willem Nieuwenhuis (1864–1953)** as **BPL 2591, 3321**, collection guide **ubl218**. `DPL 2591` in van Bergen is therefore almost certainly a typographical or siglum error for `BPL 2591`. Preserve the fact that van Bergen printed `DPL`; do not silently pretend the original note already read `BPL`.

Sources:

- Leo van Bergen, *Studium* 2 (2009), article PDF: https://platform.openjournals.nl/studium/article/download/20520/22181
- Leiden University Libraries, `Overzicht van archieven en collecties`, entry `Archief van Anton Willem Nieuwenhuis (1864-1953)`, BPL 2591 / 3321, collection guide ubl218: https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/subject-guides/subject-guides-bijzondere-collecties/versie11-overzicht-van-collecties-en-archieven-oktober-2023.pdf

## 3. Independent institutional context before 1913

The possibility of a genuine 1913 memorandum is historically independent of van Bergen's date.

J. A. Loebèr Jr., `De nieuwe koers in het Rijks Ethnographisch Museum te Leiden`, *De Gids* 76 (1912), pp. 104–121, describes the Leiden museum as a scientific base for ethnographic study during a period of reorganization. The surrounding public discussion makes the museum, its collections, Leiden expertise, systematic ethnographic investigation, and the colonial significance of that research part of the same institutional field before 1913.

This establishes **pre-1931 museum-centre/reorganization context**. It does not by itself identify van Bergen's 1913 memorandum.

Source: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_gid001191201_01/_gid001191201_01_0078.php

## 4. Independent 1913 Nieuwenhuis programme: objects -> capacities

A. W. Nieuwenhuis published in 1913:

*A. W. Nieuwenhuis, Die Veranlagung der malaiischen Völker des ost-indischen Archipels, erläutert an ihren industriellen Erzeugnissen*, *Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie*, Supplement zu Bd. 21 (Leiden / Paris / Leipzig: E. J. Brill / E. Leroux / C. F. Winter, 1913), 56 pp. + xxxi plates.

The title itself states the specific operation at issue: the `Veranlagung` of peoples is to be elucidated through their industrial products. This is much closer than a generic thematic parallel to van Bergen's description of the 1913 annex and to the 1931 project apparatus's object -> capacity chain.

Bibliographic control:

- CiNii Books: https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA77579746
- *Anthropos* 8, no. 6 (Nov.–Dec. 1913), pp. 1164–1165 contains P. W. Koppers's review of the book: https://www.jstor.org/stable/i40019807

A contemporaneous July 1913 colonial-periodical notice also records the appearance of Nieuwenhuis's study as the supplement to volume 21 of the *Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie*. The existence/date of the work is therefore not being inferred retrospectively from the 2009 article.

The Nieuwenhuis programme subsequently continued in further instalments; later parts explicitly treated cognitive capacities including `Das logische Denken`. That continuation supports the existence of a sustained research programme linking material products and mental/cognitive dispositions. It still does not prove that the 1931 annex reused the 1913 text.

## 5. Evidence decision

### 1913 programme / antecedent

**BODY / SECURE as a separately documented antecedent.**

Secure proposition:

> By 1913 Nieuwenhuis was already articulating a Leiden ethnological programme in which material/industrial products could be used to infer collective mental capacities, with an explicitly civilizational and colonial practical horizon; van Bergen independently documents a 1913 annex to a same-titled Centre/museum-reorganization memorandum in Nieuwenhuis's archive.

The conjunction rests on van Bergen's archival citation plus independent 1912 institutional context and Nieuwenhuis's independently bibliographed 1913 *Die Veranlagung* programme.

### 1931 reuse / textual identity

**NOTE / PARTIAL.**

Do **not** presently write:

- `the 1931 annex was written in 1913`;
- `Huizinga reused Nieuwenhuis's 1913 annex`;
- `the 1931 packet copied the 1913 memorandum`;
- `13 July 1931 is certainly a dating error`;
- `van Bergen's 1913 is certainly a typo`.

The identical/same-titled memorandum frame and unusually specific object -> mind/capacity -> civilization/colonial-use chain make textual/institutional continuity a serious hypothesis. But no direct comparison between **BPL 2591 F9N** and the **Yale 1931 packet annex** has yet been controlled.

## 6. Article consequence if direct reuse is proved

If BPL/Yale comparison establishes reuse, the Leiden section changes materially. The 1931 phrase `primitive or semi-cultured races` would then be shown not simply as classificatory language generated inside Rockefeller-era institution building but as the reactivation, transmission, or revision of a prewar Leiden colonial-museum research programme.

That would sharpen the article's non-teleological claim:

`1926 METHOD CRITIQUE OF GENERAL TERMS != CLASSIFICATION DISAPPEARS`

and more specifically:

`OLDER CLASSIFICATORY / INSTITUTIONAL FORM CAN PERSIST OR BE REACTIVATED WHILE ITS EXPLANATORY SUFFICIENCY AND DISCIPLINARY AUTHORITY ARE RENEGOTIATED`

This is potentially stronger evidence for the `waning` argument than treating 1931 classification as a fresh contradiction of Huizinga's 1926 methodological caution.

Do not patch this consequence into Draft 04 before the source-identity test is passed.
