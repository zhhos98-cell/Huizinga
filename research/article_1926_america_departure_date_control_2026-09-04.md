# April 1926 America departure chronology control — 2026-09-04

Purpose: remove an over-precise departure date from Draft 04 while preserving the secure chronology around Huizinga's 7 April lecture and transatlantic journey.

## Secure points

- Huizinga delivered `Taak en termen der beschavingsgeschiedenis` to the Historisch Genootschap at Utrecht on **7 April 1926**. The surviving published record consists of seven theses; DBNL reproduces the meeting report from pp. 5–7.
- On **13 April 1926**, Huizinga wrote to his son Jakob **aboard R.M.S. Berengaria**. The letter says that since the previous day the ship had been meeting a head-on storm. This securely establishes that he was already on the transatlantic passage by 13 April.
- The published *Amerika dagboek* is dated **14 April–19 June 1926**. The diary's start date is not by itself the ship's European departure date.

## Conflict / caution

Jo Tollebeek later wrote that Huizinga left for America the day after the 7 April lecture. That statement cannot presently be reconciled with the controlled shipboard correspondence and should not be used to supply an exact sailing date without passenger/sailing evidence. Conversely, Draft 04's phrase `roughly a week before his 13/14 April departure` is also too precise: 13/14 April is supported as shipboard / diary chronology, not securely as the European departure date.

## Article decision

**BODY / FACTUAL CALIBRATION.** Replace the current departure wording with a minimum secure claim:

`On 7 April 1926, only days before his transatlantic crossing—and six days before a surviving letter places him aboard the *Berengaria*—...`

Do not infer an exact European embarkation date until a passenger list, Cunard sailing schedule, or equivalent primary control is located.
