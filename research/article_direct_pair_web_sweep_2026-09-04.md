## 2. Newly recovered dense direct correspondence, November–December 1932

Inventory entries:

- **13 Nov 1932 — Malinowski (London) → Huizinga** — incipit: `The last I heard from you or your family, was a letter from Jim` — not in *Briefwisseling* — Yale.
- **16 Nov 1932 — Huizinga (Leiden) → Malinowski** — incipit: `It was a great pleasure to have a word from you` — *Briefwisseling* II [951] — Yale.
- **27 Nov 1932 — Malinowski (Hertfordshire) → Huizinga** — incipit: `Many thanks for your letter of the 16 XII` — *Briefwisseling* II [952] — The Hague, Literatuurmuseum. The inventory date is 27 Nov; the `16 XII` incipit therefore likely contains a transcription/date anomaly and should not be normalized silently.
- **1 Dec 1932 — Huizinga (Leiden) → Malinowski** — incipit: `I was very sorry to hear about the state of your health` — not in *Briefwisseling* — Yale.
- **3 Dec 1932 — Huizinga (Leiden) → Malinowski** — incipit: `After writing my letter of yesterday it occurred to me that perhaps my mentioning the fact that I was going to defend you might rouse all sorts of suspicions` — not in *Briefwisseling* — Yale.
- **3 Dec 1932 — Malinowski → Huizinga** — incipit: `Many thanks for your kind letter of December 1st.` — not in *Briefwisseling* — Yale.

### Evidential consequence

The 1932 coda was not a single isolated publication-thank-you. The correspondence inventory shows a rapid sequence of family/personal, health and scholarly/institutional exchange over roughly three weeks.

The phrase `I was going to defend you` is high-value but **UNCONTROLLED AS TO OBJECT/CONTEXT**. Do not equate it with the 1933 KNAW candidature without the letter body or independent nomination records.

Inventory:

### Consequence

The disciplinary self-limitation of 23 May occurred **three days after** another direct scholarly exchange involving a letter and reprint. This is stronger evidence than a generic claim that the friendship continued: non-leadership was articulated inside active circulation, not after a cooling relationship.

## 4. Newly recovered late-1933 Hofstra pair chain — highest article value

Inventory:

- **27 Oct 1933 — Huizinga (Leiden) → Malinowski** — incipit: `This morning I came across a review of Hofstra's book` — *Briefwisseling* II [1059] — Yale.
- **30 Oct 1933 — Malinowski (London) → Huizinga** — incipit: `It was very kind indeed of you` — *Briefwisseling* II [1060] — Yale.

The book is Sjoerd Hofstra’s 1933 dissertation:

**Sjoerd Hofstra, *Differenzierungserscheinungen in einigen afrikanischen Gruppen. Ein Beitrag zur Frage der primitiven Individualität* (Amsterdam: Scheltema & Holkema, 1933).**

Bibliographic controls:
- AfricaBib record: https://www.africabib.org/rec.php?RID=049433954
- Cambridge review metadata, *Africa* 7.2 (1934): https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/africa/article/abs/differenzierungserscheinungen-in-einigen-afrikanischen-gruppen-ein-beitrag-zur-frage-der-primitiven-individualitat-by-drsjoerd-hofstra-pp-214-amsterdam-sheltema-holkemas-boekhandel-nv1933/DA7DB21E9A13E82524435503F5F0F8D2

### Huizinga’s immediate reuse in his own historical argument

On **6 November 1933**, ten days after his letter to Malinowski about a review of Hofstra’s book and seven days after Malinowski’s reply, Huizinga delivered `[Renaissance en Nieuwe Tijd]` at the University of Amsterdam for the Allard Pierson Stichting. The published text cites Hofstra’s book, p. 126, for W. C. Willoughby’s example of a prophet among `natuurvolken` calling people back to old loyalties, with the ideal age located in the past. Huizinga uses the example inside a discussion of the sense of `return` in revolution and the historical relation between Renaissance / new time / past.

Primary text:
- DBNL, `[Renaissance en Nieuwe Tijd]`: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/huiz003verz05_01/huiz003verz05_01_0048.php
- DBNL dates the lecture to 6 Nov 1933 and supplies the Hofstra footnote.

### Article significance

This is the strongest newly recovered bridge from **pair correspondence → anthropology of the primitive → Huizinga’s own historical writing**.

It blocks a teleology in which the 1933 play comparison simply replaces or leaves `primitive` behind. In late October 1933 Huizinga and Malinowski were still exchanging material about a book whose subtitle explicitly posed `the question of primitive individuality`; days later Huizinga used the same book in a historical argument. The ethnographic example did not function simply by making its subjects Europe’s past. It supplied a relation **to the past** — return to old loyalties — inside a European history of temporal self-understanding.

Working formulation:

`PRIMITIVE REMAINS IN CIRCULATION + ETHNOGRAPHIC RELATION ENTERS HISTORICAL ARGUMENT WITHOUT SIMPLE STAGE-EQUIVALENCE`.

## 5. Hofstra / Malinowski relation — current control

Hofstra’s later published work records direct intellectual indebtedness to Malinowski. In his 1937 *Africa* article `Personality and Differentiation in the Political Life of the Mendi`, Hofstra thanked Professor Malinowski for criticism of the first draft and cited the 1933 dissertation as the theoretical background.

Control:
- Cambridge Core article metadata/notes: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/africa/article/abs/personality-and-differentiation-in-the-political-life-of-the-mendi/6CACC9E7673D4E381A7BF595A543BEE1

This confirms a substantive Hofstra–Malinowski intellectual relation, but it does **not by itself date the precise form of that relation to October 1933**. Do not overstate the 1933 supervision/assistantship relation without a contemporaneous control.

## 6. Historiographical counterpoint: G. W. Locher, 1958

G. W. Locher, `Huizinga en de culturele antropologie`, *Bijdragen tot de taal-, land- en volkenkunde* 114 (1958): 170–191, explicitly argued that Huizinga’s global image of primitive culture was **not modified** by the work and figure of the admired Malinowski. Locher also stressed an internal ambiguity in Malinowski: anti-evolutionist and yet repeatedly using evolutionary views; synchronic/functionalist description presented as exemplary `savage society` and generalized broadly.

Open-access text:
- Brill PDF: https://brill.com/view/journals/bki/114/1-2/article-p170_10.pdf
- DOI metadata: https://doi.org/10.1163/22134379-90002273

### Article use

Locher is a direct historiographical challenge, not merely support. A useful relation is:

`LOCHER: MALINOWSKI DID NOT MODIFY HUIZINGA’S GLOBAL IMAGE OF PRIMITIVE CULTURE`

`THIS ARTICLE: CATEGORY/IMAGE CAN PERSIST WHILE ITS INFERENTIAL AND EXPLANATORY WORK IS REDISTRIBUTED`.

The argument should therefore avoid claiming a clean conceptual conversion of Huizinga’s primitive. The stronger claim is that the pair archive lets us observe **persistence plus changing work**.
