# 1919–1933 relational vocabulary / denser configuration control — 2026-09-04

Purpose: prevent the article from implying that Malinowski supplied Huizinga with relational vocabulary de novo. The controlled issue is not lexical origin but what ethnographic description did to the configuration and evidentiary burden of comparison.

## 1919 baseline: *Herfsttij der Middeleeuwen*

In the 1919 first edition Huizinga was already treating several terms later named in the Huizinga–Malinowski exchange as historical relations or socially organized qualities.

- **Friendship (`vriendschap`)**: Huizinga describes fifteenth-century friendship as a stylized social relation. The princely `mignon` is explicitly called `een geformaliseerd instituut`; friendship is tracked through blood-brotherhood, sworn companionship, equal dress, rank difference and court practice. Collected-works control: *Verzamelde Werken* III, DBNL TEI `sources/dbnl/verzamelde_werken/huiz003verz04_01.xml`, VW pp. 63–64.
- **Generosity / `mildheid`**: in his reconstruction of aristocratic social ideals, Huizinga lists `Waarheid, dapperheid, zedelijkheid en mildheid` among the qualities assigned to nobility, and elsewhere `mildheid` appears among the aristocratic virtues of the *Roman de la rose*. VW III, pp. 68, 138.
- **Efficacy of words**: chapter XVII already states that belief in `de werking van het gesproken woord` appears in its fullness in primitive culture and persists in late-medieval blessings, magical formulas and legal formulas. This is separately controlled in `research/article_1919_herfsttij_spoken_word_pair_control_2026-09-04.md`.

These passages establish an old historian's vocabulary of friendship, generosity and verbal efficacy; they do not establish a Malinowskian method before Malinowski.

## 31 December 1926 pair exchange

Immediately after `We emphasize the functional side of cultural phenomena`, Huizinga names medieval / feudal relations, the efficacy of words, and sentiments including vanity, generosity and friendship. The important fact is therefore not that Malinowski introduced those nouns. Huizinga attached an already active historical repertoire to the new book exchange and to Malinowski's ethnological descriptions.

## 1933 configuration

In *Over de grenzen van spel en ernst in de cultuur*, the Kula/potlatch sequence again names `mildheid, vriendschap, vertrouwen, eer, hoogmoed en avontuur`. But the comparison is carried by a denser configuration: circulation, reciprocal obligation, trust, prestige, competitive display, turn-taking and return. The old moral / relational vocabulary is embedded in observed and reconstructed sequences rather than functioning as a free-standing analogical list.

## Article decision

**BODY / ONE-SENTENCE CALIBRATION.** Replace the sentence `Malinowski’s ethnography supplied relations; Huizinga tested their historical reach.` It overstates one-way supply. Preferred controlled formulation:

`Malinowski’s ethnography did not supply the relational vocabulary from scratch. It bound older historical objects—generosity, friendship and the efficacy of words—to observed circuits of reciprocity, obligation, trust and exchange; Huizinga tested that denser configuration’s historical reach.`

No new paragraph. The point is lexical continuity plus relational thickening, not influence reversal or a claim that all later relations were already present in 1919.
