# KVB printed catalogue, vol. 6 (1940–1949): direct control for Saint Lucas / Bibliopolis — 2026-09-04

_Status: high-priority contemporary bibliographic control. The volume is digitized and its internal page architecture is now identified; target-name hits have not yet been extracted from the full register._

## 1. The exact contemporary catalogue exists and covers our window

The sixth volume of the printed catalogue of the Bibliotheek van de Vereeniging ter Bevordering van de Belangen des Boekhandels is:

**Emma Dronckers, _Catalogus der Bibliotheek van de Vereeniging ter bevordering van de belangen des boekhandels te Amsterdam. Dl. VI. Supplement-Catalogus 1940–1949, met Register_ ('s-Gravenhage: Martinus Nijhoff, 1949), XX + 400 pp.**

A contemporary review in _Het Boek_ confirms that Dronckers issued it in 1949 on retiring after twenty-five years as librarian and that it records acquisitions for 1940–49.

Sources:
- _Het Boek_ review: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_boe031194901_01/_boe031194901_01_0019.php
- Folger catalogue record for the multi-volume series: https://catalog.folger.edu/record/28249

## 2. Why this is unusually important for Catalogue 3

The earlier printed catalogue's fourth volume explicitly organized the book-trade material into categories including:

- `Fondscatalogi`
- `Catalogi van fondsveilingen`
- **`Antiquariaats- en magazijncatalogi`**
- `Catalogi van boeken-, prenten- en teekeningen veilingen`

Volume 6 is the supplement covering **1940–1949**, exactly the period in which:

- Saint Lucas Catalogue 2 appeared (1943);
- Rapiarijs circulated (1946–48);
- the postwar Catalogue 3 / _Wetenschappelijke adversaria_ appeared or was advertised (ca. 1948);
- _Bibliopolis_ was founded (ca. 1948).

Thus the KVB's own 1949 printed supplement is a **contemporary accession/control catalogue**, not a later retrospective search tool.

Source:
- Folger catalogue record, contents of vols. 4 and 6: https://catalog.folger.edu/record/28249

## 4. Search targets inside vol. 6

First search the **alphabetical register, pp. 357–400**, for:

- `Saint Lucas Society`
- `St. Lucas Society`
- `Saint Lucas`
- `Vosmaer`
- `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`
- `Bibliopolis`
- `Rapiarijs`
- `Rapenburg`
- `Wyttenbachweg` / `Wijttenbachweg`
- `Oegstgeest`

Then inspect **pp. 281–302** and any pages pointed to by the register for dealer/catalogue entries.

Because Catalogue 3 may have been accessioned under a generic issuer heading, also inspect neighbouring entries under `Leiden`, `Oegstgeest`, `antiquariaat`, `catalogus/catalogue`, and any Saint Lucas series heading.

## 5. Negative search results must be interpreted carefully

The current web `find` interface against the Delpher landing page does **not** search the entire 400-page OCR object; attempts to find `Vosmaer`, `Saint Lucas`, `Bibliopolis`, and `Rapenburg` through that interface returned no match only on the surfaced page/context. This is **not a validated negative result for the volume**.

Likewise, Brill's public landing page does not expose searchable full-book text without entering the chapter/PDF layer. The next step requires either:

1. page-level Delpher OCR search/navigation;
2. direct inspection of the 44-page alphabetical register;
3. institutional/full-text Brill access;
4. asking KVB/Allard Pierson staff to check the printed register.

## 6. Stronger institutional implication

A 2005–06 report of the Bibliotheek van het Boekenvak records that its 1990s database of **personalia and business data (`bedrijfsgegevens`)** was specifically cleaned and expanded for **twentieth-century business records** in 2005, and was then accessible both via the UvA website and the old `Bibliopolis` information system.

This supplies a second, independent KVB data layer:

- printed accession/catalogue control: **vol. 6 (1940–49)**;
- business/person authority data: **20th-century bedrijfsgegevens database**.

If Saint Lucas does not appear by catalogue title, C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's firm may still have a business authority record formerly exposed through that database.

Source:
- Stichting Bibliotheek van het Boekenvak, Jaarverslag 2005–2006: https://www.bibliotheekvanhetboekenvak.nl/doc/SBB%20Jaarverslag%202005_2006.pdf

## 7. Current priority

This route should now rank **above generic union-catalogue title searching**. A hit in the 1949 KVB supplement would establish that a Saint Lucas/Bibliopolis item was already in the industry's own library contemporaneously and could provide the older shelfmark or entry language needed to retrieve it today.

If no hit exists after the actual register has been inspected, that will be a meaningful negative result. Until then, ordinary web silence has little evidential value.
