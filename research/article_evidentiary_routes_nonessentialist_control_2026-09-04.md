# Evidentiary routes — non-essentialist wording control — 2026-09-04

Purpose: keep the article's contrast between ethnographic and historical warrant tied to the dossiers actually reconstructed, rather than turning it into an essential distinction between disciplines.

Current wording says that for `primitive` the step from classification to historical claim `could not be the same in an observed social field and in a damaged textual or visual tradition`. This is stronger and more philosophical than the evidence requires.

The secure claim is narrower:

- Malinowski's published ethnographic descriptions used observation of living social relations;
- Huizinga's controlled historical dossiers show claims passing through notes, copies, photographs, autograph letters, textual transmission, preservation and correction;
- these materials therefore document different evidentiary routes in the comparison reconstructed here.

They do not establish that anthropology and history must always have categorically different warrants.

## Article decision

Replace the final formulation with dossier-specific language: `In the materials traced here, a relation described through field observation and a historical claim assembled through damaged textual or visual transmission were warranted through different checks.`
