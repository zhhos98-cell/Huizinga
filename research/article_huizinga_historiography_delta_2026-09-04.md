# Huizinga historiography — article-facing delta, 2026-09-04

_Status: bounded secondary-literature control created because Draft 04 underused Huizinga scholarship. This is not a generic literature review. A source belongs in the article only when the archive confirms, narrows, redirects, or complicates its proposition._

## 1. Anthropology: Arnade → Vale → this article

Peter Arnade, `Huizinga: Anthropologist Avant la Lettre?`, in *Rereading Huizinga* (2019), pp. 259–274, emphasizes Huizinga's anthropological affinities and argues that his treatment of ritual, symbol and cultural imagination anticipates later anthropological thinking, especially symbolic anthropology.

Malcolm Vale's 2021 *English Historical Review* essay/review pushes against making that genealogy too clean. Vale stresses that Huizinga's early philological, linguistic and comparative-linguistic formation remained active in his historical work and warns that accounts eager to make him an early anthropological historian can set that formation aside.

**Article relation:** Draft 04 should not choose between `anthropologist avant la lettre` and `philologist who remained a historian`. The Huizinga–Malinowski correspondence makes the tension historical rather than classificatory. Huizinga's traffic with anthropology was real and direct: books, correspondence, teaching, comparison and institutional planning. Yet the same archive repeatedly records limits to disciplinary conversion (`benevolent outsider`; `Being no anthropologist...`). The case therefore confirms Arnade's anthropological proximity while supporting Vale's warning against retrospective disciplinary relabelling. It adds actor-made evidence that proximity and non-conversion were simultaneous.

Best placement: the existing Travel paragraph that currently cites Vale. Replace `Recent reassessment has warned...` with a three-way historiographical relation rather than adding a separate literature-review paragraph.

References:
- Arnade, P. (2019) `Huizinga: Anthropologist Avant la Lettre?`, in P. Arnade, M. Howell and A. van der Lem (eds), *Rereading Huizinga: Autumn of the Middle Ages, a Century Later*. Amsterdam: Amsterdam University Press, 259–274.
- Vale, M. (2021) `Huizinga's Autumntide: The centenary of a masterpiece`, *The English Historical Review* 136(580): 672–693.

## 2. Interwar present: Rydin → this article

Thor Rydin's *The Works and Times of Johan Huizinga (1872–1945): Writing History in the Age of Collapse* (Amsterdam University Press, 2024) relocates Huizinga's historical work inside experiences of uncertainty, rupture and loss. Its governing claim is that Huizinga's historical perspectives were informed by these transformations and that historical work helped him make meaning of them.

**Article relation:** Draft 04 inherits Rydin's insistence that Huizinga be historicized inside his interwar present rather than treated as a timeless theorist of culture. It shifts the scale from experiences of loss to the contemporaneous reorganization of the human sciences. The pair archive shows Huizinga responding to his present not only through historical representations of rupture but through practical calibration among disciplines, foundations, museums, books and institutional projects. This is an extension in object, not a refutation of Rydin.

Best placement: one sentence in the introduction, attached to the paragraph that explains why the Huizinga–Malinowski encounter matters as an interwar event.

Reference:
- Rydin, T. (2024) *The Works and Times of Johan Huizinga (1872–1945): Writing History in the Age of Collapse*. Amsterdam: Amsterdam University Press.

## 3. Source work: Small / van der Lem → this article

Graeme Small and Anton van der Lem's contributions to *Rereading Huizinga* (2019) make Huizinga's source practice itself an object of historiography. Small reconstructs the selective narrative-source base of *Herfsttij*. Van der Lem reconstructs the archive's material organization, including the subject/thematic pigeonhole system and hundreds of envelopes of notes.

The 2024 unabridged English *Autumntide of the Middle Ages*, edited by Small and van der Lem and translated by Diane Webb, continues this source-critical recovery through a complete source bibliography and editorial attention to Huizinga's revisions.

**Article relation:** Draft 04 extends this scholarship beyond the making of *Herfsttij*. Its American cards, travel-memory contrast, Burgundian photographs, autograph corrections and history/science notes show later comparative work moving through multiple inscription regimes. The claim should be modest: not that Huizinga used one unchanged `method`, but that material source organization remained visible while the media, checks and comparative objects changed.

Best placement: Travel, after the American-notes paragraph or immediately before the Burgundian dossier paragraph. One or two sentences are enough.

References:
- Small, G. (2019) `The Making of The Autumn of the Middle Ages I: Narrative Sources and Their Treatment in Huizinga's Herfsttij`, in P. Arnade, M. Howell and A. van der Lem (eds), *Rereading Huizinga*, 169–210.
- van der Lem, A. (2019) `The Making of The Autumn of the Middle Ages II: The Eagle and His Pigeonholes: How Huizinga Organized His Sources`, in P. Arnade, M. Howell and A. van der Lem (eds), *Rereading Huizinga*, 211–226.
- Huizinga, J. (2024) *Autumntide of the Middle Ages*, trans. D. Webb, ed. G. Small and A. van der Lem. Leiden: Leiden University Press.

## 4. French / Annales reception: Greilsammer — note-level control, not a new scene

Myriam Greilsammer's `A Late and Ambivalent Recognition` in *Rereading Huizinga* reconstructs the uneven and sometimes theoretically dissonant relationship between Huizinga and French historians, including Bloch. The useful point for Draft 04 is not to turn the article into a Huizinga–Annales story. It is to block an ex nihilo account of Malinowski: before the surviving pair exchange, Huizinga had already read and judged a major historical treatment of ritual efficacy and persistent belief in his 1925 review of Bloch's *Les rois thaumaturges*.

**Article relation:** Greilsammer makes the pre-contact intellectual environment more complicated; Draft 04 uses the 1925 review only as a control. Malinowski changed the direct traffic, examples and jurisdictional problem, not Huizinga's ability to recognize ritual efficacy as a historical subject from nothing.

Best placement: note-level addition attached to the first claim that Malinowski's questions entered Huizinga's historical method. Do not add a Bloch paragraph.

Reference:
- Greilsammer, M. (2019) `A Late and Ambivalent Recognition: (The Autumn of) Johan Huizinga and the French Historians of the nouvelle histoire`, in P. Arnade, M. Howell and A. van der Lem (eds), *Rereading Huizinga*, 275–308.
