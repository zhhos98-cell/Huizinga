# KVK / KVB discovery route for Saint Lucas Catalogue 3

_Status: active external-research note, 2026-09-03._

## Why this route matters

The target is the postwar Saint Lucas Society Catalogue 3, described by J.F. Heijbroek as **`Wetenschappelijke adversaria`** and advertised around 1948 with Huizinga-associated material. Exact-title web searches have not yet produced a surviving copy or call number. This may be a cataloguing problem rather than evidence of loss.

## KVK as metacatalogue radar

The Karlsruhe Virtual Catalog (KVK) is useful as a cross-catalogue discovery layer. Search variants should include:

- `Saint Lucas Society`
- `St. Lucas Society`
- `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`
- `Rapenburg 83`
- `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`
- `Catalogue 3` / `Catalogus 3`

KVK results should be treated as pointers to underlying union/library records, not final evidence.

## KVB / Allard Pierson is now the strongest repository prior

Heijbroek's 2019 postscript records that **Catalogue I of the Saint Lucas Society was found in the library of the Vereniging met de Lange Naam (now KVB) at the University Library Amsterdam**. That copy is manually annotated `’s-Gravenhage (1938)`. This is direct proof that at least one Saint Lucas bookseller catalogue survives in the KVB collection and that individual copies can preserve unique handwritten metadata.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, postscript in *De Boekenwereld* 35.4 (2019): https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-35_4-2019compressed.pdf

Allard Pierson currently lists a dedicated **collection of antiquarian booksellers' and auction catalogues** among its book-trade holdings.

Source:
- Allard Pierson, Book trade: https://www.allardpierson.nl/en/book-trade

An older KVB library description estimated approximately **150,000 antiquarian, publisher and auction catalogues**. This is a historical figure, not a current audited count, but confirms the scale and collecting remit.

## Why exact-title searches may fail

KVB annual reports provide an important technical explanation. By 1999 the auction-catalogue database held about 22,500 records. The library then began a separate project to automate the antiquarian-bookseller catalogue collection. The report explicitly notes that **antiquarian catalogues are harder to standardize because essential bibliographic data are often missing**. In 2000 the project to enter the antiquarian-catalogue collection into the automated catalogue was underway.

Consequently, Saint Lucas Catalogue 3 may be catalogued under issuer/bookseller, place, series, an internal collection heading, or minimal descriptive metadata rather than under the advertised subtitle `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`.

Sources:
- KVB Jaarverslag 1999: https://catalogus.boekman.nl/pub/JS/K04-1999.pdf
- KVB Jaarverslag 2000: https://catalogus.boekman.nl/pub/JS/K04-2000.pdf

## Legacy UvA digital-object route

UvA Erfgoed demonstrates that KVB catalogues can be digitized as full digital objects. Example:

**Van Stockum's Antiquariaat, *Boekveilingcatalogus, 25 mei 1936*** — shelfmark **UBM: KVB NV 4337**, with persistent handle, downloadable METS, IIIF manifest and page images.

Source:
- https://uvaerfgoed.nl/viewer/image/11245_3_25589/

This proves the technical possibility that an otherwise hard-to-find KVB catalogue can survive in the legacy UvA viewer with item-level digital images. No Saint Lucas Catalogue 3 object has yet been located there.

## Related old Bibliopolis database

The former `bibliopolis.nl` platform now redirects to the KB's **Nederlandse Boekgeschiedenis** site. Its `Personen` search explicitly retains **Personalia BKVB** among the source databases for Dutch printers, publishers and booksellers through the twentieth century. This is potentially useful for reconstructing the Saint Lucas / C.J.J.G. Vosmaer business record, although dynamic result pages are not well indexed by Google.

Sources:
- https://www.nederlandseboekgeschiedenis.nl/nl/personen
- https://www.nederlandseboekgeschiedenis.nl/

## KVB printed master catalogue, volume VIII

A potentially decisive offline/search-only index is the KVB's own printed master catalogue:

**`Catalogus der Bibliotheek van de Vereeniging ter Bevordering van de Belangen des Boekhandels te Amsterdam. Achtste deel. Supplement-catalogus 1932-1973.`** Amsterdam: Universiteitsbibliotheek, 1979, approximately 602 pages.

A contemporary review describes its internal structure in unusually useful detail. The section on **antiquarian and magazijn catalogues occupies roughly pp. 113–380/381**, arranged alphabetically by antiquarian bookseller and chronologically under each firm, with catalogue title/number and number of entries or pages. Later sections index book/print/drawing auction catalogues and provide extensive indexes.

This means that Saint Lucas Catalogues 1 (1938), 2 (1943), and the postwar Catalogue 3 fall squarely within the chronological scope of volume VIII. If copies had entered the KVB collection by the 1973 cutoff, this printed index should in principle list them under `Saint Lucas Society`, `St. Lucas Society`, `Vosmaer`, or another firm heading and could supply catalogue number/title and physical extent even if modern catalogue metadata are poor.

Important caveat: Heijbroek's discovery of Catalogue I at KVB in 2019 does **not** prove that the copy was already in the collection by 1973. Absence from volume VIII would therefore not prove loss or non-holdings today.

Sources:
- Review / bibliographical description of volume VIII in *De Gulden Passer* (1979/1980), including section layout.
- Bibliographical chronology describing pp. 113–381 as the antiquarian/magazijn-catalogue section.

### Online status

Internet Archive exposes searchable full text for KVB volume VI (`catalogusderbibl06vereuoft`), but no public full-text scan of volume VIII has yet been located. This makes volume VIII itself a high-value target for library digitization, snippet search, or a search-only surrogate.

## HathiTrust search-only lead

The HathiTrust serial record for the KVB catalogue exposes several University of Michigan digitized volumes. Its MARC holdings map includes:

- v.1 — `mdp.39015081563127`
- v.2 — `mdp.39015081563135`
- v.4 — `mdp.39015081562996`
- v.5 — `mdp.39015081563291`
- v.6 — `mdp.39015081563143`
- v.7 — `mdp.39015081563309`
- **`Suppl.` — `mdp.39015081563283`**

The `Suppl.` item is restricted to **search-only** access. HathiTrust documentation states that limited/search-only volumes can still be searched for terms and return page/scan hit information, which could be enough to locate `Saint Lucas`, `Vosmaer`, or `Wetenschappelijke adversaria` without reading the full copyrighted volume.

However, the MARC enumeration identifies the Michigan item only as **`Suppl.`**. It has **not yet been proved** that `mdp.39015081563283` is the 1979 volume VIII / 1932–1973 supplement. The serial record separately enumerates `v.7`, and the contents note does not resolve the later volume sequence. Do not cite this HathiTrust ID as volume VIII until its date/volume designation is independently established.

Operational next step: resolve the Michigan barcode/HTID against the University of Michigan item record or another MARC holding, then use HathiTrust's within-volume text search for `Saint Lucas`, `St. Lucas`, `Vosmaer`, `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`, and `Rapenburg`.

## Next searches

1. Resolve HathiTrust `mdp.39015081563283` (`Suppl.`) to an exact volume/date; if it is the 1979 volume VIII, run search-only term queries against it.
2. Search union catalogues by issuer and address rather than title alone.
3. Search Allard Pierson/KVB for adjacent Saint Lucas catalogue records, starting from the known Catalogue I copy.
4. Search legacy UvA Erfgoed using Saint Lucas / Vosmaer / Rapenburg / catalogue-number variants.
5. If the public catalogue remains opaque, ask the Allard Pierson book-history curator for the **shelfmark of Saint Lucas Catalogue I** and whether adjacent Saint Lucas catalogues, especially no. 3, are present in the antiquarian-catalogue collection.
6. Treat any annotated KVB copy as particularly valuable: Catalogue I already demonstrates that handwritten date/place metadata can survive only on the physical copy.
