# KVK / KVB discovery route for Saint Lucas Catalogue 3

_Status: active external-research note, 2026-09-03._

## Why this route matters

The target is the postwar Saint Lucas Society Catalogue 3, described by J.F. Heijbroek as **`Wetenschappelijke adversaria`** and advertised around 1948 with Huizinga-associated material. Exact-title web searches have not yet produced a surviving copy or call number. This may be a cataloguing problem rather than evidence of loss.

## KVK as metacatalogue radar

The Karlsruhe Virtual Catalog (KVK) is useful as a cross-catalogue discovery layer. Search variants should include:

- `Saint Lucas Society`
- `St. Lucas Society`
- `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`
- `Rapenburg 83`
- `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`
- `Catalogue 3` / `Catalogus 3`

KVK results should be treated as pointers to underlying union/library records, not final evidence.

## KVB / Allard Pierson is now the strongest repository prior

Heijbroek's 2019 postscript records that **Catalogue I of the Saint Lucas Society was found in the library of the Vereniging met de Lange Naam (now KVB) at the University Library Amsterdam**. That copy is manually annotated `’s-Gravenhage (1938)`. This is direct proof that at least one Saint Lucas bookseller catalogue survives in the KVB collection and that individual copies can preserve unique handwritten metadata.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, postscript in *De Boekenwereld* 35.4 (2019): https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-35_4-2019compressed.pdf

Allard Pierson currently lists a dedicated **collection of antiquarian booksellers' and auction catalogues** among its book-trade holdings.

Source:
- Allard Pierson, Book trade: https://www.allardpierson.nl/en/book-trade

An older KVB library description estimated approximately **150,000 antiquarian, publisher and auction catalogues**. This is a historical figure, not a current audited count, but confirms the scale and collecting remit.

## Why exact-title searches may fail

KVB annual reports provide an important technical explanation. By 1999 the auction-catalogue database held about 22,500 records. The library then began a separate project to automate the antiquarian-bookseller catalogue collection. The report explicitly notes that **antiquarian catalogues are harder to standardize because essential bibliographic data are often missing**. In 2000 the project to enter the antiquarian-catalogue collection into the automated catalogue was underway.

Consequently, Saint Lucas Catalogue 3 may be catalogued under issuer/bookseller, place, series, an internal collection heading, or minimal descriptive metadata rather than under the advertised subtitle `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`.

Sources:
- KVB Jaarverslag 1999: https://catalogus.boekman.nl/pub/JS/K04-1999.pdf
- KVB Jaarverslag 2000: https://catalogus.boekman.nl/pub/JS/K04-2000.pdf

## Legacy UvA digital-object route

UvA Erfgoed demonstrates that KVB catalogues can be digitized as full digital objects. Example:

**Van Stockum's Antiquariaat, *Boekveilingcatalogus, 25 mei 1936*** — shelfmark **UBM: KVB NV 4337**, with persistent handle, downloadable METS, IIIF manifest and page images.

Source:
- https://uvaerfgoed.nl/viewer/image/11245_3_25589/

This proves the technical possibility that an otherwise hard-to-find KVB catalogue can survive in the legacy UvA viewer with item-level digital images. No Saint Lucas Catalogue 3 object has yet been located there.

## Related old Bibliopolis database

The former `bibliopolis.nl` platform now redirects to the KB's **Nederlandse Boekgeschiedenis** site. Its `Personen` search explicitly retains **Personalia BKVB** among the source databases for Dutch printers, publishers and booksellers through the twentieth century. This is potentially useful for reconstructing the Saint Lucas / C.J.J.G. Vosmaer business record, although dynamic result pages are not well indexed by Google.

Sources:
- https://www.nederlandseboekgeschiedenis.nl/nl/personen
- https://www.nederlandseboekgeschiedenis.nl/

## Next searches

1. Search union catalogues by issuer and address rather than title alone.
2. Search Allard Pierson/KVB for adjacent Saint Lucas catalogue records, starting from the known Catalogue I copy.
3. Search legacy UvA Erfgoed using Saint Lucas / Vosmaer / Rapenburg / catalogue-number variants.
4. If the public catalogue remains opaque, ask the Allard Pierson book-history curator for the **shelfmark of Saint Lucas Catalogue I** and whether adjacent Saint Lucas catalogues, especially no. 3, are present in the antiquarian-catalogue collection.
5. Treat any annotated KVB copy as particularly valuable: Catalogue I already demonstrates that handwritten date/place metadata can survive only on the physical copy.
