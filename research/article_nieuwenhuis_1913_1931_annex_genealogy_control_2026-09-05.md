# Nieuwenhuis 1913–1931 annex genealogy control — 2026-09-05

Purpose: control whether the Nieuwenhuis annex circulated with Huizinga's July 1931 Leiden Centre memorandum should be treated as newly generated 1931 classificatory language or as a reuse / revision of an older Leiden ethnological programme.

## Current evidentiary split

### BODY / SECURE — 1913 antecedent programme existed

Leo van Bergen, `De oprichting van de 'Nederlandsche Vereeniging voor Tropische Geneeskunde': een zaak van nationaal belang`, *Studium* 2 (2009), pp. 92–104, explicitly states that in **1913** A. W. Nieuwenhuis wrote an annex for a `Memorandum on the Development of a Centre of Anthropological Research in Connection with the Reorganisation of the Ethnographical Museum at Leyden`, prepared in cooperation with the Leiden university administration. Van Bergen summarizes the annex as treating ethnographical museum objects not merely as art works but as evidence for the mental capacities of peoples, with colonial importance because such evidence could indicate whether they were `voor hoogere beschaving vatbaar`. His note 38 cites `Archief Univ. Leiden, DPL 2591: F9N: Brieven aan Nieuwenhuis`.

Leiden University Libraries' current overview of special collections identifies Anton Willem Nieuwenhuis's archive as **BPL 2591, 3321**, collection guide `ubl218`. The `DPL 2591` in van Bergen is therefore almost certainly a shelfmark typo for **BPL 2591**; do not silently normalize the subitem `F9N` until the collection guide / original folder is directly inspected.

Independent 1913 publication evidence confirms that this was already Nieuwenhuis's active research programme. In 1913 Brill published his *Die Veranlagung der malaiischen Völker des Ost-Indischen Archipels, erläutert an ihren industriellen Erzeugnissen* as a supplement to vol. 21 of *Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie* (56 pp. + 31 plates). A colonial weekly dated **17 July 1913** announced the supplement as newly published and described it as a topic already discussed in the Nederlandsche Anthropologische Vereeniging. Later installments continued the project, including `Das logische Denken`.

This independently secures the historical proposition:

`MATERIAL / INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTS -> INFERRED CAPACITY / VERANLAGUNG` was already a Nieuwenhuis research operation by 1913.

### NOTE / PARTIAL — relationship to the 1931 annex

The Huizinga–Malinowski pair apparatus controls a July 1931 packet consisting of Huizinga's seven-page signed memorandum plus a two-page signed Nieuwenhuis annex titled `Ethnographical objects as data for psychological research about primitive or semi-cultured races`. The old pair control records the annex as **13 July 1931** and the Huizinga memorandum as **17 July 1931**; Huizinga sent the packet to Malinowski on 19 July.

The 1931 annex is summarized in the repo as moving through an unusually specific chain:

`wicker-work / textiles / metalwork / carving -> observation / memory / causal-logical thought / application / perseverance -> susceptibility to a higher stage of civilisation -> administration / indigenous industry`.

This is strongly congruent with both van Bergen's 1913 annex summary and Nieuwenhuis's 1913 *Veranlagung* programme. However, the repository does **not** currently contain a diplomatic transcription of the 1931 annex or a visible primary-source statement proving that the 1931 two-page annex was copied, revised, translated, or re-dated from a 1913 text.

Therefore:

`1913 PROGRAMME / ANNEX ANTECEDENT = SECURE`

`1931 TEXTUAL REUSE / DIRECT GENEALOGY = PARTIAL / UNPROVEN`

Do **not** currently write in Draft 04 that the 1931 annex was an eighteen-year-old text, a reprint, or a direct reuse.

## Chronology discrepancy to preserve

There is an unresolved date tension:

- van Bergen (2009) deliberately places the annex in a sequence `1904 -> 1913 -> 1917` and explicitly says `In 1913 schreef Nieuwenhuis een annex...`;
- the repo's older direct-pair apparatus records an annex of the same title as `13 July 1931`.

The similarity between `1913` and `13 July 1931` makes a date-transposition hypothesis tempting, but the van Bergen passage is contextually deliberate rather than a stray isolated date. Do not declare either source mistaken without direct inspection of **BPL 2591 / F9N** and/or the physical 1931 annex.

## Article consequence

The secure 1913 antecedent is already historically significant even without proving direct textual reuse. It shows that the classificatory / psychological logic appearing in the 1931 institutional packet had a substantial prehistory in Nieuwenhuis's own Leiden ethnology rather than being naturally attributable to Rockefeller-era institution building or to Malinowski.

This sharpens the article's anti-teleological control:

`HUIZINGA'S 1926 CAUTION ABOUT GENERAL TERMS != OLD DEVELOPMENTAL / COLONIAL CLASSIFICATION DISAPPEARS FROM LEIDEN INSTITUTIONAL PRACTICE`.

If direct reuse is later proved, the stronger formulation becomes available:

`1931 INSTITUTION BUILDING REACTIVATED AN OLDER LEIDEN ETHNOLOGICAL PROGRAMME`.

Until then, keep this consequence in research/control or a bounded note, not as a body-level genealogy claim.

## Reopen criterion

Reopen this branch only if one of the following becomes available:

1. direct access to Leiden UBL **BPL 2591 / F9N** with item dates/descriptions;
2. scan/transcription of the signed two-page Nieuwenhuis annex circulated with Huizinga's July 1931 memorandum;
3. an independent 1912–1914 Leiden university / museum record naming the same memorandum title and Nieuwenhuis annex;
4. a source explicitly stating that the 1931 packet reused, translated, or revised the earlier memorandum.

Do not repeat generic searches for the English memorandum title or infer genealogy from title similarity alone.
