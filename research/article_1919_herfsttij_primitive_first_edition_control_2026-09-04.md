# 1919 *Herfsttij* primitive baseline — first-edition control — 2026-09-04

Purpose: source-lock a pre-Malinowski occurrence of `primitive` in Huizinga's historical practice before any local Draft 04 revision. This is category-specific documentary history, **not** use of the late Middle Ages as an analogy for the later waning of `primitive`.

## Edition control

The DBNL collected-works text of *Herfsttij der Middeleeuwen* is set from the fifth edition (1941), so its body cannot by itself prove that a sentence belonged to the 1919 first edition.

The 1919 first-edition scan independently confirms the relevant wording. Public first-edition witnesses checked 2026-09-04:

- Dutch Wikisource, scan page `Pagina:Huizinga, Herfsttij der Middeleeuwen (1919).pdf/182`;
- Project Gutenberg transcription explicitly titled *Herfsttij der Middeleeuwen* (1919).

Both preserve the passage from the symbolism chapter.

## Controlled wording

In the discussion of symbolic thought Huizinga first describes symbolism, from the standpoint of causal thought, as a mental short circuit: a relation is grasped through meaning and purpose rather than through a causal chain. He then writes that, from an ethnological standpoint, one may call this `een zeer primitieve geestesfunctie`. He characterizes `het primitieve denken` by weak perception of identity boundaries between things and says the symbolizing function is closely connected with it.

The surrounding first-edition text continues to connect medieval realist/symbolic thought with the `wijsheid van den wilde, het kind, den dichter en den mysticus`; elsewhere in the same chapter Huizinga makes direct comparisons between medieval and ethnological material. Only the first controlled sentence is needed for Draft 04.

## Article function

**PRE-CONTACT BASELINE / COUNTEREVIDENCE TO ONE-WAY INFLUENCE.** Before meeting Malinowski, Huizinga was already using an explicitly ethnological `primitive` to characterize a European historical form of thought. The 1926 encounter therefore did not introduce the possibility of carrying ethnological comparison into medieval history. What changes in the pair exchange is better located in the relations, practices and evidentiary work that are made to carry comparison.

This finding must not be used to argue that *Herfsttij* models the later history or `waning` of `primitive`. It is evidence only for Huizinga's own pre-contact use of the category.
