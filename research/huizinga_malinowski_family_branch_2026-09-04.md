# Huizinga–Malinowski family-retention branch — 2026-09-04

_Status: active provenance hypothesis note. This branch is deliberately kept separate from the Vosmaer/Saint Lucas route because Huizinga's books were divided among his children before/alongside the sale of the majority to C.J.J.G. Vosmaer._

## 1. Methodological correction: the three Malinowski books cannot yet be presumed to have entered Vosmaer's stock

Huizinga Online's account of the library dispersal is structurally two-stage: after Johan Huizinga's death, books were **distributed among the children according to their interests or retained as mementos**, while **the majority** was sold to the Leiden antiquarian C.J.J.G. Vosmaer. The word `majority` leaves a real family-retention branch.

Therefore the working provenance tree for the three Malinowski presentation copies must remain:

`Malinowski -> Johan Huizinga -> {family-retained branch | Vosmaer/Saint Lucas branch | other later disposition}`

The Saint Lucas route remains the strongest business/dispersal target, but it should not absorb the family branch by assumption.

Source:
- Huizinga Online, `Leven | Life`: https://huizinga-online.nl/leven-life/

## 2. Direct 1926 clue: Huizinga singled out Malinowski to Leonhard

In a letter from Boston dated **19 May 1926**, addressed to his children and Heleen Krayenhoff, Huizinga specifically turns to his son Leonhard:

> `Leonhard, ik zal je als ik terug ben, meer vertellen over mijn nieuwen vriend Malinowski; hij is zeer interessant, hij heeft punten van overeenkomst met Jolles, maar ook groote verschillen.`

This is significant because it is not a retrospective generic family association. It is a contemporary statement made immediately after Huizinga's American encounter with Malinowski, identifying Malinowski as someone he expected to discuss further with **Leonhard in particular**.

It does **not** prove that Leonhard later received any of Malinowski's books. It does make Leonhard a higher-priority candidate when applying the family's reported `according to interest` division rule.

Source:
- Johan Huizinga, *Briefwisseling*, vol. II, letter 648, Boston, 19 May 1926: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b9/Briefwisseling%2C_Deel_II_1925-1933%2C_DigUBL.pdf

## 3. Leonhard's later biography makes the branch materially plausible, but remains circumstantial

Leonhard Huizinga (1906–1980) studied law, travelled through **Asia and Africa**, subsequently settled on **Java as a planter**, and later became a journalist and writer. This biographical profile is consistent with a possible interest in books on ethnography, colonial societies or travel, but it should not be used as provenance evidence by itself.

Source:
- G.J. van Bork, DBNL biographical entry on Leonhard Huizinga: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/bork001schr01_01/bork001schr01_01_0518.php

## 4. The Leonhard descendant branch demonstrably retained Johan Huizinga's books

A much stronger object-level clue comes from Anton van der Lem's work on Huizinga's reading. In his discussion of Huizinga's copy of Robert Fruin's *Verspreide Geschriften*, Van der Lem states that the set was then in the possession of Huizinga's **oldest granddaughter, Mrs F.M. Scholten-Huizinga at Sterksel**, who allowed him to inspect it. Van der Lem records material details: Huizinga's set was bound in blue cloth and he marked articles he had read in the tables of contents.

Leiden UBL's recovered-books list subsequently records two books/sets donated by **F.M. Scholten-Huizinga**:

- **HUIZIN 2032** — Henri Pirenne, *Histoire de l'Europe des invasions au XVIe siècle* (Paris, 1936), with presentation inscription from Jenny Henri and Jacques Pirenne; gift F.M. Scholten-Huizinga.
- **HUIZIN 2039** — Robert Fruin, *Verspreide Geschriften*, 11 vols. (The Hague, 1900–1905); gift F.M. Scholten-Huizinga.

Thus this is not merely a genealogical hypothesis. A descendant branch connected to Leonhard demonstrably held and later returned identifiable volumes from Johan Huizinga's working library.

Sources:
- Anton van der Lem, `Het Eeuwige verbeeld in een afgehaald bed`, DBNL, note 51: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/lem_002eeuw01_01/lem_002eeuw01_01_0004.php
- UBL/Huizinga Online, recovered HUIZIN list: https://huizinga-online.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Lijst-van-teruggevonden-boeken.pdf

## 5. Name/initials anomaly: F.M. versus S.M. Scholten-Huizinga

A 1994 *Biografie Bulletin* piece, `Grootvader Huizinga: Herinneringen van een kleindochter`, is printed and catalogued by DBNL under **S.M. Scholten-Huizinga**. The speaker calls herself the **oldest grandchild** and explicitly writes that **Leonhard was her father**. The speech was delivered on 21 October 1993 at the presentation of Anton van der Lem's Huizinga biography.

This conflicts with Van der Lem's identification of the oldest granddaughter in Sterksel as **F.M. Scholten-Huizinga**, and with UBL's HUIZIN donor entries under F.M. Scholten-Huizinga.

The present note does **not** silently normalise the initials. The discrepancy may be a publication/metadata error, an alternate name form, or evidence that two people are being conflated. Until a first-rate genealogical or archival record resolves it, search both forms:

- `F.M. Scholten-Huizinga`
- `S.M. Scholten-Huizinga`
- `Fransje Huizinga`
- `Scholten-Huizinga, Sterksel`

Source:
- `Grootvader Huizinga`, *Biografie Bulletin* 4 (1994), DBNL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_bio001199401_01/_bio001199401_01_0006.php

## 6. Leonhard demonstrably retained family papers as well

The editors' introduction to Huizinga's *Briefwisseling* notes that, in 1963, **Leonhard Huizinga used a number of letters in his possession** when writing *Herinneringen aan mijn vader*. This is documentary evidence that material from Johan Huizinga's personal archive remained in Leonhard's hands well after 1945.

Again, papers are not books. The point is narrower: the Leonhard branch is independently demonstrable as a custodian of material from Johan Huizinga's estate, making a targeted search of that branch archival rather than speculative.

Source:
- Johan Huizinga, *Briefwisseling*, editorial introduction: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8e/Briefwisseling%2C_Deel_I_1894-1924%2C_DigUBL.pdf

## 7. Family retention was genuinely multi-branch

The recovered HUIZIN list documents gifts from several family nodes, including:

- Laura Conley-Huizinga;
- Jacoba de Jonge-de Meijere;
- Elisabeth Huizinga;
- F.M. Scholten-Huizinga.

This supports the literal reading of Huizinga Online's dispersal statement: the library was not transferred wholesale to one dealer and later reconstructed from one trade stream. It fragmented across family branches and the antiquarian market.

For the Malinowski copies, a **batch-reconstruction model** is therefore preferable to a single-chain model.

Source:
- UBL/Huizinga Online, recovered HUIZIN list: https://huizinga-online.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Lijst-van-teruggevonden-boeken.pdf

## 8. Immediate family-branch search protocol

1. Search UBL acquisition/donor documentation for **F.M./S.M. Scholten-Huizinga**, `Fransje`, `Sterksel`, HUIZIN 2032 and HUIZIN 2039.
2. Determine whether the Pirenne and Fruin donations arrived together, and whether their accession file names additional Huizinga books retained by the same donor.
3. Reconstruct the chain by which the Fruin set moved from Johan Huizinga to the granddaughter: direct bequest, transfer through Leonhard, or another family intermediary.
4. Search Leonhard Huizinga papers/estate material for book lists, inheritance correspondence, household inventories and references to his father's library.
5. Search Leonhard's *Herinneringen aan mijn vader* and other autobiographical writing for `Malinowski`, `boeken`, `bibliotheek`, the 1926 American trip, and descriptions of post-1945 family division. The currently indexed DBNL title page does not expose searchable full text, so absence in ordinary web search is not a negative result.
6. If a Leonhard/Scholten-Huizinga donor file is located, test the three titles directly: *Crime and Custom in Savage Society*, *Sex and Repression in Savage Society*, *Argonauts of the Western Pacific*.
7. Keep parallel searches for the other children because the 1926 Leonhard clue raises probability but does not exclude another family branch.

## 9. Archive-porosity correction: Nationaal Archief inv. 836 cannot be treated as the complete Saint Lucas record universe

Nationaal Archief 2.21.271 documents that the Vosmaer family archive remained in the Rapenburg house long after the death of Mrs Vosmaer-Röell in 1970. In autumn 1985 the family sought advice on a destination; inventorying began in May 1986. The archive was taken into long-term management rather than transferred outright in ownership.

Heijbroek's Saint Lucas/Parool research provides a material warning about completeness. During the clearing of Rapenburg 83 after C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's death, a substantial group of wartime *Het Parool* sheets was found behind a bookcase; his later account describes family retention and rediscovery of this material years later. He also states that his reconstruction drew on conversations with the Vosmaer family and documents from their archive.

Therefore:

- **inv. 836 remains the highest-priority public archival file for Saint Lucas**;
- its small extent (`1 omslag`) must not be read as proof that all surviving Saint Lucas business/circular/catalogue material is in that file;
- family-retained, house-clearance and formally accessioned archival materials followed partly separate paths.

Sources:
- Nationaal Archief, Collectie 548 Vosmaer, 2.21.271: https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/onderzoeken/archief/2.21.271
- J.F. Heijbroek, `De Saint Lucas Society`, *De Boekenwereld* 34.4 (2018): https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 10. Two collateral cautions

### De Eenhoorn
Nationaal Archief inv. **833** concerns C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's partnership with Adrianus Leonardus Welling in the `Industrie- en Handelonderneming De Eenhoorn`, 1943–1955. Its dates overlap the Huizinga/Saint Lucas disposal window, but no evidence currently identifies De Eenhoorn with Saint Lucas. Treat inv. 833 as a **secondary legal/business-context file**, not as a Saint Lucas synonym.

### Rapiarijs chronology
Heijbroek's article/captions produce an unresolved dating tension: *Rapiarijs* is described as appearing from April 1946 to autumn 1947, yet the article later refers to an **unnumbered issue 'from 1948'**. Do not regularise this chronology from secondary wording. The original newsletter/copy needs inspection; the discrepancy may reflect an approximate date, an unnumbered late issue, or a caption/text inconsistency.

## Running assessment

The new family evidence changes the search topology. The highest-value question is no longer only whether Catalogue 3 contained one of the Malinowski books. It is now a fork test:

1. **Vosmaer/Saint Lucas test** — did any of the three copies enter the dealer's postwar stock?
2. **Leonhard/descendant test** — did the books remain in the family branch to which Huizinga had already linked Malinowski conversationally in 1926, and which demonstrably retained Huizinga's books and papers?

The second branch is still inferential as to the three Malinowski volumes, but it is now supported by enough independent evidence to merit the same archival discipline as the dealer route.
