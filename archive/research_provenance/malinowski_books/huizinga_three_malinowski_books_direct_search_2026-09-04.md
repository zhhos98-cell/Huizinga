# Direct search for Huizinga's three Malinowski books — 2026-09-04

_Status: target-only provenance search. Scope is restricted to locating the three physical books Huizinga received from Bronislaw Malinowski by 12 December 1926._

## Target objects

1. Bronislaw Malinowski, *Crime and Custom in Savage Society* (London, Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner & Co., 1926).
2. Bronislaw Malinowski, *Sex and Repression in Savage Society* (London, Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner & Co., title-page date 1927; already in Huizinga's possession by 12 Dec. 1926).
3. Bronislaw Malinowski, *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (London, George Routledge & Sons, 1922).

All broader Huizinga-library, Saint Lucas, family, KVB/KB/UBL and Vosmaer work is useful only insofar as it helps identify these three copies.

## 1. Current direct-search result

Exact-title searches combining each target with `Huizinga`, `Johan Huizinga`, `Vosmaer`, `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`, `Rapenburg 83`, `Saint Lucas`, `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`, `ex libris`, `provenance`, `signed`, `inscribed`, and `presentation copy` have not yet produced a surviving copy explicitly attributed to Huizinga.

This is a **metadata negative**, not a provenance negative. Current antiquarian listings frequently omit names, bookplates and inscriptions unless the seller considers them commercially important.

A particularly relevant warning comes from Dutch marketplace Boekwinkeltjes: seller boilerplate explicitly notes that minor features such as `een ingeplakte ex-libris of een naam op het schutblad` are not always mentioned. Therefore seller-description text alone cannot exclude a Huizinga/Vosmaer copy.

## 2. Presentation-copy fingerprint: Malinowski used personalised inscriptions

A first-edition copy of *Crime and Custom in Savage Society* presented by Malinowski to George Pitt-Rivers survives on the antiquarian market. The inscription is not a generic signature but a personalised multi-line dedication referring to the book's argument and `reciprocal friendship`.

This materially changes the object-search protocol. Huizinga's three books were sent directly by Malinowski in the context of an active scholarly friendship, so front matter must be inspected for a possible autograph presentation inscription even when seller metadata says nothing about Huizinga.

Reference example:
- Bernard Quaritch catalogue, item 58, first edition *Crime and Custom*, inscribed by Malinowski to George Pitt-Rivers: https://www.quaritch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Boston-2019-catalogue-final.pdf
- AbeBooks listing of the same/related Pitt-Rivers copy: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/book-search/title/crime-custom-savage-society/author/malinowski-bronislaw/first-edition/

### Inspection priority inside each physical book

1. front pastedown;
2. free front endpaper;
3. half-title;
4. title page;
5. verso of title page;
6. rear pastedown/endpaper for inventory marks;
7. top-right area of title page for the small ink-number pattern seen in C.J.J.G. Vosmaer books.

## 3. Vosmaer fingerprint remains secondary, but image inspection must use all variants

C.J.J.G. Vosmaer provenance can appear through several bookplate forms, not only the armorial/unicorn design. Institutional collections document at least:

- initials/monogram type;
- a 1930 Dutch `image of carving` type;
- Dutch armorial type.

Sources:
- Kent State, Ione K. Wiechel Bookplate Collection: https://www.library.kent.edu/ione-k-wiechel-bookplate-collection
- Library of Congress, Ruthven Deane Collection, no. 9722: https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/31/resources/6521
- University of Debrecen, C.J.J.G. Vosmaer ex libris: http://hdl.handle.net/2437/84971

A Vosmaer plate alone is not proof of Huizinga provenance. The target identification standard is **Vosmaer signal + independent Huizinga/Malinowski signal**, or a direct Huizinga inscription/presentation provenance.

## 4. Book-by-book search state

### A. *Crime and Custom in Savage Society* (1926)

This is currently the strongest title for establishing Malinowski's presentation-copy habits because a contemporary personalised presentation copy is publicly documented. First editions are identifiable as Kegan Paul, London, 1926, xii + 132 pp. with photographic plates and publisher's advertisements.

Current exact-title web and Dutch-market searches have not surfaced a Huizinga/Vosmaer copy.

Search next:
- all first-edition seller listings with images, including sold/archived listings;
- title/half-title images for presentation inscriptions;
- Netherlands/Belgium dealer stock where provenance may be underdescribed;
- institutional copy-specific MARC/local notes for owner names and bookplates.

### B. *Sex and Repression in Savage Society* (1927 imprint; received before 12 Dec. 1926)

This remains the **highest-diagnostic target** because Huizinga demonstrably possessed it before the printed calendar year on the title page. A surviving copy may therefore be an early presentation/distribution copy with unusually strong provenance evidence.

Current first-edition market listings include London Kegan Paul copies and first American Harcourt Brace copies, but none inspected in indexed text carries Huizinga/Vosmaer provenance. One current American first-edition listing notes merely `Name and date inside front cover`; this is not a Huizinga lead unless the name/date can be visually checked, and the American issue is less likely than the London presentation context without further evidence.

Current Dutch/Belgian first-edition stock includes a 1927 London copy offered by Antiquariaat Tanchelmus and another by Joseph Burridge Books, but indexed descriptions contain no provenance detail.

Search next:
- prioritise **London 1927 Kegan Paul** copies;
- request/inspect front endpaper, half-title and title-page images;
- explicitly search for copies described as advance, review, author's presentation, pre-publication or with 1926 inscriptions/dates;
- record any copy with a pre-1927 ownership date even if the owner is not Huizinga, since it helps define distribution chronology.

### C. *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (1922)

The target edition is the original London George Routledge & Sons issue (with New York E.P. Dutton imprint also present in many records). Because the book was already four years old when Malinowski sent it to Huizinga, a presentation inscription dated 1926 would be especially diagnostic.

Current first-edition searches have not surfaced a Huizinga/Vosmaer copy. General marketplace results are dominated by later impressions and reprints, so the search must strictly filter to 1922.

Search next:
- 1922 copies only;
- inspect copies with owner stamps/bookplates rather than excluding them;
- prioritise British/continental provenance and sold association copies;
- check whether any 1922 copies carry inscriptions made by Malinowski in 1926.

## 5. Direct-target search protocol from this point forward

For each title, maintain a copy-level candidate table with:

- seller/institution;
- edition/imprint/year;
- binding/state;
- visible ownership inscription;
- visible Malinowski inscription/signature;
- bookplate type;
- ink/inventory number;
- seller inventory number;
- provenance wording;
- image availability;
- current/sold status;
- confidence rank.

Candidate ranking:

**A — direct hit**: Huizinga name/inscription, Malinowski-to-Huizinga presentation inscription, or documented Huizinga provenance.

**B — strong candidate**: first-edition target copy with C.J.J.G. Vosmaer plate/number plus another independent Huizinga-family/Saint Lucas clue.

**C — inspect**: first-edition target copy in Netherlands/Belgium/UK with unexplained ex libris, owner name, old inventory number or seller images that expose front matter.

**D — background only**: ordinary clean copy with no provenance clues.

## 6. Immediate priority order

1. **Sex and Repression (London 1927)** — highest diagnostic value because Huizinga had it by 12 Dec. 1926.
2. **Crime and Custom (London 1926)** — best presentation-inscription control; broad first-edition survival.
3. **Argonauts (London 1922)** — look especially for 1926 presentation inscriptions on an older edition.

Only after copy-level searches stall should Saint Lucas Catalogue 3, UBL BA2, KB/KVB dealer-catalogue archives, Leonhard/Fransje family material or Vosmaer business records be used, and then only to test these exact three titles.
