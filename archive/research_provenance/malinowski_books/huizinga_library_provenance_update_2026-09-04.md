# Huizinga library provenance update — 2026-09-04

_Status: external provenance research delta. This note supplements `research/huizinga_library_malinowski_provenance.md` without altering the frozen OCR/review corpus._

## 1. Search-window refinement: 1946–1948 is now the operational bottleneck

The postwar disposal of Johan Huizinga's books still cannot be assigned a precise sale date. The evidence does, however, support a narrower **search window**:

- Johan Huizinga died on 1 February 1945.
- Anton van der Lem reports that Auguste Huizinga-Schölvinck and Laura returned to Leiden in **November 1946** and that the books had probably been stored in the meantime; he explicitly says that the moment at which the books were divided has not yet been traced.
- Nationaal Archief 2.21.271 **inv. 836**, the file specifically concerning C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's work as director of the Saint Lucas Society, is dated **1947 and undated**.
- J.F. Heijbroek places the appearance of Vosmaer's *Bibliopolis* around **1948**, and its notice for Saint Lucas **Catalogue 3, _Wetenschappelijke adversaria_**, refers to several books with letters/dedications of the late Prof. Huizinga.

Accordingly, **1946–1948 should be used as a focused archival search window, not asserted as the date of sale**.

## 2. New institutional route: Leiden University Library as a Saint Lucas customer

Heijbroek states that **one of the Saint Lucas Society's most important customers was the library of Leiden University**. The article describes Vosmaer performing work for the university library including microfilming books and autographs, reproducing images, stencilling lecture notes and publishing dissertations.

This materially changes the Catalogue 3 strategy. The Leiden University Library should not be treated only as the present-day institution reconstructing Huizinga's library under HUIZIN shelfmarks. It was also a **contemporary institutional customer of the very antiquarian business that absorbed most of Huizinga's books**.

### Operational implication

Search UBL institutional records for roughly **1946–1952**, with a first emphasis on 1947–1949, for:

- `Saint Lucas Society` / `St. Lucas Society`;
- `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer` / `Vosmaer`;
- `Rapenburg 83`;
- `Bibliopolis`;
- `Catalogue 3` / `Catalogus 3` / `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`;
- invoices, acquisition registers, antiquarian correspondence, circulars, received catalogues, supplier files and director's correspondence.

A.H.M.C. Kessen became university librarian in 1947, so surviving director-level correspondence for Kessen's first years is a plausible named target. This is a search lead only; no Kessen–Vosmaer correspondence has yet been identified online.

Source:
- Heijbroek 2018, esp. the section describing Leiden University Library as a major Saint Lucas customer: https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 3. Second dispersal branch: the unidentified Koninklijke Bibliotheek gift of 1965

Anton van der Lem records an important later transfer: when **Auguste Huizinga-Schölvinck remarried in 1965**, she donated **about fifty books** from Huizinga's library to the Koninklijke Bibliotheek. Van der Lem adds that the KB unfortunately did not record which books were involved.

This opens a separate provenance branch from the Vosmaer/Saint Lucas route. A title search in the KB catalogue is structurally insufficient because the relevant contemporary record may have been a batch accession without item-level Huizinga provenance.

### Operational implication

Prioritise KB archival/administrative records for **1965**:

- accession or acquisition registers;
- gift/donation registers;
- donor files under `Auguste Huizinga-Schölvinck`, `A. Huizinga-Schölvinck`, `Huizinga`, and possible remarried-name variants;
- batch accession numbers and internal shelfmark conversion records;
- correspondence connected with the donation.

A collateral source survives at Leiden's Academisch Historisch Museum: `Verslag van het gesprek met Mevr. A. Huizinga-Schölvinck`, dated **5 June 1965**. It is close in time to the gift and may help reconstruct household/dispersal chronology if it discusses the library, but its contents must be inspected before any such claim is made. Van der Lem also cautions that her recollections require source criticism.

Source:
- Anton van der Lem, `Een bibliotheek als wederopstanding`: https://huizinga-online.nl/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Een-bibliotheek-als-wederopstanding.pdf

## 4. Material-provenance correction: C.J.J.G. Vosmaer used/collected multiple ex-libris types

The previous object-fingerprint protocol over-weighted a single heraldic/armorial C.J.J.G. Vosmaer bookplate. Institutional bookplate collections show that the fingerprint is more heterogeneous.

The **Ione K. Wiechel Bookplate Collection** at Kent State lists under C.J.J.G. Vosmaer three distinct records/descriptions:

1. `[Initials]`
2. `[Dutch, 1930. Image of carving.]`
3. `[Dutch armorial.]`

The Library of Congress Ruthven Deane Collection separately preserves an armorial `Ex Libris C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`, call number **PR 13 CN 1935:144, no. 9722**; its indexed imagery includes unicorns and coats of arms. The University of Debrecen also preserves a C.J.J.G. Vosmaer ex-libris image under persistent handle **2437/84971**.

Most importantly, Johan Schwencke's 1928 list of Dutch ex-libris collectors already includes **`C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, Rapenburg 83, Leiden`**. A 1930 continuation again lists him at the same address. This establishes that Vosmaer was actively participating in ex-libris collecting/exchange long before Huizinga's postwar dispersal.

### Consequence for the Malinowski search

A C.J.J.G. Vosmaer bookplate is therefore an **object-level lead, not a unique Huizinga tracer**, and visual searches must allow for multiple plate designs. The working fingerprint should now be:

- any of the known Vosmaer ex-libris variants: initials/monogram, 1930 carving-image type, armorial/heraldic type;
- known placement inside front board/wrapper/pastedown where seller images permit inspection;
- small ink inventory number in the upper-right area of the title page, where present;
- `Rapenburg 83`, `Saint Lucas`, or other Vosmaer provenance language in catalogue descriptions.

For a Huizinga attribution, seek a **second independent provenance signal**: Huizinga inscription/dedication/letter, a Saint Lucas catalogue entry, a recoverable stock/inventory number, family documentation, institutional accession record, or another secure chain-of-custody marker.

Sources:
- Kent State, Ione K. Wiechel Bookplate Collection: https://www.library.kent.edu/ione-k-wiechel-bookplate-collection
- Library of Congress, Ruthven Deane Collection of Bookplates: https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/31/resources/6521
- University of Debrecen, `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer ex librise`: http://hdl.handle.net/2437/84971
- Johan Schwencke, `Het Nederlandsche Ex-Libris`, *Den Gulden Winckel* 27 (1928): https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_gul001192801_01/_gul001192801_01_0049.php

## 5. NBI DS 5647: exact request path recovered

Open Archieven resolves the NBI entry for **C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, Leiden** as original dossier identification **DS 5647** and supplies the practical request instruction omitted from the high-level name index:

> `Onderdeel van een omslag met meerdere dossiers. Deze omslag is aan te vragen via archiefinventaris 2.09.16.01, inventarisnummer 3219, ten name van Amsterdam (Technisch Bureau).`

Thus, if this collateral branch is pursued, the request target is **Nationaal Archief 2.09.16.01, inv. 3219, under Amsterdam (Technisch Bureau)**, with DS 5647 identified as the internal dossier. The Open Archieven record describes the registration period as 1945–1967.

The dossier's existence must remain evidentially neutral. It is relevant only if inspection reveals postwar property, business or stock information bearing on C.J.J.G. Vosmaer/Saint Lucas.

Source:
- Open Archieven, registration C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, DS 5647: https://www.openarchieven.nl/ghn%3A5f312b0a-fc2e-447e-e053-09f0900afd8f

## 6. Negative-result log: title-only web searching is saturating

On **2026-09-04**, indexed-web searches combining the three target Malinowski editions with `Vosmaer`, `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`, `ex libris`, `Rapenburg 83`, and related provenance terms did **not** produce a direct match to a surviving Huizinga/Vosmaer copy.

This is not evidence that the copies are absent from institutional or private holdings. It is operational evidence that the next gain is more likely to come from:

1. archival acquisition/business records;
2. scanned catalogue images not fully text-indexed;
3. provenance images in antiquarian/auction listings;
4. presentation-copy inscriptions or stock numbers;
5. institutional bookplate and donor files.

Exact-title Google-style searching should therefore move below Catalogue 3 / UBL / KB / archival work in priority.

## 8. Current evidential map

The secure early chain remains:

`Malinowski -> Huizinga (three books received by 12 Dec. 1926)`

The postwar chain remains unresolved, but it now has **two institutionally distinct routes**:

- `Huizinga household -> C.J.J.G. Vosmaer / Saint Lucas -> unknown buyers or retained stock`
- `Huizinga household -> Auguste Huizinga-Schölvinck -> Koninklijke Bibliotheek (c. 50 books, 1965; titles unrecorded in the source consulted)`

The immediate research problem is therefore no longer simply 'where did Huizinga's library go?' It is to reconstruct **separate disposal batches**, identify the documentary systems that recorded each batch, and then test the three Malinowski presentation copies against those systems.
