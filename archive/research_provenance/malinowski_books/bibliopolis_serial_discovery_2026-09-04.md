# *Bibliopolis* (C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, 1948–1952): serial-discovery route — 2026-09-04

_Status: active bibliographic discovery note. The immediate purpose is to locate surviving issues of the periodical in which Saint Lucas Catalogue 3, `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`, was advertised._

## 1. Why the mother periodical has become a primary target

J.F. Heijbroek states that around 1948 C.J.J.G. Vosmaer founded/distributed *Bibliopolis*. Its first subtitle was **`international booktrade magazine`**, later changed to **`journal devoted to the historical, technical and economic aspects of books and the booktrade`**. The periodical appeared between **1948 and 1952** and used **Wyttenbachweg 43, Oegstgeest** as correspondence address.

Heijbroek describes it chiefly as a small `vraag en aanbod` publication, with occasional articles, in memoriam pieces, notices of antiquarian catalogues from the Netherlands and abroad, and the passing reference to Saint Lucas **Catalogue 3, `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`**:

> `An international collection with many important works. We note several books with letters and dedications of the late Prof. Huizinga`.

Thus locating the *Bibliopolis* issue containing this notice could supply a **specific issue/date, surrounding advertisements, order instructions, address, and possibly series context** even if Catalogue 3 itself remains missing.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, `De Saint Lucas Society`, *De Boekenwereld* 34.4 (2018): https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 2. Public-index search result: the serial itself is unusually bibliographically faint

A broad indexed-web pass on 2026-09-04 searched:

- exact title + both subtitles;
- `Bibliopolis` + `Vosmaer`;
- `Bibliopolis` + `Oegstgeest`;
- `Bibliopolis` + `Wyttenbachweg 43`;
- title + individual years 1948, 1949, 1950, 1951, 1952;
- title restricted to WorldCat, KB, UvA, Leiden and Allard Pierson domains;
- exact wording from the Catalogue 3 advertisement.

No independent serial holding or issue record surfaced. Search results overwhelmingly collapse into:

1. Heijbroek's 2018 article;
2. the unrelated modern KB database formerly called `Bibliopolis`;
3. modern businesses/publishers named Bibliopolis.

This is **not evidence that no institutional copy survives**. It is operational evidence that Vosmaer's *Bibliopolis* may have been catalogued as book-trade ephemera, dealer literature, or a grouped catalogue series rather than as a standard periodical title.

## 3. Implication: search collection systems, not ordinary serial OPAC indexing

The next discovery sequence should privilege:

1. **KVB / Allard Pierson book-trade catalogue collections**;
2. **KB `Verzameling Catalogi` / Veiling- en boekhandelscatalogi**;
3. **UBL BA2 institutional archive**, especially received dealer catalogues/circulars and Saint Lucas supplier correspondence;
4. **Erfgoed Leiden en Omstreken visual/ephemera holdings**;
5. Vosmaer family papers / objects used by Heijbroek;
6. international union catalogues under subtitle/issuer/address rather than title alone.

When querying staff, ask for both the serial and **unbound single issues/circulars from C.J.J.G. Vosmaer / Saint Lucas / Wyttenbachweg 43**.

## 4. Jan Post: a potentially useful internal dating marker

Heijbroek says *Bibliopolis* contained a notable **in memoriam for the Amsterdam antiquarian Post**. Independent book-trade memoir literature identifies a J. Post antiquarian shop at **Nieuwe Spiegelstraat 59, Amsterdam**, founded in 1937, whose catalogues increasingly emphasized old books, typography and bibliography. This is very likely the Post intended by Heijbroek, and if securely identified the obituary could provide an internal chronological marker for an early *Bibliopolis* issue.

Source:
- H. de la Fontaine Verwey, `Herinneringen van een bibliothecaris 5: Nieuwe Spiegelstraat 59`, *De Boekenwereld* 2 (1985–86): https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_boe022198501_01/_boe022198501_01_0049.php

## 5. Critical caution: modern secondary sources conflict on Jan Post's identity/dates

A later bookseller-history compilation calls the antiquarian **Jan Post (1889–1948)** and associates him with Nieuwe Spiegelstraat 59. On the same page, however, captions around an obituary/grave introduce inconsistent biographical data, including a `1907–1948` label and a grave record for a differently initialized Post. A separate genealogy compilation gives a Jan Post born 1889 who died in 1947, without adequate evidence in the present search to prove that this is the antiquarian.

Therefore:

- `Post = Nieuwe Spiegelstraat 59 antiquarian` is a strong working identification;
- the **exact death date is not yet secure**;
- the *Bibliopolis* in memoriam should not yet be used to assign an exact founding month/issue.

Sources for the discrepancy:
- Librariana, page on Anton Pieck / antiquariaat Jan Post: https://ilibrariana.wordpress.com/2012/04/27/anton-pieck-als-tekenaar-van-heemsteedse-taferelen/
- Genealogie Online, Jan Post 1889–1947 (identity not established): https://www.genealogieonline.nl/genealogie-post-oost/I155041.php

## 6. Better way to resolve the Post locator

Prioritise primary/contemporary evidence:

1. search Amsterdam civil death records for the proprietor of J. Post, Nieuwe Spiegelstraat 59;
2. retrieve the **Het Parool obituary/death notice** reproduced or referenced in later book-trade pages;
3. inspect Chamber of Commerce/business registration for Antiquariaat J. Post around 1947–49;
4. compare the widow/son named in A.L. van Gendt's recollection of Post's 1944 arrest;
5. once identity and death date are secure, search *Bibliopolis* issues after that date for the in memoriam.

A.L. van Gendt recalls that when Post was arrested by the Germans he encountered **Mrs Post**, who said she had to care for her son and could not run the shop alone. This family information may help disambiguate the civil record.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek & F.W. Kuyper, `In gesprek met A.L. van Gendt`, *De Boekenwereld* 2 (1985–86): https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_boe022198501_01/_boe022198501_01_0050.php

## 8. Current evidential map

### Secure
- Vosmaer distributed *Bibliopolis* roughly 1948–1952.
- it used Wyttenbachweg 43, Oegstgeest as correspondence address;
- it changed subtitle during its run;
- it mentioned Catalogue 3 / `Wetenschappelijke adversaria` and Huizinga-associated books;
- it contained an in memoriam for an Amsterdam antiquarian Post.

### Strong but pending direct identity proof
- Heijbroek's Post is J. / Jan Post of Nieuwe Spiegelstraat 59.

### Unresolved
- exact first issue/date;
- volume/issue numbering pattern;
- surviving institutional holding;
- exact issue containing Catalogue 3 notice;
- exact issue containing Post in memoriam;
- Jan Post's correct civil identity/death date among contradictory later sources.

## Running conclusion

The failure to retrieve *Bibliopolis* through ordinary title searching is itself methodologically useful: Catalogue 3 may be hidden inside a **dealer-ephemera documentary ecology** rather than a conventional serial catalogue. The shortest path is now to locate a *Bibliopolis* issue through book-trade collections or a contemporary internal marker, then use the issue's own bibliographic apparatus to date and reconstruct Catalogue 3.
