# KVB volume 6 register-search correction — 2026-09-04

_Status: method correction to `kvb_catalogue_vol6_1940_1949_control_2026-09-04.md`._

## Core correction

The alphabetical register of Emma Dronckers's 1949 sixth supplement should **not** be treated as a comprehensive business/issuer index.

A contemporary 1940 review of Dronckers's fifth volume explicitly complained that its register was **restricted to authors' names (`schrijversnamen`)**. Dronckers's 1949 preface to volume 6 then states that its new alphabetical register is a **continuation of the register with which she closed Catalogue V**, carrying the library's alphabetisation through 1 January 1949.

Sources:
- Review of Catalogue V, `Nieuwsblad voor den Boekhandel`, 1940, noting that the register was limited to authors' names: https://dbc.rug.nl/digital/collection/tijdschrift/id/36/
- Emma Dronckers, preface to Catalogue VI: https://brill.com/display/book/9789004542662/front-2.pdf

## Consequence for Saint Lucas / Bibliopolis

A failure to find:

- `Saint Lucas Society`
- `C.J.J.G. Vosmaer`
- `Bibliopolis`
- `Rapiarijs`

in the alphabetical register **would not be a meaningful negative result** if those names function only as dealers, issuers or corporate provenance.

The correct search priority inside volume 6 is therefore:

1. **`V. De Verspreiding van Het Boek. Boekhandel.` pp. 281–302**;
2. the continued classification for **antiquarian and stock catalogues (`Antiquariaats- en magazijncatalogi`)** inherited from the earlier catalogue architecture;
3. only secondarily the alphabetical register, useful if an item was catalogued under a personal author/editor name.

Brill's electronic table of contents confirms the book-trade chapter at pp. 281–302.

Source:
- https://brill.com/display/book/9789004542662/B9789004542662_s007.pdf

## Operational implication

The next manual/institutional lookup request should not be phrased merely as `please check the index for Vosmaer`. It should ask staff to inspect the **book-trade / antiquarian-catalogue section for 1940–49 acquisitions**, including issuer headings and grouped dealer catalogues.

This distinction matters because Catalogue 3 may have been accessioned as an anonymous or minimally titled dealer catalogue under Saint Lucas, while the printed author index would omit the firm entirely.
