# C.J.J.G. Vosmaer estate handling after 1986: Rapenburg 83, Heijbroek, and Rijksmuseum

_Status: active estate-routing lead, synced 2026-09-03._

This note distinguishes the post-1986 handling of **C.J.J.G. Vosmaer (1907–1986)** and the contents of Rapenburg 83 from the separate nineteenth-century provenance of the inherited *Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana* associated with Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888).

## 1. J.F. Heijbroek personally participated in clearing Rapenburg 83

J.F. Heijbroek's 2018 article on the Saint Lucas Society contains a first-person statement about the period immediately after C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's death. He writes that, **while clearing the building at Rapenburg 83 (`Bij het ontruimen van het pand Rapenburg 83 (door de auteur)`) after C.J.J.G.'s death in 1986**, a large mass of paper was found behind a bookcase in the monumental library. It proved to be an incomplete remainder of the stencilled Leiden underground edition of *Het Parool*.

Heijbroek then consulted C.J.J.G.'s widow, D.M. Vosmaer-Hudig, who explained the wartime production and distribution context.

This is important for the Huizinga-library search because Heijbroek was not merely reconstructing Saint Lucas from published sources decades later: he had **direct physical access to the Rapenburg 83 library during the post-death clearance**.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, `De Saint Lucas Society`, *De Boekenwereld* 34.4 (2018), Google-indexed text from the Leiden PDF: https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 2. Heijbroek's Rijksprentenkabinet role makes the clearance institutionally relevant

Contemporary Rijksmuseum/Rijksprentenkabinet reporting shows that by 1988–1989 **drs. J.F. Heijbroek was working intensively as organizer, writer and final editor of the exhibition/catalogue on the collection of Mr. Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888)**. Rijksmuseum object records also cite his 1989 catalogue, *De verzameling van mr. Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888)*, for provenance of objects subsequently acquired from C.J.J.G.'s heirs.

This does not prove that Heijbroek cleared Rapenburg 83 in an official Rijksmuseum capacity, but it makes the overlap significant: the person who physically participated in the 1986 house/library clearance soon became the key Rijksprentenkabinet researcher/curator of the Vosmaer family collection that was transferred to the museum.

Sources:
- Rijksmuseum annual-report material for 1988, noting Heijbroek's work on the Vosmaer exhibition/catalogue.
- Rijksmuseum collection records citing J.F. Heijbroek (ed.), *De verzameling van mr. Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888)* (1989).

## 3. C.J.J.G.'s heirs transferred substantial material to Rijksmuseum in 1989

Rijksmuseum provenance records document a major 1989 purchase from **C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's heirs**, including more than two hundred drawings inherited through the older Vosmaer family line, for fl. 650,000. Separate Rijksmuseum records show numerous photographs acquired in 1989 with the credit line:

`Schenking van de erven van de heer C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, Leiden.`

The 1989 institutional annual report also states that **letters were acquired from the heirs of Mr. C.J.J.G. Vosmaer in Leiden** (`brieven verworven van de erven van de heer C.J.J.G. Vosmaer te Leiden`).

Examples / sources:
- Rijksmuseum, RP-T-1989 provenance records, e.g. https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200143930
- Rijksmuseum photographic holdings with 1989 heir-donation credit lines, e.g. https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200146536
- Rijksmuseum / Ministry annual-report material for 1989, Google-indexed text containing the phrase `brieven verworven van de erven van de heer C.J.J.G. Vosmaer te Leiden`.

## 4. What this establishes—and what it does not

Confirmed:

- Rapenburg 83 was physically cleared after C.J.J.G.'s death in 1986.
- J.F. Heijbroek personally participated in that clearance.
- C.J.J.G.'s widow D.M. Vosmaer-Hudig was available as an informant about material found there.
- Rijksmuseum/Rijksprentenkabinet later acquired substantial groups of drawings, photographs and letters from C.J.J.G.'s heirs in 1989.
- Heijbroek was directly involved in the Rijksprentenkabinet's research/presentation of the Vosmaer family collection at precisely this time.

Not yet established:

- that Heijbroek handled or inventoried C.J.J.G.'s personal/business books during the clearance;
- that Huizinga-derived books were offered to or acquired by Rijksmuseum;
- that the three Malinowski books remained at Rapenburg 83 until 1986;
- that any book disposal followed the same route as the drawings, photographs or letters.

The distinction is essential: much of the material demonstrably transferred to Rijksmuseum belonged to the older family collection of Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888) and merely passed through C.J.J.G. as descendant/custodian.

## 5. New archival questions

The estate-handling problem can now be framed concretely:

1. Did Heijbroek or Rijksprentenkabinet staff prepare a **clearance list, selection list, acquisition file, correspondence file or estate inventory** for Rapenburg 83 in 1986–1989?
2. Do Rijksmuseum acquisition files for the 1986 and 1989 Vosmaer transfers record material **declined, left behind, sent elsewhere, or classified as books**?
3. Does correspondence with **D.M. Vosmaer-Hudig** or other heirs mention disposal of C.J.J.G.'s personal library / Saint Lucas stock?
4. Was the 1989 group of `brieven` catalogued at item level, and did it include twentieth-century C.J.J.G./Saint Lucas correspondence as well as older Carel Vosmaer material?
5. Is there a record of the eventual destination of the **bookcases/books** present in the monumental library at Rapenburg 83 during the clearance?

## 6. Relevance to the Huizinga–Malinowski provenance chase

This is now a separate Tier-2 route alongside the direct Saint Lucas archival route. The highest-value source remains Nationaal Archief 2.21.271 inv. 836 (C.J.J.G.'s Saint Lucas Society file, 1947+n.d.). But if the Malinowski books survived in C.J.J.G.'s possession until his death, the **1986 Rapenburg 83 clearance and subsequent heir correspondence** become the crucial next transition point.

The key evidential target is therefore not the Rijksmuseum art objects themselves but any **estate/acquisition correspondence describing what was sorted and where non-art/library material went**.
