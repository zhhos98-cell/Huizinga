# Van Stockum working catalogues at Haags Gemeentearchief

_Status: active collateral provenance lead, synced 2026-09-03._

This note records a concrete archival route for tracing buyers/consignors in Van Stockum sales. It is deliberately kept separate from the main Huizinga provenance note because the December 1986 Van Stockum sale concerns primarily the inherited nineteenth-century *Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana* associated with Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888), not automatically C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's post-1945 Saint Lucas/Huizinga stock.

## 1. Van Stockum's twentieth-century auction archive is at HGA

A 2016 interview with Van Stockum auctioneer Peter Pruimers states that the firm's post-1906 archive had been transferred to the Haags Gemeentearchief. Pruimers describes the archive as providing a broad overview of the firm's twentieth-century operations and, crucially, containing **auction catalogues annotated per lot with the consignor, buyer and price** (`veilingcatalogi met aantekeningen per kavel met betrekking tot de inbrenger, de koper en de prijs`).

This is materially stronger than an ordinary priced catalogue. If the relevant working copy survives for a sale, it can in principle identify both the person who consigned a lot and the successful buyer.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek and Nick ter Wal, interview with Peter Pruimers, *De Boekenwereld* 32 (2016): https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_boe022201601_01/_boe022201601_01_0054.php

## 2. Archival access point

The current HGA finding aid for **0770-01 W.P. van Stockum** explicitly cross-references a separate archive:

**1500-01 — Van Stockum's Veilingen**

The 0770-01 page confirms that this separate auction archive exists as a distinct archival access point. The item-level public interface for 1500-01 has proved unstable / difficult to resolve through ordinary browsing, so Google-indexed deep links and archival identifiers remain the preferred discovery route.

Source:
- Haags Gemeentearchief, 0770-01 W.P. van Stockum, `Zie ook: 1500-01 - Van Stockum's Veilingen`: https://www.haagsgemeentearchief.nl/archieven-mais/overzicht?miadt=59&micode=0770-01&milang=nl&minr=994816&mivast=59&miview=inv2&mizig=210

## 3. Remote digitization is feasible once the item number is known

Haags Gemeentearchief offers a **free Digitaliseren op Verzoek service** for eligible public archival items that are not yet digitally available. The workflow requires the user to identify the archival item/inventarisnummer first, then click the scan-on-request control. HGA states that one request can be submitted per week and that the resulting digitized item is made available online, with an email link sent after scanning.

Therefore the practical bottleneck is no longer whether the Van Stockum working catalogue can be consulted remotely. It is to recover the exact **1500-01 inventarisnummer** for the relevant sale.

Sources:
- Haags Gemeentearchief, archives overview / digitization policy: https://www.haagsgemeentearchief.nl/archieven-mais
- Haags Gemeentearchief, `Archiefstuk digitaal laten maken`: https://haagsgemeentearchief.nl/bronnen-en-collecties/archieven?catid=2%3Fhighlight%3DWzhd&id=45%3Aarchiefstuk-digitaal-laten-maken&view=article

## 4. December 1986 sale: why this remains collateral, not primary

The Van Gogh Museum Journal states that portions of the collection of **Carel Vosmaer (1826–1888)** were sold through Van Gendt Book Auctions in the 1980s and that the **last part of the Bibliotheca Vosmaeriana** was sold at Van Stockum in The Hague on **3–5 December 1986**.

This means that the December 1986 working catalogue is still worth inspecting, but its documented organizing provenance is the older family library. It must not be treated as an automatic continuation of the chain:

`Huizinga -> C.J.J.G. Vosmaer (1907–1986) -> Van Stockum 1986`

without an actual Huizinga/Malinowski entry or a consignor annotation linking a target lot to C.J.J.G.'s twentieth-century holdings.

Source:
- *Van Gogh Museum Journal* 1996: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199601_01/_van012199601_01.pdf

## 5. Van Gendt branch

The same provenance note records that **Van Gendt Book Auctions sold portions of the Vosmaer collection in the 1980s** before the final 1986 Van Stockum sale. Exact Van Gendt sale dates/catalogues for those Vosmaer portions have not yet been identified.

This branch should be treated as a control for the family-library dispersal. If a target Malinowski copy unexpectedly surfaces in family-library records, earlier Van Gendt sales become relevant; otherwise they remain secondary to the direct Saint Lucas routes.
