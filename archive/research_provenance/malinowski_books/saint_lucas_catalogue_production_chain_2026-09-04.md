# Saint Lucas catalogue production chain — Trio / Pam Rueter / Th. Kleyn (2026-09-04)

_Status: production-side provenance route. Supplements the repository/library-location searches for Saint Lucas Catalogue 3._

## 1. What Heijbroek securely establishes for Catalogue 2

J.F. Heijbroek states that the earliest surviving Saint Lucas Society catalogue, **Catalogue 2**, dates from **September 1943**. Its brief colophon names **Drukkerij Trio, Den Haag** as printer. The cover showing Rapenburg 83, the entêtes, culs-de-lampe and vignettes were designed by **P.G. (`Pam`) Rueter**. **Th. Kleyn** made most of the fiches/listings for the offers.

Catalogue 2 was entirely in French and was strongest in `sociale wetenschappen en rechten` and `genealogie en heraldiek`.

Source:
- J.F. Heijbroek, `De Saint Lucas Society`, *De Boekenwereld* 34.4 (2018), pp. 45–46: https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 2. Important boundary: Trio is proven for Catalogue 2, not yet Catalogue 3

No source currently found states that the postwar **Catalogue 3, _Wetenschappelijke adversaria_**, was also printed by Trio. Therefore the chain

`Catalogue 2 -> Trio -> Catalogue 3`

must **not** be assumed simply from series continuity.

This matters because the obvious surviving business ledgers in the public summary of the Trio archive are chronologically weak for a ca. 1948 Catalogue 3:

- cost-calculation records: 1932–1944;
- sales books: 1936, 1941–1942;
- journals: 1937–1942;
- general ledgers: 1938–1942;
- printed-work samples highlighted in the WWII archive guide: 1897–1942.

The archive still deserves checking, but it is not presently a direct postwar Catalogue 3 ledger route.

Source:
- Haags Gemeentearchief WWII archive guide, Drukkerij Trio 0805-01: https://haagsgemeentearchief.nl/images/PDF_bestanden/Wegwijzer-Tweede-Wereldoorlog-Haags-Gemeentearchief-4-1-2019.pdf

## 3. Concrete printer archive: Haags Gemeentearchief 0805-01

The institutional archive of **N.V. Drukkerij Trio** survives at the Haags Gemeentearchief as **archief 0805-01**, covering roughly 1897/1898–1988/1990. Public descriptions identify, among other material:

- board minutes and attachments, 1912–1953;
- correspondence of the management board with commissioners, 1929–1948;
- inventories and valuation files;
- balances/profit-and-loss accounts through 1952;
- production/cost-calculation records through 1944;
- sales books for selected pre-1943 years;
- printed samples;
- drawings, designs, proofs and printed work for other designers.

The archive therefore offers a **production-side test**, especially for whether Saint Lucas remained a Trio client after 1943, but the exact postwar client/order series must be identified in the complete inventory before requesting boxes.

Sources:
- SHIE, `Trio, drukkerij (1898–1990)`: https://shie.nl/bedrijven/trio-drukkerij-1898-1990/
- Haags Gemeentearchief archive guide, 0805-01: https://haagsgemeentearchief.nl/images/PDF_bestanden/Wegwijzer-Tweede-Wereldoorlog-Haags-Gemeentearchief-4-1-2019.pdf

## 4. The stronger continuity is Pam Rueter's design work, not yet Trio's printing

Heijbroek's captions show postwar continuity in **Pam Rueter's Saint Lucas design system**:

- Catalogue 2 (1943): Rueter cover and ornamental system;
- **four entêtes numbered 5 through 8** designed by Rueter `voor de catalogi van de Saint Lucas Society in Leiden`;
- two further Saint Lucas designs, one used in advertisements in *Het Parool* after 5 May 1945;
- Rueter designed the masthead/visual identity for **Rapiarijs**, the Saint Lucas newsletter appearing from April 1946 to autumn 1947;
- an additional unused Saint Lucas letter-design on the following page is described as probably also by Rueter.

This establishes a visual-production continuity across the war and into the immediate postwar years. It does **not** by itself identify which catalogue number used which of headers 5–8, nor where Catalogue 3 was printed.

Source:
- Heijbroek 2018, captions and text pp. 46–48: https://www.bibliotheek.universiteitleiden.nl/binaries/content/assets/ul2ub/bijzondere-collecties/de-boekenwereld/de-boekenwereld-34_4-2018.pdf

## 5. Why the numbered entêtes matter

The labels `nr. 5 t/m 8` indicate that discrete Saint Lucas catalogue-header designs survived as physical or reproduced objects. This creates a new discovery question:

> Where were the Rueter Saint Lucas designs photographed/reproduced from for Heijbroek's article?

The captions in the text extraction do not state a holding institution or object number. Heijbroek closes the article by saying the reconstruction relied on conversations with the Vosmaer family and **documents from their archive**, and thanks Therus Vosmaer. Accordingly, a family-archive origin for at least some illustrated Saint Lucas ephemera is plausible, but must not be asserted item-by-item without image credits or author clarification.

Operationally, the original designs or production proofs may preserve:

- catalogue numbering;
- printer/production annotations;
- dates;
- handwritten job references;
- adjacent unused or rejected designs;
- evidence tying the postwar Catalogue 3 to a printer or print run.

## 6. Pam Rueter's work survives in several institutional collections, but no dedicated Saint Lucas archive is yet identified

Current public searches find substantial Rueter material at the **Rijksmuseum**, including prints, ex libris albums and a generic `Document betreffende Pam Georg Rueter` (RP-D-2017-1439). Some Rueter works entered the Rijksprentenkabinet through transfer from the former Instituut Collectie Nederland. The public catalogue does not currently surface a Saint Lucas Society design under Rueter's name.

**Huis van het boek** also holds Rueter ex-libris material and, independently, important Drukkerij Trio proofs/archival objects. Its archival-collections page highlights surviving Trio proof material from the famous ca. 1930 Piet Zwart project. This makes Huis van het boek a secondary production-history repository worth checking for Rueter/Trio ephemera, although no Saint Lucas object has yet been identified there.

## 7. Trio itself was active and producing refined ephemera in 1948

The lack of a postwar Saint Lucas printing attribution cannot be explained by Trio having ceased operations. Antiquarian and institutional evidence shows active Trio production in 1948, including the firm's 50th-anniversary publication and smaller printed ephemera; one surviving associated group even contains a **Pam G. Rueter Easter greeting from 1948**.

This keeps Trio technically and socially plausible as the Catalogue 3 printer, but plausibility is not attribution.

Source:
- Fokas Holthuis, description of Drukkerij Trio 50th-anniversary volume and inserted ephemera: https://fokas.nl/2022/09/29/nieuwsbrief-1015-halfperkament-iii/

## 8. Revised production-side search protocol

### A. Heijbroek / Vosmaer-family visual source
1. Identify the image-credit/provenance of the illustrated Catalogue 2, Rueter entêtes 5–8, Rapiarijs design and Saint Lucas advertisement designs.
2. Ask whether the original design sheets remain with Therus/Vosmaer family papers or were transferred to an institution.
3. Inspect adjacent design sheets for `Catalogue 3`, `Wetenschappelijke adversaria`, dates, print notes or stock/job numbers.

### B. Drukkerij Trio archive, HGA 0805-01
1. Obtain the complete digital inventory, not only the WWII thematic excerpt.
2. Search for client/order correspondence around **1946–1950**.
3. Search `Saint Lucas`, `St. Lucas`, `Vosmaer`, `Rapenburg 83`, `Wyttenbachweg 43`, `Bibliopolis`, `Rapiarijs`.
4. Check whether proof/sample series continue beyond the 1897–1942 range highlighted by the thematic guide.
5. If a client/order trail exists, extract job date, print run, format, invoice and proof references.

### C. Pam Rueter institutional material
1. Rijksmuseum/Rijksprentenkabinet: inquire whether RP-D-2017-1439 or related transfer dossiers contain Saint Lucas commissions.
2. Huis van het boek: search Rueter design/ex-libris records and Trio archival/proof holdings for Saint Lucas.
3. RKD and other artist archives: search correspondence indexes for `Vosmaer`, `Saint Lucas`, `Trio`.

## 9. Current evidential ranking

- **Catalogue 2 printed by Trio, Sept 1943:** secure.
- **Catalogue 2 visual system by Pam Rueter:** secure.
- **Th. Kleyn compiled most Catalogue 2 fiches:** secure.
- **Rueter continued Saint Lucas design work postwar / headers 5–8 survive:** secure.
- **Catalogue 3 printed by Trio:** unverified.
- **Rueter designed Catalogue 3 specifically:** plausible but unverified.
- **Illustrated Rueter/Saint Lucas originals remain in Vosmaer family archive:** plausible, source context supports inquiry, but unverified item-by-item.

## Running consequence

The Catalogue 3 search should now run on two orthogonal axes: **bibliographic survival** (KVB/Allard Pierson, KB Verzameling Catalogi, UBL BA2) and **production survival** (Vosmaer-family design ephemera, Pam Rueter material, possibly Trio HGA 0805-01). A missing published copy would no longer end the inquiry if a printer job, proof, design sheet or catalogue-header sequence can reconstruct its date and production context.
