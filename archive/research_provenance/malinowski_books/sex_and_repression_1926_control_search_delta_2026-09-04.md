# *Sex and Repression*: 1926 control-copy search delta — 2026-09-04

_Status: direct-object search only. Target remains Johan Huizinga's physical copy._

## 1. A second publicly documented 1926 copy has not yet surfaced

Targeted searches for *Sex and Repression in Savage Society* combined with `presentation copy`, `presentation inscription`, `inscribed by the author`, `signed presentation copy`, and `1926` did not locate another physical copy carrying a 1926 inscription or ownership date.

This is a public-metadata negative, not evidence that no such copy existed. It does, however, make Huizinga's documented possession by 12 December 1926 increasingly unusual in relation to the normal bibliographic record.

## 2. The normal bibliographic record is remarkably uniform

Public union/digital-library records located in this pass consistently describe *Sex and Repression* as a 1927 publication. HathiTrust/Online Books Page exposes multiple digitized copies, but all are catalogued as 1927; Google Books and Open Library likewise expose the ordinary 1927 state. No separately catalogued `1926` edition has yet appeared.

At Yale, the Malinowski Papers list two physical `author's copies` of *Sex and Repression*, both catalogued under 1927 (b.19 f.161–162). By contrast, *Crime and Custom* has an explicitly catalogued rough proof dated 14 April 1926 in addition to two author's copies. The finding aid does **not** separately list a 1926 proof/advance copy of *Sex and Repression*.

Sources:
- University of Pennsylvania Online Books Page / HathiTrust links: https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Malinowski%2C%20Bronislaw%2C%201884-1942
- Yale MS 19 finding aid: https://ead-pdfs.library.yale.edu/4100.pdf

### Evidential consequence

The absence of a separately catalogued 1926 state does not disprove one. It raises the probability that Huizinga's copy, if it was *Sex and Repression*, belonged to a very small author-controlled circulation that never became a bibliographically recognised issue.

## 3. Contemporary production chronology now has three fixed points

### 19 February 1926 — Malinowski expected publication soon

In a letter to the Frazers from the LSE, Malinowski says material is to be added to his `Sex & Repression`, **`which will be published soon`**. This places the work in an advanced publication trajectory at least ten months before Huizinga acknowledged receipt.

Archive: Trinity College Cambridge, Frazer Papers, **FRAZ/2/139**, 19 Feb. 1926.

Source:
- https://archives.trin.cam.ac.uk/index.php/informationobject/browse?collection=445&languages=en&names=13264&sf_culture=en&sort=alphabetic&sortDir=desc&topLod=0&view=table

### 12 December 1926 — Huizinga has the `medium` book

Huizinga acknowledges receipt of the small, medium and big Malinowski books. The *Briefwisseling* editors identify the medium one as *Sex and Repression*.

### February 1927 — final preface

The standard edition's preface is signed at the LSE in **February 1927**. Therefore a December 1926 object cannot simply be equated with the final standard state without explaining how the later preface entered the book.

This sequence — `expected soon Feb 1926 -> physical Huizinga object Dec 1926 -> final preface Feb 1927` — is now the most important material-history problem in locating the copy.

## 4. The text itself had already circulated in component form

Parts of *Sex and Repression* had appeared before the book:

- `Psycho-Analysis and Anthropology` in *Psyche* (1923/1924), with the first major block later becoming `The Formation of a Complex`;
- a German book-form version, *Mutterrechtliche Familie und Ödipus-Komplex* (1924), corresponding substantially to the first part;
- `Complex and Myth in Mother-Right` (1925), later forming the second major part.

Thus a pre-final book object in 1926 need not imply that the whole intellectual content was newly typeset at once. It could have been assembled from already mature/printed material plus newer sections and subsequently received altered preliminaries before publication.

This makes the **preliminaries** of any candidate copy especially diagnostic: title page, dedication leaf, preface, contents, and their pagination/collation.

## 5. No surviving covering letter is presently controlled in the direct pair spine

The project’s reconciled Huizinga–Malinowski chronology currently has, for 1926:

- 12 Dec 1926 Huizinga -> Malinowski: acknowledgement of the three books;
- 31 Dec 1926 Huizinga -> Malinowski: methodological follow-up (LSE MALINOWSKI/36/39).

No text-controlled Malinowski covering letter naming or describing the book dispatch is presently in the project spine. Huizinga's phrase `your letter, soon followed by your books` confirms such prior communication but not that its text survives in the published correspondence.

Accordingly, the best archival route is not to assume a missing letter can be recovered from *Briefwisseling*. It is to inspect Malinowski's publisher/author records for complimentary-copy or proof dispatch evidence.

## 6. Current direct archival bottleneck

Yale MS 19 remains the strongest paired test:

- **b.7 f.547, reel 4U — George Routledge and Sons, 1922–1938**: publisher correspondence spanning all three target books;
- **b.4 f.280, reel 2U — Huizinga, Johan, 1926–1933**: direct correspondence;
- **b.19 f.161–162, reel 6U — Sex and Repression author's copies nos. 1–2**: physical controls.

The decisive questions for f.547 are now very narrow:

1. Was the book delayed after being expected `soon` in February 1926?
2. Did Malinowski receive proofs, advance sheets, or bound copies before December 1926?
3. Are complimentary/presentation copies or dispatch recipients named?
4. Was a new preface requested or inserted in February 1927?
5. Did title-page date, preliminaries, pagination, or binding change between late 1926 and commercial release?

For f.161–162:

1. Do both author's copies contain the February 1927 preface?
2. Are the preliminaries cancellans/cancellandum, tipped-in, or otherwise physically altered?
3. Do the bindings, gatherings or publisher's advertisements show evidence of a pre-publication state?

## Running assessment

For locating Huizinga's actual book, *Sex and Repression* has now become a **state-identification problem rather than a generic first-edition provenance search**. Ordinary clean 1927 copies are low-value. A copy with anomalous preliminaries, absent/different February 1927 preface, 1926 inscription/ownership date, proof status, or Malinowski/Vosmaer/Huizinga provenance should be escalated immediately.
