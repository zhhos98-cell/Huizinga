# Rijksmuseum acquisitions from the heirs of C.J.J.G. Vosmaer — 2026-09-04

_Status: institutional-transfer route. Relevant to locating Saint Lucas / Pam Rueter / Vosmaer-family ephemera after the 1986 house/archive dispersal._

## 1. Positive object-level evidence: material from C.J.J.G. Vosmaer's heirs entered the Rijksmuseum in 1987 and 1989

The Rijksmuseum online catalogue contains multiple objects with acquisition credit lines explicitly naming the **heirs of C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, Leiden**.

Examples include:

- **RP-T-1987-4**, Carel Vosmaer, design for an Almanak print relating to the Leiden Masquerade of 1850 — `Schenking van de erven van de heer C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, Leiden`, acquisition 1987.
- **RP-T-1987-5**, Carel Vosmaer, `Munt en Kruis of Een Medaille en Hare Keerzijde` — same donor formulation, 1987.
- **RP-F-00-1094**, group portrait of three soldiers — `Schenking van de erven van de heer C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, Leiden`, acquisition 1989.
- **RP-F-00-1101** and related photographs — same donor formulation, 1989.

## 2. A separate 1986 purchase occurred directly from C.J.J.G. Vosmaer

The Rijksmuseum also records at least one 1986 purchase directly from C.J.J.G. Vosmaer, e.g. a profile portrait of Carel Vosmaer. Its provenance states that the object passed through Gualtherus Carel Jacob Vosmaer to C.J.J.G. and was bought from him in 1986 with other objects.

This establishes a sequence rather than a one-off transaction:

`C.J.J.G. Vosmaer -> Rijksmuseum (1986)`

followed by

`heirs of C.J.J.G. Vosmaer -> Rijksmuseum (1987/1989)`.

Source:
- Rijksmuseum profile portrait of Carel Vosmaer: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Profielportret%2Bvan%2BMr.%2BCarel%2BVosmaer--7d8fdad447fe9ed4d6192c9d0f1a493a

## 3. 1989 annual-report search evidence indicates letters also entered from the heirs

A searchable excerpt from the Rijksmuseum annual-report material for 1989 states that **`brieven` were acquired from the heirs of C.J.J.G. Vosmaer at Leiden**. The full annual-report PDF is too large for the current web viewer/screenshot route, so the composition, quantity and accession numbers of those letters have not yet been directly inspected.

Accordingly, the safe claim is limited:

- the Rijksmuseum received not only drawings/photographs but also **letter material** from the Vosmaer heirs in this dispersal period;
- the exact relationship of those letters to the family archive, Saint Lucas or Pam Rueter remains unknown.

Source lead:
- Rijksmuseum annual report 1989, searchable mirror: https://catalogus.boekman.nl/pub/JS/R11-1989.pdf

## 4. Consequence for the Saint Lucas / Rueter visual-production route

J.F. Heijbroek's 2018 Saint Lucas article reproduces Saint Lucas graphic material by or attributed to **Pam Rueter**, including catalogue header designs (`entêtes`) and postwar advertising designs. The article does not identify object numbers or holding institutions in the extracted captions, but Heijbroek also states that his reconstruction relied on conversations with the Vosmaer family and documents from their archive.

Because:

1. Heijbroek worked in the Rijksprentenkabinet/Rijksmuseum milieu;
2. the Rijksmuseum demonstrably received multiple batches of Vosmaer-family objects from C.J.J.G. and his heirs during 1986–89;
3. the museum also received letter material from the heirs;

Rijksmuseum **acquisition dossiers for the 1986, 1987 and 1989 Vosmaer transfers** should be treated as a high-priority route for locating the provenance of the illustrated Saint Lucas/Rueter materials.

This is a discovery argument, not evidence that the Saint Lucas designs are already in the museum collection.

## 6. Evidential boundary

### Secure
- C.J.J.G. Vosmaer sold objects directly to the Rijksmuseum in 1986.
- his heirs donated material in 1987 and 1989.
- public catalogue objects document those transfers.
- annual-report search evidence states that letters from the heirs were acquired in 1989.

### Unverified
- that Saint Lucas Society business records entered the Rijksmuseum;
- that the Rueter catalogue entêtes illustrated by Heijbroek are museum objects;
- that the 1989 letters concern Saint Lucas or Huizinga.

The value of the route is institutional: it gives a concrete post-1986 destination for Vosmaer-family material outside the Nationaal Archief and explains why museum acquisition documentation may preserve provenance information not visible in the public archive inventory.
