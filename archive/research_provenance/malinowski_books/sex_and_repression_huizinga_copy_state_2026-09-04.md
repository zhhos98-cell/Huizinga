# Huizinga's *Sex and Repression* copy: pre-final-state problem — 2026-09-04

_Status: direct-object identification note. Scope is only the physical copy of Bronislaw Malinowski, *Sex and Repression in Savage Society*, that Johan Huizinga had received by 12 December 1926._

## 1. The chronological anomaly is stronger than a 1926/1927 imprint mismatch

Huizinga's letter of **12 December 1926** says Malinowski's letter had been followed by three books, described as `the small, the medium and the big one`. The editors of *Briefwisseling* identify them respectively as:

1. *Crime and Custom in Savage Society* (London, 1926)
2. *Sex and Repression in Savage Society* (London, 1927)
3. *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* (London, 1922)

The standard first edition of *Sex and Repression* has a preface signed:

`B. M. / Department of Anthropology, London School of Economics / February, 1927.`

Therefore the physical item in Huizinga's hands by 12 Dec. 1926, **if the editors' identification of the medium volume is correct, cannot have been identical in state to an ordinary completed first-edition copy containing that February 1927 preface**.

This is now the highest-value physical discriminator in the three-book search.

Sources:
- Huizinga, *Briefwisseling*, vol. II, letter 685 and editorial note: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b9/Briefwisseling%2C_Deel_II_1925-1933%2C_DigUBL.pdf
- Standard *Sex and Repression* preface text, dated February 1927: https://www.legimi.de/e-book-sex-and-repression-in-savage-society-illustrated-bronislaw-malinowski%2Cb4101441.html

## 2. Malinowski himself expected the book to be a 1926 publication

In the 1926 *Crime and Custom in Savage Society*, Malinowski refers readers to `my Sex and Repression in Savage Society (1926)`. In the same book he also refers to *The Father in Primitive Psychology* as 1926, although standard bibliographies date the separately published book to 1927.

This shows that the `1926` date was not invented by a later cataloguer. Malinowski himself expected/represented these related works as 1926 publications while *Crime and Custom* was in print. At least one publication schedule therefore slipped into 1927.

Source:
- *Crime and Custom in Savage Society*, authorial note referring to *Sex and Repression in Savage Society (1926)*: https://rcin.org.pl/Content/29211/PDF/WA004_20379_U6901_Malinowski-Crime_oh.pdf

## 3. Working physical-state hypotheses

The Huizinga medium copy should now be tested against four mutually distinguishable possibilities:

### H1 — advance bound copy / pre-publication state
A substantially complete book was bound and available to Malinowski in late 1926, but the final February 1927 preface was absent or later replaced/added in trade copies.

### H2 — bound proof / proof sheets
Malinowski sent Huizinga a materially book-like proof state. Huizinga naturally called it a `book`; the editors later identified it by the final published title. Such a copy might lack the final title-page/preliminaries, carry proof marks, or have provisional pagination/binding.

### H3 — an early issue later altered before general release
A small number of copies may have existed in late 1926 and the title/preliminaries may have been reset before commercial publication. No evidence for such an issue has yet been found; this remains a testable possibility, not a claim.

### H4 — editorial misidentification in *Briefwisseling*
The `medium` volume may have been another Malinowski title rather than *Sex and Repression*. This must remain open because a standard final-state *Sex and Repression* is chronologically impossible in December 1926. The size ordering still makes the editors' identification plausible: *Crime and Custom* is the small volume, *Argonauts* the large, and *Sex and Repression* naturally fits the middle position.

## 4. Yale gives the two files that can decide the issue

The Yale Bronislaw Malinowski Papers, MS 19, identify two directly relevant archival nodes:

### Publisher correspondence
**b. 7, f. 547, reel 4U — `George Routledge and Sons`, 1922–1938.**

This single correspondence file spans the production periods of all three Huizinga target books: *Argonauts* (1922), *Crime and Custom* (1926), and *Sex and Repression* (1926–27 publication process). It is currently the highest-priority source for author-copy dispatch, proofing, delays, complimentary copies and publication scheduling.

### Huizinga correspondence
**b. 4, f. 280, reel 2U — `Huizinga, Johan`, 1926–1933.**

This should be checked alongside f.547 for any covering letter from Malinowski, acknowledgement, dispatch language, title identification, or indication that a proof/advance copy was sent.

Source:
- Yale, Guide to the Bronislaw Malinowski Papers, MS 19: https://ead-pdfs.library.yale.edu/4100.pdf

The same finding aid records:

- b.18 f.158 — *Crime and Custom*, rough proof, dated 14 April 1926
- b.19 f.159 — *Crime and Custom*, author's copy no.1 (1926)
- b.19 f.160 — *Crime and Custom*, author's copy no.2 (1926)
- b.19 f.161 — *Sex and Repression*, author's copy no.1 with annotations and notes (catalogued 1927)
- b.19 f.162 — *Sex and Repression*, author's copy no.2 (catalogued 1927)

These are the correct physical comparators. In particular, f.161 and f.162 should be checked for the February 1927 preface, title-page state, binding, publisher's ads, accession annotations and any evidence of an earlier printing/binding state.

## 6. Search rule for surviving copies

A normal London 1927 first edition with the February 1927 preface and no earlier provenance should move **down** the candidate list.

Immediately elevate any copy with one or more of the following:

- Malinowski inscription dated **1926**;
- Huizinga ownership/presentation inscription;
- title page or binding identifying the work but **without the February 1927 preface**;
- proof/advance/review-copy language;
- provisional or anomalous preliminaries/pagination;
- 1926 ownership date from any recipient, because it establishes another pre-final circulating copy;
- C.J.J.G. Vosmaer provenance combined with any of the above.

The practical search target is therefore no longer `a 1927 first edition of Sex and Repression`. It is **a pre-February-1927 physical state of the work, ideally traceable to Malinowski and Huizinga**.

## 7. Immediate decision sequence

1. Inspect Yale MS 19 b.7 f.547 for late-1926/early-1927 Routledge correspondence about *Sex and Repression*.
2. Inspect Yale b.19 f.161–162 as physical controls for the two author's copies.
3. Reinspect Huizinga b.4 f.280 for any covering letter/dispatch evidence not reproduced in the published correspondence.
4. Search auction/dealer/institutional provenance records specifically for *Sex and Repression* copies dated or inscribed **1926**, rather than generic `first edition` copies.
5. If no pre-1927 state can be documented outside Huizinga's letter, reopen the editors' identification of the medium volume instead of forcing the chronology.

## Running conclusion

This is the strongest direct-object lead found so far. The physical Huizinga copy, if it was indeed *Sex and Repression*, should be bibliographically abnormal. That abnormality may make it substantially easier to identify than an ordinary first edition once the publisher correspondence and Yale author copies are compared.
